Born on 17 March 1548 in Kuramae, Nukata District, Mikawa Province, the son of Honda Tadataka, a vassal of Tokugawa Ieyasu since childhood, he took part in his first battle as early as 1560, at Washizu Fort, and recorded his first individual kill in 1561, at fourteen. Promoted to hatamoto in 1566 with command of fifty-four cavalry, he fought at Ieyasu's side in every major battle of the clan for more than fifty years, from the Battle of Anegawa in 1570, where he distinguished himself, to Sekigahara, by way of Hitokotosaka in 1572, the defeat at Mikatagahara against Takeda Shingen in 1573, and Nagashino in 1575, never seriously wounded across fifty-seven campaigns, a reputation for invulnerability reinforced by his emblematic antlered helmet.

He accompanied Ieyasu's escape through Iga Province after the Honno-ji incident in 1582, then took part in the Komaki campaign against the Toyotomi in 1584. Daimyo of the Otaki Domain from 1590 to 1601, worth 100,000 koku, and then of the Kuwana Domain from 1601 to 1609, worth 150,000 koku, he married his daughter Komatsuhime to Sanada Nobuyuki, forging an alliance between the two families.

Regarded as one of the Tokugawa's "Four Guardian Kings" alongside Ii Naomasa, Sakai Tadatsugu and Sakakibara Yasumasa, he was praised even by his enemies for his bravery, to the point that Takeda Shingen is said to have once called him "a treasure surpassing even Ieyasu's own." He retired in 1609, leaving the succession to his son Tadamasa, and died peacefully in his sleep in Edo on 3 December 1610, aged sixty-two.
