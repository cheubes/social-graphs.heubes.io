Born Akechi Tama in 1563, in Echizen Province, the daughter of Akechi Mitsuhide and Tsumaki Hiroko, she married the daimyo Hosokawa Tadaoki at sixteen, in 1578, with whom she had five or six children, including Hosokawa Tadatoshi. Her life changed in 1582 when her father assassinated Oda Nobunaga, making the teenage Tama the daughter of a traitor: out of political caution, her husband kept her secluded for several years rather than risk being associated with the betrayal.

Secretly converted to Christianity in 1587, as soon as she learned of the ban Hideyoshi had decreed against Christians, unable to leave her residence to be baptised, she received the sacrament from her own maid, under the baptismal name Gracia; she then studied Latin and Portuguese and read Thomas à Kempis's The Imitation of Christ. She thereafter led a quiet but firmly held life of faith, despite Hideyoshi's ban on samurai conversions.

In 1600, when Ishida Mitsunari tried to take her hostage during the Sekigahara campaign to force her husband to join the Western coalition, she refused to surrender and, her faith forbidding ritual suicide, asked the family's loyal retainer, Ogasawara Shosai, to kill her rather than let her be captured; he did so on 25 August 1600 before taking his own life by seppuku. Contemporary Jesuit records suggest, however, that it was her husband who had previously ordered her killed should her honour ever be endangered. She is buried at the Sozenji temple in Osaka and shares a grave with Tadaoki at Koto-in in Kyoto, an episode that struck both sides deeply on the eve of Sekigahara.
