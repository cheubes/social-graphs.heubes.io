Born Shojumaru on 3 December 1568 in Himeji, Harima Province, the son of Kuroda Yoshitaka, known as Kanbei, chief strategic adviser to Toyotomi Hideyoshi, raised partly as a hostage at Oda Nobunaga's court after his father was tried in 1577 as an alleged spy, he was even kidnapped and narrowly escaped execution before being rescued by Takenaka Hanbei. After Nobunaga's death in 1582, he served Hideyoshi, trained at his side.

He actively took part in the unification campaigns, fighting at Shizugatake in 1583, then subduing Takarabe Castle during the Kyushu campaign in 1587-1588, where he orchestrated the assassination of Ki Shigefusa and had his hostage Tsuruhime executed by crucifixion; he inherited leadership of the Kuroda clan in 1589 when his father retired. He actively took part in the invasions of Korea between 1592 and 1598, commanding five thousand men in the first invasion, taking part in the siege of Pyongyang in 1592 and then the Battle of Jiksan in 1597, where he commanded in the field alongside Kato Kiyomasa.

After Hideyoshi's death, he drew closer to Tokugawa Ieyasu, even playing an active role in winning other lords over to his cause before Sekigahara. His decisive contribution to the 1600 victory, particularly in the negotiations preceding Kobayakawa Hideaki's defection, earned him rich rewards from the new Tokugawa regime, which granted him Chikuzen Province, worth 520,000 koku, and the title of daimyo of Fukuoka from 1601 to 1623. He took part in the Osaka Castle campaigns between 1614 and 1615 and died on 29 August 1623, aged fifty-four, leaving several children by his two wives, Itohime and Eihime, including Kuroda Tadayuki.
