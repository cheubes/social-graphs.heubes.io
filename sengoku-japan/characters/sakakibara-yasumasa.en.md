Born in 1548 in the Ueno district of Mikawa Province, the second son of Sakakibara Nagamasa, he distinguished himself as early as 1564 through his bravery at the Battle of Azukizaka against the Ikko-ikki rebels, earning him permission from Tokugawa Ieyasu to use the character "yasu" from his own name. Appointed hatamoto in 1566, at nineteen, with command of fifty cavalry, he fought at the Battle of Anegawa in 1570, executing flanking manoeuvres, then took part between 1573 and 1579 in the campaigns to conquer Takeda clan castles in Totomi Province.

The youngest of the Tokugawa's "Four Guardian Kings," he became head of the Sakakibara clan in 1579, succeeding his elder brother Sakakibara Kiyomasa, and became one of Ieyasu's trusted commanders, notably during the conflict against Toyotomi Hideyoshi at Komaki-Nagakute in 1584, where he advised him; he was recognised in 1586 as one of the "Four Guardian Kings" (Tokugawa-Shitenno). He led the vanguard at the siege of Odawara in 1590, before being appointed supervisor of the Edo domain's administration, and then lord of the Tatebayashi Domain, worth 100,000 koku, from 1590 to 1606.

At the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, he was tasked with escorting and protecting Tokugawa Hidetada, Ieyasu's son, who was delayed en route by the siege of Ueda Castle held by the Sanada clan, preventing him from arriving in time to take part in the main fighting. He died on 19 June 1606 in Edo, aged fifty-seven or fifty-eight, and was buried at the Zendoji Temple in Tatebayashi.
