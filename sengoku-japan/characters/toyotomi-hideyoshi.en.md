Born Hiyoshimaru on 16 February 1537 (lunar calendar) in Nakamura, Owari Province, into a peasant family with no clan name, the son of Kinoshita Yaemon, who died in 1543 when he was only seven, and Omandokoro, brother to Toyotomi Hidenaga and to his sisters Tomo and Asahi no kata, Kinoshita Tokichiro first served the Imagawa clan under Matsushita Yukitsuna before joining Oda Nobunaga's forces in 1558 as a lowly ashigaru, a sandal-bearer, with no traceable warrior lineage. He married One, known as Nene, adopted daughter of Asano Nagakatsu, in 1561.

He rose, through military and diplomatic talent alone, through every rank of the Oda hierarchy, becoming one of Nobunaga's most distinguished generals in 1568 and taking the name Hashiba Hideyoshi; he was appointed daimyo of three districts in northern Omi Province in 1573. In 1582, while besieging Takamatsu Castle on Nobunaga's behalf, he learned of Akechi Mitsuhide's betrayal; he immediately struck a truce with the Mori clan and marched back to avenge his master at Yamazaki, beginning that same year the construction of Osaka Castle. This lightning response let him establish himself as Nobunaga's principal successor, at the expense of rivals such as Shibata Katsuie, defeated in 1583.

Within a decade, he subdued or won over every major clan in Japan, from the Mori in the west to the Hojo at Odawara in 1590, conquering Kyushu from the Shimazu clan between 1586 and 1587 and completing the political unification of the country that Nobunaga had begun; he launched the "sword hunt" in 1588, confiscating weapons from peasants. Never able to claim the title of shogun for lack of recognised warrior ancestry, he had himself named imperial regent in 1585, taking the name Toyotomi, then resigned in 1592 to become taiko, retired regent, while launching that same year two costly invasions of Korea; in 1591, he forced Sen no Rikyu to commit suicide and crushed the Kunohe rebellion, before stripping his nephew Hidetsugu of his titles in 1595 and driving him too to suicide. His son Hideyori was born in 1593, his only surviving heir after the premature death of his first son, Tsurumatsu. His death on 18 September 1598 at Fushimi Castle, in Kyoto, kept secret for a time by the Council of Five Elders to preserve morale among the troops engaged in Korea, left Hideyori, then five, under the guardianship of that council of regents, which included Tokugawa Ieyasu, opening the way to the power struggle that would culminate at Sekigahara; he reportedly expressed regret over the Korean invasions and urged his allies to draw his young son closer to Tokugawa Ieyasu.
