Born into a peasant family with no clan name, Kinoshita Tokichiro entered Oda Nobunaga's service as a lowly sandal-bearer before rising, through military and diplomatic talent alone, through every rank of the Oda hierarchy, taking the name Hashiba Hideyoshi and later Toyotomi.

In 1582, while besieging Takamatsu Castle on Nobunaga's behalf, he learned of Akechi Mitsuhide's betrayal; he immediately struck a truce with the Mori clan and marched back to avenge his master at Yamazaki. This lightning response let him establish himself as Nobunaga's principal successor, at the expense of rivals such as Shibata Katsuie, defeated in 1583.

Within a decade, Hideyoshi subdued or won over every major clan in Japan, from the Mori in the west to the Hojo at Odawara in 1590, completing the political unification of the country that Nobunaga had begun. Never able to claim the title of shogun for lack of recognised warrior ancestry, he had himself named imperial regent instead, and in his final years launched two costly invasions of Korea. His death in 1598 left a five-year-old heir, Hideyori, under the guardianship of a council of regents that included Tokugawa Ieyasu, opening the way to the power struggle that would culminate at Sekigahara.
