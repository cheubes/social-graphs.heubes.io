Born Nagao Kagetora on 18 February 1530 in Echigo Province, the son of Nagao Tamekage and Tora Gozen, brother of Nagao Harukage and Aya-Gozen, he studied from seven to fourteen at the Rinsen-ji temple before receiving, at fifteen, in 1544, joint command of Tochio Castle. He succeeded his brother as head of the Nagao clan in 1548, was then adopted in 1551 by Uesugi Norimasa, inheriting his title of Kanto Kanrei, and took over leadership of the Yamanouchi-Uesugi family in 1561, changing his name to Uesugi Terutora and then Kenshin.

A devout Buddhist monk of the war god Bishamonten, he ruled Echigo Province and established himself as one of the most respected tacticians of his time, even in the eyes of his enemies. His rivalry of more than a decade with Takeda Shingen, marked by the five Battles of Kawanakajima in 1553, 1555, 1557, 1561 and 1564, made for one of the most celebrated duels of feudal Japan, with neither side ever winning decisively; the fourth and most intense battle saw Kenshin charge up to Shingen and strike at him with his sword, which Shingen fended off with his iron war fan, with casualties estimated at seventy-two per cent of Kenshin's forces and sixty-two per cent of Shingen's.

Having no biological children, he adopted as heirs his nephew Kagekatsu and Kagetora, originally a son of Hojo Ujiyasu. His sudden death on 19 April 1578, aged forty-eight, likely from oesophageal cancer though a ninja assassination has also been suggested, as he was preparing a major campaign against Oda Nobunaga, plunged the clan into a brief succession war and permanently removed one of the last serious threats to Oda hegemony; his death poem reflects on the fleeting nature of existence.
