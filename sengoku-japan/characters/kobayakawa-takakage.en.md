Born Tokujumaru in 1533 in Yoshida, Aki Province, the third son of Mori Motonari and Lady Myokyu, he became at twelve, in 1543, the fourteenth head of the Takehara-Kobayakawa clan following his adoption by Kobayakawa Okikage, then, designated in 1550 heir to the Numata-Kobayakawa branch, merged the two lines. He married Lady Toida, daughter of Kobayakawa Masahira.

He formed with his brother Kikkawa Motoharu the "Two Rivers" system that militarily supported the Mori clan, distinguishing himself through a more flexible political sense than his brother's: he had Niitakayama Castle built as a new base in 1552, led naval forces at the Battle of Miyajima in 1555, and then took part in the siege of Moji in 1561.

In 1582, while defending Takamatsu Castle under siege by Toyotomi Hideyoshi, news of Oda Nobunaga's assassination at Honno-ji precipitated a swift truce between the two sides; Takakage skilfully negotiated the agreement and moved his main castle to Mihara, opening the way to closer ties between the Mori clan and Hideyoshi. Having become one of Hideyoshi's loyal supporters, he received Iyo Province in 1585, took part in the Kyushu Campaign in 1586, earning him three provinces totalling 371,300 koku, and was granted the Toyotomi surname in 1588. Appointed to the Council of Five Elders in 1595, he retired immediately, handing his estate in 1597 to his adopted son Hideaki, who was destined for a very different fate. He died on 26 July 1597 at Mihara Castle, Bingo Province, aged sixty-three or sixty-four; Kuroda Kanbei is said to have lamented that "the last wise man in Japan" had just passed.
