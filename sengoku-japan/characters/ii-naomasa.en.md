Born on 4 March 1561 in Hoda Village, Totomi Province, into a branch of the Ii clan in the service of the Imagawa, he lost his father, Ii Naochika, executed on a false charge of treason by Imagawa Ujizane; the child escaped and was raised by Ii Naotora, head of the clan. Sent to a temple at thirteen, in 1574, to prepare for service, he was noticed the following year by Tokugawa Ieyasu, who made him his page at Hamamatsu Castle and granted him the domain of Iinoya.

He fought his first battle in 1576 at the Battle of Shibahara against Takeda forces, received an income of 10,000 koku in 1578, raised to 20,000 koku in 1580, and then distinguished himself in 1581 at the siege of Takatenjin. He accompanied Ieyasu's escape through Iga Province after the Honno-ji incident in 1582 and took part that same year in the Tensho-Jingo War against the Hojo clan, before playing a crucial role in 1584 at the Battle of Komaki and Nagakute, earning praise even from his opponent Toyotomi Hideyoshi.

He commanded an elite unit entirely equipped in bright red armour and banners nicknamed the "Red Devils," made up of former Takeda samurai, from whom he adopted the tradition of scarlet lacquer for its psychological effect on the enemy; the mere sight of this unit on a battlefield sometimes sufficed to unnerve the opposition. Promoted to the rank of jiju in 1590, he took part that same year in the Odawara campaign, his domain rising to 120,000 koku, then led suppression campaigns in Mutsu Province between 1590 and 1591. One of the Tokugawa's "Four Guardian Kings," he played a central role at the Battle of Sekigahara on 21 October 1600, where his thirty vanguard spearmen struck the first blow against the Western army and where he was wounded by a stray bullet while pursuing Shimazu Yoshihiro; he then received 180,000 koku and control of Sawayama Castle, and later Hikone, and led the negotiations that won several former Western lords over to the new regime. He died on 24 March 1602 at Sawayama Castle, aged forty-one, from complications of that wound.
