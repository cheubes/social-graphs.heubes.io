Of uncertain origin but learned and capable, Akechi Mitsuhide entered Oda Nobunaga's service in the early 1560s and rose quickly through the ranks thanks to his military and diplomatic skills, eventually receiving major commands and the fief of Tanba Province.

In June 1582, while marching to reinforce the siege of Takamatsu Castle held by Toyotomi Hideyoshi's forces against the Mori clan, he abruptly turned back and marched on Kyoto instead, where Nobunaga was staying with a small escort at the Honno-ji temple. The exact motives behind this betrayal, the most famous in Japanese history, remain debated: repeated humiliations, personal ambition, and fear of being sidelined have all been suggested.

Having forced Nobunaga to take his own life, Mitsuhide failed to consolidate power: Hideyoshi, learning of the attack, hastily struck a truce with the Mori clan and marched back to confront him at Yamazaki less than two weeks later. Defeated, Mitsuhide died while fleeing, earning him the derisive nickname 'the thirteen-day shogun.'
