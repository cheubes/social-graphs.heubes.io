Born on 10 March 1528 at Tara Castle, in Mino Province, the son of Akechi Mitsutsuna and a daughter of the Wakasa Takeda clan, otherwise of uncertain origin, learned and capable, Akechi Mitsuhide first served the Ashikaga shogunate as a bodyguard and protector of the "wandering shogun" Ashikaga Yoshiaki, before suggesting he turn to Oda Nobunaga; he entered Nobunaga's service in the early 1560s and arrived with him in Kyoto in 1568.

He rose quickly through the ranks thanks to his military and diplomatic skills: he defended Yoshiaki against the Miyoshi clan's attack in 1569, received the Sakamoto area in 1571, where he built a castle, and then pacified Tanba Province between 1575 and 1579 through diplomacy as much as siege warfare, receiving it as a fief in 1578 worth 550,000 koku; in 1580 he became Nobunaga's most important commander, replacing Sakuma Nobumori. His daughter, Hosokawa Gracia, married Hosokawa Tadaoki.

In June 1582, while marching to reinforce the siege of Takamatsu Castle held by Toyotomi Hideyoshi's forces against the Mori clan, he abruptly turned back with thirteen thousand men and marched on Kyoto instead, where Nobunaga was staying with a small escort at the Honno-ji temple, surrounded on 21 June and then set ablaze. The exact motives behind this betrayal, the most famous in Japanese history, remain debated: repeated humiliations, personal ambition, and fear of being sidelined have all been suggested; Nobunaga's body, never recovered, long fuelled doubt over his death and undermined Mitsuhide's credibility with other daimyo. Defeated less than two weeks later at Yamazaki by Hideyoshi, who marched back after hastily striking a truce with the Mori, Mitsuhide was killed on 2 July 1582 while fleeing, by the bandit leader Nakamura Chobei, earning him the derisive nickname "the thirteen-day shogun."
