Born in 1547 at Nagoya Castle, in Owari Province, the fifth daughter of Oda Nobuhide and an unnamed concubine, the younger sister of Oda Nobunaga, known for her beauty, whom he cherished so much that he is said to have once declared, "If Oichi were a man, she would make a fine warrior." In 1567, following Nobunaga's conquest of Mino, she was married at twenty, at her brother's expense, to the rival warlord Azai Nagamasa, to seal the alliance between the Oda and Azai clans; she bore him a son, Manjumaru, and three daughters, Yodo-dono (Chacha), Ohatsu and Oeyo.

This alliance lasted only three years: in 1570, Nagamasa betrayed Nobunaga during the Battle of Anegawa, and legend has it that Oichi sent her brother a tied sack of beans to warn him of the impending two-front assault. She remained at Odani Castle throughout the three-year conflict that followed; during the final siege, Nobunaga asked for his sister's return, and Nagamasa let her leave with their daughters before his defeat and seppuku. Oichi and her children then remained under Oda family protection for a decade.

After Nobunaga's assassination in 1582, his third son arranged for Oichi to remarry General Shibata Katsuie, one of her brother's most loyal generals, then locked in a power struggle with Toyotomi Hideyoshi, in order to secure his loyalty to the Oda clan. Defeated at Shizugatake in 1583, the new union ended on 15 June that same year in a joint suicide at besieged Kitanosho Castle: Katsuie urged her to flee, but Oichi refused, insisting on dying alongside her second husband after securing her daughters' safety with Hideyoshi. Her three daughters went on to remarkable fates: Yodo-dono became Hideyoshi's concubine and mother of his heir Hideyori, Ohatsu married Kyogoku Takatsugu, and Oeyo married Tokugawa Hidetada, becoming mother to the third shogun.
