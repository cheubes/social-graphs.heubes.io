Born Unomatsu on 8 January 1556 in Echigo Province, the son of Nagao Masakage and Aya-Gozen, sister of the renowned daimyo Uesugi Kenshin, he was adopted by Kenshin after his father's death. The nephew and adopted son of Uesugi Kenshin, Kagekatsu took over the Uesugi clan after a brief succession war following Kenshin's death in 1578, defeating his adoptive brother Uesugi Kagetora at the siege of Otate that same year and forcing him to commit suicide in 1579; he then married Takeda Katsuyori's sister.

In 1582, Oda Nobunaga's generals conquered the Uesugi territories of Kaga, Noto and Etchu; at the siege of Uozu, facing overwhelming odds, Kagekatsu reportedly wrote that he would "become an unmatched hero" if victorious or have his "name go down in history" if defeated. Uozu castle fell on 3 June 1582, eighteen days before Nobunaga's death. He then allied with Toyotomi Hideyoshi, taking part in the 1590 Odawara campaign, and joined the Council of Five Elders; in 1598, a redistribution of holdings by Hideyoshi raised his fief from 550,000 to 1.2 million koku, in the Aizu region.

After Hideyoshi's death, his relationship with Tokugawa Ieyasu deteriorated quickly: his castle construction in Aizu prompted Ieyasu to demand explanations, which Kagekatsu refused to give, prompting Ieyasu to launch the Aizu campaign against him in 1600. It was this march north that left the field open for Ishida Mitsunari to raise the Western coalition, indirectly making Kagekatsu one of the triggers of the Sekigahara campaign, where he was quickly defeated at the sieges of Shiroishi and then Hasedo. Despite this defeat, he declared allegiance to the Tokugawa and received the Yonezawa domain, worth 300,000 koku, still fighting for the shogunate during the sieges of Osaka in 1614-1615. He died on 19 April 1623 in Yonezawa, aged sixty-seven, succeeded by his illegitimate son, Uesugi Sadakatsu; he was buried at Mount Koya.
