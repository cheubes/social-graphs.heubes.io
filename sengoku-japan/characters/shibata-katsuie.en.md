Born in 1522 in Kamiyashiro, in what is now the Meito ward of Nagoya, Owari Province, descended from the Shiba clan, he first served Oda Nobuhide and then Oda Nobuyuki, taking part as early as 1547 in the Battle of Kanoguchi. In 1556, he backed Nobuyuki against Oda Nobunaga in a succession dispute; after defeat at the Battle of Ino, Nobunaga, impressed by his loyalty and bravery, spared Katsuie, who then pledged his allegiance to him.

One of Oda Nobunaga's oldest and most loyal generals, he earned the nickname "Oni Shibata" (Demon Shibata) through brilliant military victories: severely wounded in 1571 at the siege of Nagashima, he fought in 1575 at Nagashino and then in 1577 at the Battle of Tedorigawa, and commanded Kitanosho Castle from 1576, overseeing the conquest of the Hokuriku region; he was entrusted with the conquest and later governance of Echizen Province, where he fought notably against the Uesugi clan.

After Nobunaga's death in the Honno-ji incident in 1582, he opposed Toyotomi Hideyoshi at the succession council, backing Oda Nobutaka as heir; the disagreement escalated into open conflict. Defeated at the Battle of Shizugatake in 1583, he retreated to his castle at Kitanosho where, surrounded by Hideyoshi's troops, he took his own life by seppuku on 14 June 1583, aged sixty or sixty-one, alongside his new wife Oichi, whose three daughters from a previous marriage thus became his stepdaughters, rather than surrender.
