A distant cousin and childhood companion of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, Kato Kiyomasa built a reputation as a formidable warrior in the clan's service, notably during the invasions of Korea, where his brilliant command earned him the nickname 'Kato the Demon.'

His purely martial brand of loyalty put him at lasting odds with the Toyotomi clan's civil administrators, especially Ishida Mitsunari, whom he blamed for tensions that cost the lives of fellow generals during the Korean campaign. After Hideyoshi's death, this hostility pushed him into Tokugawa Ieyasu's camp, though without renouncing his attachment to the Toyotomi house; he even attempted, in vain, to mediate between Hideyori and Ieyasu shortly before his death.
