Born on 25 July 1562 in what is now the Nakamura ward of Nagoya, the son of Kato Kiyotada and Ito, a cousin of Toyotomi Hideyoshi's mother, a distant cousin and childhood companion of Hideyoshi, the young Toranosuke, orphaned of his father early on, entered Hideyoshi's service at fifteen, in 1576, receiving a stipend of 170 koku. He distinguished himself in combat as early as the Battle of Yamazaki in 1582 and then at Shizugatake in 1583, where he earned the title of one of the "Seven Spears of Shizugatake" and 3,000 additional koku; following the Kyushu campaign in 1587, he received 250,000 koku in Higo Province and Kumamoto Castle.

He built a reputation as a formidable warrior in the clan's service during the invasions of Korea between 1592 and 1598, where, as one of the three senior commanders of this seven-year war, he captured Seoul, Busan and other cities alongside Konishi Yukinaga, defeated Korean regulars at the Battle of the Imjin River, and pacified Hamgyong, before brilliantly defending Ulsan Castle from December 1597 to November 1598 against a 60,000-strong Chinese force, earning him the nickname "Kato the Demon"; he also hunted tigers for sport during this period, presenting the pelts to Hideyoshi.

His purely martial brand of loyalty put him at lasting odds with the Toyotomi clan's civil administrators, especially Ishida Mitsunari, whom he blamed for tensions that cost the lives of fellow generals during the Korean campaign; after Hideyoshi's death in 1598, he joined seven other generals in confronting Mitsunari over assessment disputes. This hostility pushed him into Tokugawa Ieyasu's camp during the Sekigahara campaign in 1600, where he captured several castles and received in reward the other half of Higo Province, bringing his income to nearly 500,000 koku, though without renouncing his attachment to the Toyotomi house; he even attempted, in vain, to mediate between Hideyori and Ieyasu in 1611. He fell ill at sea while returning to Kumamoto after one such meeting and died on 2 August 1611, aged forty-nine; he was buried at the Honmyo-ji temple in Kumamoto.
