Born Chacha in 1569, the eldest daughter of Oichi and the daimyo Azai Nagamasa, she lost her father as a child when Oda Nobunaga crushed the Azai clan, and later her stepfather Shibata Katsuie and her mother, who took their own lives together in 1583 after their defeat by Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who then became her adoptive father and protector.

Pregnant by Hideyoshi from 1588, to his great joy, as he then had no son, she received Yodo Castle in March 1589, remodelled for her by her brother-in-law. She bore him two sons, Tsurumatsu, who died in 1591, and Hideyori, born in 1593 and designated Hideyoshi's successor; the family moved to Fushimi Castle in 1594. Now known as Yodo-dono, after Hideyoshi's death in 1598 she wielded considerable influence as the young heir's mother and protector, settling at Osaka Castle, where she became its de facto ruler, also founding the Yogen-in temple in memory of her parents; during the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600, she chose to maintain a strategic neutrality, refusing to actively back either side or authorise Hideyori's deployment, while nonetheless standing against the rising power of Tokugawa Ieyasu.

The Hoko-ji Bell incident in 1614 gave Ieyasu a pretext for conflict; during the sieges of Osaka Castle in 1614-1615, she co-led the last armed resistance to Tokugawa authority alongside her son. When the shogunate broke the truce in 1615, she took her own life together with Hideyori as the castle fell, bringing the Toyotomi line to a final end; no account or record of her final moments survives, and her body was never found.
