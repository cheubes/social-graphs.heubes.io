Born in 1558 or 1565, likely in present-day Shiga Prefecture, the son of a father whose identity remains disputed, traditionally identified as Otani Moriharu, a retainer of the Otomo clan, without confirmation from the records, he had three known children, Chikurin-in, Otani Yoshikatsu and Kinoshita Yoritsugu; his daughter married Sanada Yukimura. A capable administrator in Toyotomi Hideyoshi's service, he served as a horse guard during the Mino Province campaign in the 1580s.

He took part in the Battle of Shizugatake in 1583, where he reportedly bribed Shibata Katsutoyo into defecting, then defended Tokugawa Ieyasu's trustworthiness against Ishida Mitsunari's suspicions during the siege of Odawara in 1590. He held the court title of Junior Assistant Minister of Justice (Gyobu-shoyu) and governed the Tsuruga Domain from 1589 to 1600. He shared with his friend Mitsunari the same rise through bureaucratic rather than military channels, which drew them close within the Toyotomi clan despite the illness, likely leprosy, that gradually weakened him, leaving him by 1600 nearly blind and unable to stand without help, requiring a palanquin to move about.

In 1600, though he first tried to dissuade Mitsunari from defying Ieyasu, judging the venture doomed, he ultimately chose to join him out of personal loyalty rather than calculation, committing some 6,500 troops to the Western Army. At Sekigahara, too weak to fight on horseback, he directed his troops from a litter and took his own life on 21 October 1600, aged thirty-four or forty-two depending on which birth year is accepted, after watching Kobayakawa Hideaki's defection decide the battle against his side, his routed troops letting Tokugawa forces exploit the collapsing flank.
