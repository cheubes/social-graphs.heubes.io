Born Mankichi on 22 December 1546 in Himeji, the son of Kuroda Mototaka, he became head of the clan at twenty-one when his father retired in 1567. He married Kushihashi Teru, with whom he had two sons, Kuroda Nagamasa, born in 1568, and Kuroda Kumanosuke, born in 1582, and also adopted Kuroda Kazushige. In 1577, he pledged loyalty to the Oda clan under Toyotomi Hideyoshi during the Chugoku campaign, then, imprisoned between 1578 and 1579 at Arioka Castle during Araki Murashige's revolt, suffered permanent damage to one leg and blindness in one eye.

A renowned strategist, he became one of Hideyoshi's most trusted military advisers, serving as chief strategist alongside Takenaka Hanbei and helping plan numerous campaigns: he fought at the siege of Takamatsu in 1582 and then at the Battle of Yamazaki, engaged between 1583 and 1585 at Shizugatake and Komaki-Nagakute, and led the conquest of Shikoku, also contributing to the siege of Odawara in 1590. Baptised as "Dom Simeão" in 1587 after discovering Christian communities in Kyushu, he nonetheless abandoned Christianity following Hideyoshi's edict and took the Buddhist name Josui. His crucial role in consolidating Hideyoshi's power paradoxically fed Hideyoshi's own fear that Kanbei might eventually overthrow him.

In 1600, while his son Nagamasa fought alongside Tokugawa Ieyasu at Sekigahara, Kanbei raised his own army on the island of Kyushu and led a lightning campaign there, marked by the fighting at Ishigakibaru and the siege of Yanagawa, taking advantage of the chaos, perhaps for a time harbouring ambitions of his own. Ieyasu's swift victory, however, cut short these designs before they could fully materialise. After Nagamasa succeeded him, Kanbei died on 20 March 1604 in Iizuka, in present-day Fukuoka Prefecture; his last words, it is said, urged humility, far from any pursuit of favour or wealth.
