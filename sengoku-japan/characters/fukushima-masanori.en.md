Born Fukushima Ichimatsu in 1561 in Futatsudera, Owari Province, the son of the barrel merchant Fukushima Masanobu, a cousin of Toyotomi Hideyoshi through his mother, the younger sister of Hideyoshi's own mother, he served from childhood as a page to Hideyoshi thanks to these family ties. His first battle, the siege of Miki Castle, took place between 1578 and 1580; after the Battle of Yamazaki in 1582, he was awarded 500 koku, then distinguished himself in 1583 at the Battle of Shizugatake by defeating the enemy samurai Haigo Gozaemon and taking his head, earning him 5,000 additional koku and the title of one of the "Seven Spears of Shizugatake."

Promoted to daimyo after the Kyushu Expedition in 1587, he received the Imabari fief in Iyo Province, worth 110,000 koku, then took part in the Korean campaigns between 1592 and 1598, capturing Cheongju as early as 1592. In 1595, he led ten thousand men in pursuit of Toyotomi Hidetsugu, earning a 90,000-koku increase and the Kiyosu fief. Like Kato Kiyomasa, his rivalry with the administrator Ishida Mitsunari, which came to a head in 1598 in a confrontation involving six other generals, led him, after Hideyoshi's death, to side with Tokugawa Ieyasu.

He played a leading role at the Battle of Sekigahara on 21 October 1600, where his troops, at the vanguard of the Eastern Army, opened hostilities against Ukita Hideie's forces, directly contributing to Ieyasu's victory, whom he then served faithfully as lord of the Hiroshima Domain until 1619, though his loyalty always remained tinged with attachment to the old Toyotomi house. Shortly after Ieyasu's death, he was accused of violating the Buke Shohatto by repairing Hiroshima Castle without authorisation; stripped of his 500,000 koku of territory, he was reassigned to a far more modest 45,000-koku holding and died on 26 August 1624, aged sixty-two or sixty-three.
