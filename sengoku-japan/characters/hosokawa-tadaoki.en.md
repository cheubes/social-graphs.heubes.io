Born Kumachiyo on 28 November 1563, the son of Hosokawa Fujitaka and Numata Jako, he fought his first battle at fifteen under Oda Nobunaga and received Tango Province in 1580. A cultured daimyo recognised as a master of the tea ceremony, he married Tama, the daughter of Akechi Mitsuhide, in 1578; she would later become known as Hosokawa Gracia.

When his father-in-law betrayed and assassinated Nobunaga in 1582, Tadaoki refused to follow him and remained loyal to Nobunaga's cause and later Hideyoshi's, at the cost of a painful rift with his wife, whom he kept secluded for several years out of political caution; he fought at Komaki and Nagakute in 1584 on Hideyoshi's side, then took part in the Odawara campaign in 1590, forging a friendship with Tokugawa Ieyasu during the 1590s.

In 1600, he sided firmly with Ieyasu, commanding five thousand men in the vanguard on 20 October at the Battle of Sekigahara; Gracia's death during the campaign, targeted by Ishida Mitsunari, only strengthened his resolve to fight, where he distinguished himself. Daimyo of Nakatsu from 1600 to 1602 and then of Kokura from 1602 to 1620, worth 370,000 koku, he also took part in the siege of Osaka between 1614 and 1615, and died on 18 January 1646 at the Daitoku-ji temple in Kyoto, aged eighty-two.
