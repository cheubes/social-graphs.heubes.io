Born Sanada Nobushige, the second son of the daimyo Sanada Masayuki, he grew up in the shadow of a father famed for inflicting two humiliating defeats on the Tokugawa clan during the sieges of Ueda Castle between 1585 and 1600, notably delaying Tokugawa Hidetada's reinforcements before Sekigahara.

After the defeat of the Western coalition his father had joined, Yukimura was exiled to Mount Koya, where he spent some fifteen years in relative obscurity, before joining Toyotomi Hideyori's cause in 1614 during the first siege of Osaka Castle. There he built an advanced bastion, the Sanada-maru, which repelled several Tokugawa assaults.

During the second siege in 1615, leading a desperate charge, he came within a hair's breadth of reaching Tokugawa Ieyasu's own position before being overwhelmed by numbers and killed in battle. His bravery in this final battle earned him, in Japanese popular memory, the posthumous title of 'Japan's number one warrior.'
