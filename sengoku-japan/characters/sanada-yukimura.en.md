Born Sanada Nobushige, the second son of the daimyo Sanada Masayuki, he grew up in the shadow of a father famed for inflicting two humiliating defeats on the Tokugawa clan during the sieges of Ueda Castle between 1585 and 1600, notably delaying Tokugawa Hidetada's reinforcements before Sekigahara.

Following the Battle of Nagashino in 1575, his father inherited the Sanada clan and moved to Ueda Castle, where the young Nobushige in turn took the Sanada name; in 1582, the destruction of the Takeda clan by Oda-Tokugawa forces forced the Sanada to drift among various lords before becoming vassals of Toyotomi Hideyoshi. He married Chikurin-in, Otani Yoshitsugu's daughter and Hideyoshi's adopted daughter.

In 1600, he joined the Western Army against Tokugawa Ieyasu while his brother sided with the East; at Ueda Castle, the Sanada resisted and managed to hold off Hidetada's forty thousand men with only two thousand of their own. After the defeat of the Western coalition his father had joined, Yukimura was exiled to Mount Koya, in the Kii Peninsula, where he spent some fifteen years in relative obscurity, before joining Toyotomi Hideyori's cause in 1614 during the first siege of Osaka Castle. There he built an advanced bastion, the Sanada-maru, an earthwork barbican defended by seven thousand men, which repelled several Tokugawa assaults.

During the second siege, on 3 June 1615, leading a desperate charge, he came within a hair's breadth of reaching Tokugawa Ieyasu's own position before collapsing, exhausted, on a camp stool in the thick of the fighting; a recent theory suggests he died following a spear duel with Nishio Munetsugu. His bravery in this final battle earned him, in Japanese popular memory, the posthumous title of "Japan's number one warrior." He left several children, including Sanada Daisuke, who took his own life by seppuku alongside Toyotomi Hideyori when the castle fell.
