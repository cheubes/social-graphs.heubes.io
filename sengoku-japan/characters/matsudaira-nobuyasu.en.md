Born Takechiyo on 13 April 1559, the eldest son and designated heir of Tokugawa Ieyasu and Lady Tsukiyama, daughter of Imagawa Yoshimoto, brother of Kamehime, he married Tokuhime, daughter of Oda Nobunaga, became lord of Okazaki Castle in 1570, and commanded troops from his teenage years during the campaigns against the Takeda clan, distinguishing himself through his bravery, notably at the Battle of Nagashino in 1575. He had two daughters, Tokuhime, born in 1576, and Kumahime, born in 1577.

In 1579, Tokuhime wrote to her father Nobunaga accusing Lady Tsukiyama of a treasonous plot with the Takeda clan; the letter, which may have owed more to marital discord than to any real conspiracy, put Ieyasu, anxious above all to preserve his alliance with Nobunaga, in the position of having to confine and then execute his own wife.

Under pressure from Nobunaga, an ally whose support Ieyasu could not afford to lose, Nobuyasu, confined first at Ohama and then at Futamata Castle, received his father's order to take his own life; Ieyasu acknowledged that his son might not have been guilty, but feared he would seek revenge for his mother's death, a risk he judged unacceptable. Nobuyasu took his own life by seppuku on 5 October 1579, aged twenty, a painful episode that left the Tokugawa clan without a direct heir for a time.
