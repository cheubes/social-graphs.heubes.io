Born in 1530 at Yoshida-Koriyama Castle, in Aki Province, the son of the daimyo Mori Motonari and Lady Myokyu, the second son of Mori Motonari, he was adopted into the Kikkawa house, whose head Kikkawa Okitsune made him his heir, taking its leadership around 1550. He married the daughter of Kumagai Nobunao in 1547, with whom he had several children, notably Kikkawa Hiroie, who succeeded him as head of the clan.

He became one of the two military pillars of his father's "Two Rivers" system (Mori Ryosen), alongside his brother Kobayakawa Takakage, with Motoharu handling frontline combat while Takakage provided strategic support; renowned as a commander, he is said to have won sixty-four of his seventy-six battles. He fought at the Battle of Miyajima in 1555, claimed Izumo Province after defeating the Amago clan in 1566, took part in the battles of Torisaka and Tatarahama in 1568-1569, and then the siege of Kozuki Castle in 1578.

Fierce and uncompromising, he resisted any form of accommodation with Oda Nobunaga and later Toyotomi Hideyoshi far more stubbornly than his brother, embodying the most combative wing of the Mori clan in the face of advancing unification; he nonetheless took part in the siege of Takamatsu in 1582 and, after Nobunaga's death, served under Hideyoshi, taking part in the invasion of Shikoku in 1585. He died on 25 December 1586, aged fifty-six or fifty-seven, reportedly at Kokura Castle, shortly before Hideyoshi's invasion of Kyushu, and was succeeded by his son Kikkawa Hiroie.
