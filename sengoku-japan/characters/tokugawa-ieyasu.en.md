Born Matsudaira Takechiyo into a family of minor lords from Mikawa, he spent part of his childhood as a hostage and later as a vassal in the service of the powerful Imagawa clan. Imagawa Yoshimoto's death at Okehazama in 1560 allowed him to regain his independence and forge a lasting alliance with Oda Nobunaga, one that would hold for more than twenty years.

A loyal ally of Nobunaga and, after his death, a cautious supporter of Toyotomi Hideyoshi despite an initial clash at Komaki-Nagakute in 1584, Ieyasu patiently built up his strength in the rich eastern provinces of the Kanto granted to him in 1590. He became one of the five great regents named by the dying Hideyoshi to watch over his young son Hideyori.

This guardianship quickly turned to his advantage: in 1600, he crushed the Western coalition led by Ishida Mitsunari at Sekigahara, aided notably by Kobayakawa Hideaki's mid-battle defection. Named shogun in 1603, he founded the Tokugawa shogunate that would rule Japan for more than two centuries, and completed the elimination of the Toyotomi house during the sieges of Osaka Castle in 1614-1615, before dying of natural causes the following year.
