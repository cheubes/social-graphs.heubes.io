Born Matsudaira Takechiyo on 31 January 1543 at Okazaki Castle, Mikawa Province, the son of the daimyo Matsudaira Hirotada and Odai no Kata, daughter of the samurai lord Mizuno Tadamasa, into a family of minor lords from Mikawa, he was abducted at five by Oda Nobuhide and held hostage for three years at Honshoji Temple in Nagoya, then, after his father's death in 1549, transferred as a hostage to the court of the powerful Imagawa clan at Sunpu, where he remained until fourteen. He came of age in 1556, married Lady Tsukiyama in 1557, a relative of Imagawa Yoshimoto, fought his first battle in 1558 at the siege of Terabe, and adopted the Tokugawa surname in 1567.

Imagawa Yoshimoto's death at Okehazama in 1560 allowed him to regain his independence and forge with Oda Nobunaga, from 1562 through the Kiyosu Alliance, a lasting alliance that would hold for more than twenty years, sealed also in 1563 by the marriage of his son Matsudaira Nobuyasu to Tokuhime, Nobunaga's daughter. A loyal ally of Nobunaga, he gradually consolidated his power in Mikawa and then in Totomi and Suruga provinces, suffering defeat at Mikatagahara against Takeda Shingen in 1573 before winning at Nagashino in 1575; in 1579, under pressure from Nobunaga, he forced his wife Lady Tsukiyama and his heir Matsudaira Nobuyasu to commit suicide following accusations of conspiracy with Takeda Katsuyori.

After Nobunaga's death in 1582, he narrowly escaped through Iga Province, a perilous journey that cost around two hundred lives among his escort, then fought in the Tensho-Jingo War for control of the former Takeda territories, before becoming a cautious supporter of Toyotomi Hideyoshi despite an initial clash at Komaki-Nagakute in 1584; he patiently built up his strength in the rich eastern provinces of the Kanto granted to him in 1590 and became one of the five great regents named by the dying Hideyoshi to watch over his young son Hideyori.

This guardianship quickly turned to his advantage: in 1600, he crushed the Western coalition led by Ishida Mitsunari at Sekigahara, aided notably by Kobayakawa Hideaki's mid-battle defection. Named shogun in 1603, he founded the Tokugawa shogunate that would rule Japan for more than two centuries, formally retiring in 1605 while retaining de facto control, and completed the elimination of the Toyotomi house during the sieges of Osaka Castle in 1614-1615, before dying on 1 June 1616 at Sunpu Castle, aged seventy-three, of natural causes.
