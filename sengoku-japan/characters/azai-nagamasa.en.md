Born in 1545 in Omi Province, the son of Azai Hisamasa, who stepped down in 1560 under pressure from his retainers, allowing him to take the leadership of the Azai clan, Azai Nagamasa won several victories between 1560 and 1565 against rivals such as Rokkaku Yoshikata and Saito Tatsuoki. He sealed an alliance with Oda Nobunaga in 1564 by marrying his sister Oichi, Nobunaga seeking this union for its strategic position between the Oda clan's lands and the capital, Kyoto; the couple had three daughters, Yodo-dono, born in 1569, Ohatsu, born in 1570, and Oeyo, born in 1573.

In 1570, forced to choose between his long-standing loyalty to the Asakura clan and his alliance with Nobunaga, he broke with the latter and joined the Asakura, catching Nobunaga off guard during his campaign against them; he took part that same year in the Battle of Anegawa alongside his Asakura allies against Nobunaga's forces. This betrayal, heavy with consequences for Oichi and their daughters, ended in the destruction of the Azai clan in 1573.

On 26 September 1573, during the siege of his castle at Odani, surrounded and his position hopeless, Nagamasa took his own life by seppuku, not before entrusting his wife and daughters to Nobunaga's care; Nobunaga later had their infant son, Manpukumaru, executed.
