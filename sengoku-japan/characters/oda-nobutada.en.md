Born Kimyo-Maru in 1557, in Owari Province, the eldest son of Oda Nobunaga and Kitsuno, possibly adopted by Nohime, and brother to several children including Oda Nobukatsu and Oda Nobutaka, he was given command of military campaigns from an early age: as early as 1575, he fought under his father, notably at the siege of Iwamura Castle, where he repelled a night attack by the Takeda army. He married Matsuhime, daughter of Takeda Shingen, with whom he had a son, Oda Hidenobu, who would succeed him as head of the clan.

On 28 November 1576, he received from Nobunaga control of eastern Mino Province and part of Owari, then defeated Matsunaga Hisahide at the siege of Shigisan in 1577 and commanded reinforcements during the siege of Kozuki Castle in 1578; Nobunaga formally handed him leadership of the Oda clan during his own lifetime, an unusual arrangement meant to secure a smooth succession. In 1582, he led the conquest of Kai Province, defeating Takeda Katsuyori at the siege of Takato and then at the Battle of Tenmokuzan, helping destroy the Takeda clan.

On the very day Akechi Mitsuhide surrounded his father at the Honno-ji temple, 21 June 1582, Nobutada was nearby at Nijo Castle. Rather than flee, he chose to resist the attackers before taking his own life by seppuku, aged twenty-four or twenty-five, depriving the Oda clan of both its leaders in a single day and opening the succession struggle that Toyotomi Hideyoshi would go on to win.
