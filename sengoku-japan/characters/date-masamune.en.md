Born Bontenmaru on 5 September 1567 in Yonezawa, the son of Date Terumune and Yoshihime, daughter of Mogami Yoshimori, he lost sight in his right eye in childhood, likely to smallpox, the eye being removed either by his own choice or by his retainer Katakura Kojuro, earning him the nickname "One-Eyed Dragon of Oshu." He led his first military campaign at fourteen, in 1581, alongside his father against the Soma clan, married Megohime, daughter of Tamura Kiyoaki, then took over the Date clan at seventeen, in 1584, succeeding his father.

Following his father's kidnapping and murder by Nihonmatsu Yoshitsugu in 1585, he waged a campaign of retaliation marked by the battles of Hitotoribashi in January 1586 and Koriyama in 1588, then conquered neighbouring territories through the sieges of Kurokawa and Sukagawa and the Battle of Suriagehara, consolidating his control over southern Mutsu Province by 1589. His expansion ran into Toyotomi Hideyoshi's ultimatum in 1590, who demanded his submission during the siege of Odawara; Masamune arrived at the last moment, narrowly avoiding execution, and was forced to give up part of his conquests, losing his ancestral territories in 1591. He received in exchange the fief of Iwatesawa, which he rebuilt as Iwadeyama Castle, and served in the Imjin War between 1592 and 1598.

After Hideyoshi's death, he drew closer to Tokugawa Ieyasu, becoming a loyal ally and taking part in the Sekigahara campaign in 1600; Ieyasu granted him the Sendai Domain, where he settled in 1604 with fifty-two thousand people, turning it into a major city. He pursued in parallel an ambitious and personal foreign policy, funding between 1613 and 1620 the construction of the ship San Juan Bautista and sending an embassy of one hundred and eighty people, led by his retainer Hasekura Tsunenaga, all the way to Pope Paul V in Rome, by way of the Philippines, Mexico and Spain, where several members of the expedition settled permanently. He died in Edo on 27 June 1636, aged sixty-eight, of oesophageal cancer complicated by peritonitis, and his eldest legitimate son, Date Tadamune, succeeded him.
