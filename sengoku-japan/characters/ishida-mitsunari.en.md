Entering Toyotomi Hideyoshi's service at a young age, Ishida Mitsunari distinguished himself less as a warrior than through his administrative and logistical skills, which made him one of the regent's closest aides, notably responsible for organising the Korean campaigns.

This rise through bureaucracy rather than the battlefield earned him the hostility of more martial generals such as Kato Kiyomasa and Fukushima Masanori, an internal rift within the Toyotomi clan that Tokugawa Ieyasu skilfully exploited after Hideyoshi's death in 1598.

In 1600, Mitsunari organised the Western coalition to counter Ieyasu's growing dominance, taking advantage of his departure on campaign against the Uesugi clan. The two armies met at Sekigahara; the mid-battle defection of Kobayakawa Hideaki, on whose support he had counted, precipitated the Western army's defeat. Captured while fleeing, Mitsunari was executed shortly afterward in Kyoto.
