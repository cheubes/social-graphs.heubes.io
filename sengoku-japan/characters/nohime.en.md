Born sometime in the 1530s, possibly 1535, the date uncertain for lack of reliable sources other than the Minonokuni Shokyuki, a seventeenth-century local journal noted for containing many errors, the daughter of Saito Dosan, daimyo of Mino Province, and Omi no kata, who died in 1551 and was described as "the most beautiful woman in Mino," from the Akechi clan and possibly a cousin of Akechi Mitsuhide according to disputed genealogies, Nōhime is said to have been married at twelve, in 1546, to Toki Yorizumi, becoming a widow, it is claimed, a year later.

She married Oda Nobunaga around 1549, likely in spring, when she was about fifteen and he sixteen, as part of an alliance meant to ease tensions between the Oda and Saito clans following her father's conflicts with Nobunaga's. The marriage produced no known heir, though she reportedly adopted Oda Nobutada, Nobunaga's son by a concubine; in 1556, her father was killed by her half-brother Saito Yoshitatsu. Contemporary sources say little about her, leaving her largely in the shadows of the chronicles, her last documented contemporary mention appearing in the Tokitsugu Kyoki diary in 1569.

Her trail then fades: four competing theories circulate with no definitive answer, an early death soon after the marriage, a divorce in 1557 followed by exile to Akechi Castle, considered unlikely, death alongside Nobunaga during the Honno-ji incident in 1582, for which no reliable source exists, or survival for decades, supported by a possible mention in 1569, references to an "Azuchi dono" in 1587 documents suggesting high status, and a burial record at the Daitoku-ji temple dated 1612. This documentary uncertainty, common for the wives of secondary-ranked daimyo, has not stopped later tradition from casting her as a political adviser to her husband, though no contemporary source directly confirms it.
