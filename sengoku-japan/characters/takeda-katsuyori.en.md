The son of Takeda Shingen, Katsuyori inherited a powerful domain on his father's death in 1573, but also weighty expectations, living in the shadow of a father who had become a legend in his own lifetime.

In 1575, he committed the supposedly invincible Takeda infantry at Nagashino against the fortified positions of Oda Nobunaga and Tokugawa Ieyasu, where the latter's massed use of the arquebus decimated his cavalry and shattered the clan's military backbone. Weakened and betrayed by several vassals, Katsuyori watched his domain collapse under the combined blows of Nobunaga and Ieyasu in 1582; surrounded, he took his own life, ending the Takeda line as a great power.
