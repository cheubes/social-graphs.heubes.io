Born in 1546 in Kai Province, the son of Takeda Shingen and Suwa Goryonin, daughter of Suwa Yorishige, brother of Takeda Yoshinobu, Takeda Nobuchika and Nishina Morinobu, he first succeeded to the head of the Suwa clan, inheriting Takato Castle. He married first Toyama Fujin, who died in childbirth in 1567, and then Keirin'in, daughter of Hojo Ujiyasu, and had several children, including Takeda Nobukatsu and Takeda Katsuchika.

He defeated Hojo Ujinobu at the siege of Kanbara in 1569, captured a Tokugawa possession at the siege of Futamata in 1572, then inherited a powerful domain on his father's death in 1573, but also weighty expectations, living in the shadow of a father who had become a legend in his own lifetime. He captured Takatenjin Castle in 1574, then built Shinpu Castle in 1581, where he settled, before losing the Takatenjin fortress that same year to Tokugawa Ieyasu.

In 1575, he committed the supposedly invincible Takeda infantry at Nagashino against the fortified positions of Oda Nobunaga and Tokugawa Ieyasu, where the latter's massed use of the arquebus, one of the earliest recorded uses of coordinated volley fire with three thousand muskets, decimated his cavalry and shattered the clan's military backbone. Weakened and betrayed by several vassals, Katsuyori watched his domain collapse under the combined blows of Nobunaga and Ieyasu in 1582; after losing Takato Castle and then fleeing Shinpu Castle, he took his own life by seppuku along with his wife and son on 11 March 1582 at Mount Tenmoku, following the Battle of Tenmokuzan, ending the Takeda line as a great power.
