Destined for monastic life as a younger son of the Ashikaga shogunate, Yoshiaki found himself a claimant to the title after the assassination of his elder brother, the shogun Ashikaga Yoshiteru, in 1565. After several years wandering in search of a protector powerful enough to restore him to Kyoto, he found the decisive ally in Oda Nobunaga, who had him proclaimed the fifteenth and last Ashikaga shogun in 1568.

Reduced to a figurehead by a Nobunaga with no intention of sharing real power, Yoshiaki grew resentful and, from 1570, secretly organised a broad anti-Nobunaga network of alliances bringing together the Takeda, Asakura and Azai clans as well as the Ikko-ikki Buddhist leagues. Discovered, he was defeated and driven out of Kyoto in 1573, effectively ending the Ashikaga shogunate after more than two centuries.

Taking refuge with various lords of western Japan, he nominally kept his title without real authority until formally renouncing it in 1588, under the protection of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, whose pensioned client he remained until his death.
