Starting from a modest domain, Mori Motonari made the Mori clan the dominant power in western Japan through a combination of diplomatic cunning and military victories, notably against the powerful Amako and Ouchi clans.

Anxious to secure his legacy's cohesion, he placed two of his sons, Kikkawa Motoharu and Kobayakawa Takakage, at the head of allied houses rather than let them contest power with each other, a system known as the 'Two Rivers' that effectively backed the eldest son who remained head of the clan. He died in 1571, before the great upheavals of unification, leaving a solid domain that his successors would later have to reckon with under Oda Nobunaga and then Toyotomi Hideyoshi.
