Born on an unknown date, the daughter of Sekiguchi Chikanaga, a retainer of the Imagawa clan, and a woman descended from Ii Naohira, possibly named Sena though this remains uncertain in the historical record, linked by lineage to both the Imagawa and Ii clans, Tsukiyama-dono married the young Matsudaira Motoyasu, the future Tokugawa Ieyasu, in January 1557, while he was still a vassal of the Imagawa clan; the marriage, arranged by Imagawa Yoshimoto himself, was meant to tighten ties between the two houses. She bore him a son, Matsudaira Nobuyasu, born on 13 March 1559, and a daughter, Kamehime, born in 1560.

When Ieyasu relocated to Hamamatsu in 1570, she remained at Okazaki Castle with their son; during this separation, Ieyasu pursued several affairs, including one with Lady Saigo and another with a maid named Oman, who bore him a son, Hideyasu. After Ieyasu broke with the Imagawa to ally with Oda Nobunaga, her Imagawa origins cast growing suspicion on her.

In 1579, she arranged for the daughter of a Takeda retainer to become a concubine for Nobuyasu, whose wife Tokuhime, Oda Nobunaga's daughter, then reported to her father that Tsukiyama-dono was conspiring with the Takeda clan against the Oda; Nobunaga informed Ieyasu, who ordered the executions to demonstrate his loyalty. Accused along with her son Nobuyasu of secretly conspiring with the Takeda clan, she was stabbed to death and then beheaded by three Tokugawa vassals on 19 September 1579, at Tomizuka in Totomi Province, aged thirty-eight, on her own husband's order, under pressure, tradition holds, from Nobunaga himself; her remains were interred at multiple locations, her head eventually resting at Yahashira Shrine. The truth of the accusation remains debated by historians today.
