Born in 1577 in Bitchu Province, the fifth son of Kinoshita Iesada and a nephew of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who adopted him before he was in turn given up for adoption to Kobayakawa Takakage to strengthen ties between the Mori clan and the regent, he bore several names in turn, including Hashiba Hidetoshi and Kobayakawa Hidetoshi, before settling on Hideaki; he inherited a considerable domain at a young age but struggled to establish himself as a military leader, while holding the court title of chunagon and the rank of saemon no kami.

He took part in the Korean campaign in 1598, leading reinforcements to rescue Ulsan Castle and personally fighting on the front lines with a spear; Hideyoshi punished him on his return for this reckless charge by stripping him of his Chikugo domain, a resentment that would weigh heavily afterward.

In 1600, courted by both sides on the eve of Sekigahara, he first officially joined Ishida Mitsunari's Western coalition while secretly negotiating with Tokugawa Ieyasu; his defection actually came at the very start of the battle, on 21 October, rather than midway through as popular tradition holds, his troops attacking Otani Yoshitsugu's positions rather than the Eastern ones, a move that tipped the outcome of the fight and earned him both an enlarged domain and a lasting reputation as a traitor in Japanese popular memory. He died on 12 January 1602, aged twenty-four or twenty-five, in the Okayama Domain, according to some sources after drinking himself to the point of losing his sanity; without an heir, the Kobayakawa clan was disbanded and its fiefdoms absorbed by neighbouring daimyo.
