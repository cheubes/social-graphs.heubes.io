A nephew of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, given up for adoption to Kobayakawa Takakage to strengthen ties between the Mori clan and the regent, Kobayakawa Hideaki inherited a considerable domain at a young age but struggled to establish himself as a military leader.

In 1600, courted by both sides on the eve of Sekigahara, he first officially joined Ishida Mitsunari's Western coalition while secretly negotiating with Tokugawa Ieyasu. In the middle of the battle, his troops, stationed in a commanding position, suddenly charged the Western lines rather than the Eastern ones, a defection that tipped the outcome of the fight and earned him both an enlarged domain and a lasting reputation as a traitor in Japanese popular memory.
