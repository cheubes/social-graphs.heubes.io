Born Inuchiyo on 15 January 1538 in Arako, in what is now the Nakagawa ward of Nagoya, Owari Province, the fourth son of Maeda Toshimasa, an unusual position from which to inherit clan leadership, he entered Oda Nobunaga's service as a page from childhood before becoming one of his generals, spending, like Nobunaga, a turbulent youth dressed in kabukimono style. He married Maeda Matsu, said to have been strong-willed from childhood and instrumental in his rise, and forged a lasting friendship over the course of the campaigns with Toyotomi Hideyoshi, then a lowly sandal-bearer destined for a very different future, their animal nicknames, "Inu" (dog) for Toshiie and "Saru" (monkey) for Hideyoshi, reflecting this closeness.

He fought at Kiyosu Castle between 1552 and 1554 and then at the Battle of Okehazama in 1560, received the Kaga Domain in 1580, which would become a wealthy million-koku territory, and switched sides mid-battle at Shizugatake in 1583 to support Hideyoshi. After Nobunaga's death, he thus backed Hideyoshi in the succession struggle and became one of his closest and wealthiest vassals, governing Kaga Province; he led thirty-five thousand soldiers in the Odawara Campaign in 1590.

His loyalty and prestige earned him appointment, shortly before Hideyoshi's death in 1598, as one of the five great regents entrusted with watching over the young Hideyori; he died, however, as early as 27 April 1599 in Kanazawa, aged sixty-one, after serving only one year in this role, depriving the Toyotomi clan of a counterweight capable of standing up to Tokugawa Ieyasu.
