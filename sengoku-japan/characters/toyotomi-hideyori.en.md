Born on 28 August 1593, the late-born and only surviving heir of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, born to his concubine Yodo-dono, a niece of Oda Nobunaga, Hideyori was just five years old when his father died in 1598 and was placed under the guardianship of a council of five great regents meant to safeguard his inheritance, among them Tokugawa Ieyasu; as a child, he reportedly practised calligraphy tracing wishes for peace throughout the world.

Ieyasu quickly established himself as Japan's de facto ruler after his victory at Sekigahara in 1600, reducing Hideyori to the status of a mere lord of Osaka; to ease tensions between the two houses, Hideyori married Senhime, Ieyasu's granddaughter. Having become by his teenage years a young daimyo at Osaka Castle and the rightful heir to his father's legacy, he worried Ieyasu, who saw him as a potential threat, particularly after the 1614 opening ceremony of the Great Buddha temple in Kyoto.

Fearing a resurgence of the Toyotomi cause, Ieyasu and his son launched the Winter Siege of Osaka Castle in 1614, at the end of which Hideyori was induced to dismantle the castle's defences, and then, in June 1615, the Summer Siege, which resulted in the fortress's destruction. Hideyori, backed notably by the general Sanada Yukimura, put up fierce resistance before taking his own life alongside his mother on 4 June 1615, aged twenty-one, as the castle fell, ending the Toyotomi line and the last major uprising against Tokugawa rule for roughly two hundred and fifty years.
