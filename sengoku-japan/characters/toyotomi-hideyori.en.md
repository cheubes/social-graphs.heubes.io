The late-born and only surviving heir of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, born to his concubine Yodo-dono, Hideyori was just five years old when his father died in 1598 and was placed under the guardianship of a council of five great regents meant to safeguard his inheritance, among them Tokugawa Ieyasu.

Ieyasu quickly established himself as Japan's de facto ruler after his victory at Sekigahara in 1600, reducing Hideyori to the status of a mere lord of Osaka. Fearing a resurgence of the Toyotomi cause, Ieyasu and his son launched the two sieges of Osaka Castle in 1614 and 1615, where Hideyori, backed notably by the general Sanada Yukimura, put up fierce resistance before taking his own life alongside his mother as the castle fell, ending the Toyotomi line.
