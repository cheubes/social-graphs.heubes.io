Born on 16 October 1535 in what is now the Nishi ward of Nagoya, Owari Province, he married a niece of Oda Nobunaga, strengthening his ties to the ruling clan, and had a son, Niwa Nagashige, later lord of Shirakawa Castle. A long-serving general of Nobunaga, known for his quiet loyalty and administrative efficiency, he served from his youth as a senior retainer, taking part from the Battle of Kiyosu Castle against Oda Nobutomo, and then in engagements in 1555 and 1558.

He took part in most of the clan's major campaigns during the 1560s and 1570s: the Mino campaign in 1567, the Omi campaign in 1568, the Honganji campaign from 1570 to 1580, and the sieges of Mount Hiei and Ichijodani Castle in 1573. Named an administrator of Kyoto after Nobunaga entered the city in 1568, he oversaw the construction of Azuchi Castle between 1576 and 1579.

After Nobunaga's assassination by Akechi Mitsuhide in 1582, he abandoned his Shikoku campaign to join Hashiba Hideyoshi in avenging his lord at the Battle of Yamazaki, choosing to back Hideyoshi in the succession struggle against Shibata Katsuie and providing decisive support to the victory at Shizugatake in 1583, which earned him the provinces of Echizen and Kaga, territories valued at over 1,230,000 koku. This renewed loyalty allowed him to keep his fiefs until his death on 15 May 1585 in Nagakute, aged forty-nine, sources disagreeing over whether it was a natural death or suicide driven by a sense of having failed the Oda clan as Hideyoshi's power eclipsed it.
