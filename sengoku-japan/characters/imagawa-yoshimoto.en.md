The leader of one of the most powerful and refined clans of central Japan, Imagawa Yoshimoto extended his influence over Suruga, Totomi and Mikawa provinces, holding among others the young Matsudaira Motoyasu, the future Tokugawa Ieyasu, as a hostage and then a vassal at his court.

In 1560, at the head of a sizeable army, he marched toward Kyoto through Oda Nobunaga's territory. Confident in his numerical superiority, he let his guard down during a halt at Okehazama, where Nobunaga, hopelessly outnumbered, surprised him with a lightning attack and killed him. This unexpected defeat shattered the Imagawa clan's power at a stroke and freed Ieyasu, who immediately broke his allegiance to ally with Nobunaga instead.
