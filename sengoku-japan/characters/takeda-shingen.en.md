Born Katsuchiyo on 1 December 1521 in Kai Province, later named Harunobu, the son of Takeda Nobutora and Oi no Kata, brother of Takeda Nobushige and Takeda Nobukado, whose sisters married into the Imagawa and Anayama clans, he assisted his father at fifteen at the Battle of Un no Kuchi in 1536. Around 1540, at about nineteen, when his father reportedly planned to name his younger brother Nobushige as heir instead, he rebelled and forced him, in a bloodless coup, to retire to Suruga Province under Imagawa supervision, establishing an alliance between the two clans.

After deposing his own father in 1541, taking over as head of the clan, he turned Kai Province into one of the best-administered and most militarily feared domains in Japan, renowned in particular for the discipline of its cavalry, conquering Shinano Province throughout the 1540s and 1550s through a series of sieges and battles; he took Buddhist vows in 1559 and received the dharma name Shingen.

From 1561 to 1572, across five Battles of Kawanakajima, he fought Uesugi Kenshin of Echigo Province, a rivalry with no decisive victor that became legendary, the fourth battle in 1561 featuring a famous single combat in which Kenshin attacked him with his sword while he defended himself with his iron war fan; he lost two generals in these battles, including his brother Nobushige and Yamamoto Kansuke. He invaded and secured Suruga Province between 1567 and 1569, before turning west late in life and attacking Oda territories in 1572, capturing Iwamura Castle; that same year, he crushed Tokugawa Ieyasu's forces at Mikatagahara, inflicting one of the worst defeats of Ieyasu's career and directly threatening Oda Nobunaga, his ally.

He died on 13 May 1573, aged fifty-one, in the midst of the siege of Noda Castle in Mikawa Province, the exact circumstances remaining uncertain between an old war wound, a sniper's shot, or pneumonia; he was buried at the Erin-ji temple, in present-day Koshu, Yamanashi Prefecture. His death deprived the Takeda clan of its most capable leader at a moment when it seemed to hold the upper hand; it likewise freed Nobunaga and Ieyasu from their most formidable opponent.
