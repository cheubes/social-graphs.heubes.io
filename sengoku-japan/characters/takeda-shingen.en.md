After deposing his own father in 1541, Takeda Shingen turned Kai Province into one of the best-administered and most militarily feared domains in Japan, renowned in particular for the discipline of its cavalry.

For more than a decade he fought Uesugi Kenshin in the famous battles of Kawanakajima, a rivalry with no decisive victor that became legendary, before turning west late in life. In 1572, he crushed Tokugawa Ieyasu's forces at Mikatagahara, inflicting one of the worst defeats of Ieyasu's career and directly threatening Oda Nobunaga, his ally.

His death in 1573, in the midst of a campaign and probably from illness, deprived the Takeda clan of its most capable leader at a moment when it seemed to hold the upper hand; it likewise freed Nobunaga and Ieyasu from their most formidable opponent.
