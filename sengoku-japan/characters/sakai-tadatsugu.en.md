Born in 1527 at Ida Castle, Mikawa Province, the son of Sakai Tadachika, the eldest of the Tokugawa's "Four Guardian Kings" served Matsudaira Hirotada and then the young Tokugawa Ieyasu from 1551 and became one of his most experienced commanders. He married Princess Usui in 1563, granddaughter of Matsudaira Hirotada and sister of Tokugawa Ieyasu, with whom he had a son, Sakai Ietsugu, born in 1564.

In 1565, he persuaded Ieyasu to abandon his allegiance to the Imagawa clan and became castellan of Yoshida Castle, before receiving command of eighteen Tokugawa daimyo as chief of the council of staff in 1567; Oda Nobunaga described him as "Ieyasu's right-hand man." He fought at the battles of Azukizaka in 1564, Anegawa in 1570 and Mikatagahara in 1572, took part in the siege of Yoshida Castle in 1575, handing the post of castellan to his son Ietsugu in 1578, and played a key role at Nagashino in 1575 through a decisive night raid against the Takeda positions.

As much a diplomat as a soldier, he repeatedly represented Ieyasu before Oda Nobunaga and later Toyotomi Hideyoshi, helping preserve the alliances on which the Tokugawa clan's later rise would depend, while also fighting during the Tensho-Jingo War in 1582 and then at Komaki-Nagakute between 1584 and 1586. He accompanied Tokugawa Hidetada to Kyoto in 1590 during the Odawara campaign, then retired; he died on 17 December 1596 in Kyoto, aged sixty-eight or sixty-nine.
