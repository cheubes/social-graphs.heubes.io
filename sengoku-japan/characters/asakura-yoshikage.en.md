Born on 12 October 1533 at Ichijodani Castle, in Echizen Province, the son of Asakura Takakage and a presumed daughter of Takeda Motomitsu, he succeeded his father in 1548 as the fifth head of the Asakura clan and lord of Ichijodani Castle. He negotiated settlements with the Ikko-ikki religious leagues during the early years of his rule, securing domestic stability that made Echizen a cultural refuge during the Sengoku period, with Ichijodani modelled after Kyoto.

Appointed regent by the deposed shogun Ashikaga Yoshiaki in 1568, after Oda Nobunaga took Kyoto, he briefly hosted Yoshiaki before the latter chose to turn instead to Nobunaga, seen as better placed to restore him. In 1570, he refused a summons from Nobunaga to a banquet in Kyoto; Nobunaga then invaded Echizen, besieging Asakura forces at Kanegasaki Castle. His alliance with Azai Nagamasa allowed him to catch Nobunaga off guard that same year, but the coalition met determined resistance in July 1570 at the Battle of Anegawa, where the superior Oda-Tokugawa forces prevailed; Yoshikage then took refuge at the Enryaku-ji monastery, securing a three-year truce.

Weakened by years of attrition, the Asakura clan was finally destroyed by Nobunaga in 1573 during the siege of Ichijodani Castle; betrayed by his cousin Asakura Kageaki, Yoshikage took refuge at the Rokubo Kensho-ji temple, where, surrounded, he took his own life by seppuku on 16 September 1573, aged thirty-nine, shortly after the fall of the Azai clan, his last ally.
