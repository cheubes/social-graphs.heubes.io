Born Kipposhi on 23 June 1534, likely at Shobata Castle in Owari Province, the son of the minor daimyo Oda Nobuhide, who died unexpectedly in 1551, Oda Nobunaga spent an unconventional adolescence for an heir, given over to hunting, riding, archery and arquebus practice, and married Nohime, daughter of Saito Dosan, around 1548-1549 as part of a political alliance. He inherited an unstable domain and first earned a reputation as an eccentric before crushing his internal rivals, including his own brother Nobuyuki, assassinated in 1557, and, in 1560, the far larger army of Imagawa Yoshimoto at the Battle of Okehazama, a stunning upset that launched him onto the national stage.

He installed the shogun Ashikaga Yoshiaki in Kyoto in 1568, then broke with him when Yoshiaki tried to rally a coalition against him; Yoshiaki's expulsion in 1573 effectively ended the Ashikaga shogunate. Nobunaga went on to crush the Azai and Asakura clans, inflicted a decisive defeat on the Takeda at Nagashino in 1575 through the massed use of the arquebus, and ruthlessly suppressed the Ikko-ikki Buddhist leagues that opposed him.

In 1582, while controlling only two provinces against a ring of enemies, he was betrayed by his own general Akechi Mitsuhide, who surrounded the Honno-ji temple in Kyoto where he was staying; cornered, Nobunaga took his own life as the temple burned. He died before completing the unification of Japan, but his military and administrative reforms laid the foundations on which Toyotomi Hideyoshi and later Tokugawa Ieyasu would build.
