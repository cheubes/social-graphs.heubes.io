Born around 1410, likely in Venice, from which his name derives, little is known of his youth. He moved to Florence around 1422-1423, still a child, to train under Gentile da Fabriano, and is said to have worked with Pisanello in Rome between 1423 and 1430; his style also bears the mark of Benozzo Gozzoli.

In 1438, he wrote from Perugia to Piero di Cosimo de' Medici seeking a commission for an altarpiece, which he painted between 1439 and 1441 (the Adoration of the Magi, now in Berlin). He then worked from 1439 to 1445 on the decoration of the Portinari Chapel at the Santa Maria Nuova hospital, where Piero della Francesca served as his assistant, a collaboration that left a lasting mark on the younger painter's style. He developed a luminous style with delicately handled colour, best seen in the Santa Lucia de' Magnoli altarpiece, painted between 1445 and 1447, whose finish Vasari mistakenly praised as an oil technique when the work is in fact executed in tempera.

Domenico Veneziano died in Florence on 15 May 1461, leaving behind a small body of work but a clear influence on the following generation; the legend recorded by Vasari that he was murdered by Andrea del Castagno is now dismissed, since Castagno died as early as 1457, four years before him.
