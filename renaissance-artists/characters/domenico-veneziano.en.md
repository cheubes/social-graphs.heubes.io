Despite his name, which points to Venetian origins about which little is actually known, it was in Florence that Domenico Veneziano spent the career documented by surviving records, from the late 1430s onward. There he developed a luminous style with delicately handled colour, best seen in the altarpiece of Santa Lucia de' Magnoli. He worked alongside Piero della Francesca, who assisted him on the now-lost frescoes of the church of Sant'Egidio, a collaboration that left a lasting mark on the younger painter's style. Domenico Veneziano died in Florence in 1461, leaving behind a small body of work but a clear influence on the following generation.
