Born on 7 June 1422 at the Castello di Petroia, near Gubbio, the acknowledged son of Guidantonio da Montefeltro though rumours always surrounded his true parentage, he was legitimised by Pope Martin V as early as 1424. Knighted by Emperor Sigismund in 1437, he began a career as a condottiero at sixteen under Niccolò Piccinino, before being hired in 1450 by Francesco Sforza, Duke of Milan.

He seized Urbino on 22 July 1444 after the assassination of his half-brother Oddantonio, initially ruling as count. That same year, 1450, he lost his right eye in a jousting accident; surgeons removed the bridge of his nose and his eyelid to preserve his remaining sight, a wound that reopened in 1453 and briefly threatened his left eye as well. He had that disfigured profile immortalised in the famous double portrait diptych Piero della Francesca painted of him and his second wife, Battista Sforza, whom he married in 1460 after the death of his first wife, Gentile Brancaleoni, in 1457; Battista herself died in 1472, from complications of childbirth.

In 1474, Pope Sixtus IV raised him to the rank of Duke of Urbino, sealing a close alliance between the two men. A keen bibliophile, he assembled one of the richest libraries in Italy after the Vatican's and had his ducal palace built by Luciano Laurana and then Francesco di Giorgio Martini, turning it into a renowned centre of art and learning. He died of fever on 10 September 1482 in Ferrara, while commanding Ercole I d'Este's forces against Venice.
