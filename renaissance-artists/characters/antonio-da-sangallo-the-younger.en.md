A nephew of the architects Giuliano da Sangallo and Antonio da Sangallo the Elder, he settled in Rome as a young man, working as a draughtsman for Bramante before later continuing his work on the construction of St Peter's. Appointed chief architect of St Peter's in 1520 alongside Baldassare Peruzzi, with whom he worked for more than fifteen years, he became sole architect of the basilica on Peruzzi's death in 1536. He also designed the Palazzo Farnese, one of the great civic palaces of papal Rome, and lent his skills to the pope's military fortifications. He died in 1546 without completing St Peter's, a project Michelangelo inherited after him.
