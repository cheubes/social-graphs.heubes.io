Born Antonio Cordiani on 12 April 1484 in Florence, a nephew of the architects Giuliano da Sangallo and Antonio da Sangallo the Elder, he settled in Rome as early as 1503, very young, with his uncle Giuliano to serve Pope Julius II, working as a draughtsman for Bramante before later continuing his work on the construction of St Peter's.

Appointed chief architect of St Peter's in 1520, after Raphael's death, alongside Baldassare Peruzzi as coadjutor, he became sole architect of the basilica on Peruzzi's death in 1536. He also designed the Palazzo Farnese (1517-1541) for Cardinal Alessandro Farnese, the future Pope Paul III, one of the great civic palaces of papal Rome, as well as the church of Santa Maria di Loreto, and lent his skills to military fortifications at Ancona and Perugia, and to the Well of Saint Patrick at Orvieto.

Commissioned in 1545 by Pope Paul III to dig a new canal, he died during the work on 3 August 1546 in Terni, without completing St Peter's, a project Michelangelo inherited after him.
