Born around 1489 in the small town of Correggio near Parma, from which he took his name, he trained in circumstances that remain poorly documented, though the imprint of Leonardo da Vinci's sfumato and Mantegna's architectural illusionism is clearly visible in his work. Active mainly in Parma, he painted the illusionistic domes of the church of San Giovanni Evangelista and then of the cathedral, where the Assumption of the Virgin unfurls a swirl of figures that already anticipates the Baroque. He spent most of his career away from the major artistic centres of Florence, Rome and Venice, without ever settling permanently in any of them. He died in Correggio in 1534, aged forty-five.
