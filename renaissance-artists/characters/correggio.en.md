Born Antonio Allegri around 1489 in the small town of Correggio near Parma, from which he took his name, into a family of Florentine origin, he trained in circumstances that remain poorly documented under local masters, though the imprint of Leonardo da Vinci's sfumato and Mantegna's architectural illusionism is clearly visible in his work, the latter likely encountered during a stay in Mantua around 1506. He married Girolama Merlini in July 1520.

Active mainly in Parma, where he spent his most productive years, he decorates the Abbess's Chamber as early as 1519-1520, creating a false pergola simulating an open-air garden through illusionistic effects, before painting the illusionistic domes of the church of San Giovanni Evangelista and then of the cathedral, where the Assumption of the Virgin unfurls a spiralling swirl of figures that seems to dissolve the architectural boundaries and already anticipates the Baroque.

He spent most of his career away from the major artistic centres of Florence, Rome and Venice, without ever settling permanently in any of them, though his influence would later reach Baroque painters such as Baciccio and Lanfranco, and then Neoclassicism. He died suddenly in Correggio on 5 March 1534, aged forty-five.
