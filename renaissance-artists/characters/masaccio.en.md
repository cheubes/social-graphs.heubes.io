Nicknamed Masaccio to distinguish him from his collaborator Masolino da Panicale, he rediscovered and extended, a century after Giotto, the naturalism and sense of volume of Florentine painting. With Masolino, he painted the Brancacci Chapel frescoes at Santa Maria del Carmine, including The Tribute Money, then executed alone the Holy Trinity in Santa Maria Novella, considered the first systematic use of mathematical perspective in painting. His work left a lasting mark on Fra Angelico and Piero della Francesca, both of whom absorbed his sense of volume and space. Masaccio died in Rome in 1428, at twenty-seven and in poorly understood circumstances, leaving the Brancacci Chapel unfinished; it would only be completed decades later by Filippino Lippi.
