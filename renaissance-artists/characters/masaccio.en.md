Born Tommaso di Ser Giovanni di Mone Cassai on 21 December 1401 in San Giovanni Valdarno, the son of the notary Giovanni di Simone Cassai, who died in 1406 when Masaccio was only five, and Jacopa di Martinozzo, he descended through his paternal grandfather and great-uncle from a line of carpenters, hence the family name Cassai; his younger brother Giovanni, nicknamed Lo Scheggia, also became a painter. Nicknamed Masaccio to distinguish him from his collaborator Masolino da Panicale, he is not documented in Florence until he joined the painters' guild on 7 January 1422, and little is known of his training.

He rediscovered and extended, a century after Giotto, the naturalism and sense of volume of Florentine painting, beginning with his first documented work, the San Giovenale Triptych (1422), and a collaboration with Masolino on the Virgin and Child with Saint Anne (1424-1425). With Masolino, he painted between 1425 and 1428 the Brancacci Chapel frescoes at Santa Maria del Carmine, including The Tribute Money and The Expulsion from the Garden of Eden, while also receiving the commission for the Pisa Altarpiece in 1426; he executed alone in 1427 the Holy Trinity in Santa Maria Novella, considered the first systematic use of mathematical perspective in painting.

His work left a lasting mark on Fra Angelico and Piero della Francesca, both of whom absorbed his sense of volume and space. Masaccio died in Rome in the summer of 1428, at twenty-six and in poorly understood circumstances, legend suggesting poisoning by a rival painter, leaving the Brancacci Chapel unfinished; it would only be completed decades later by Filippino Lippi.
