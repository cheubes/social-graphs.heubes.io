Born Luca d'Egidio di Ventura around 1441-1445 in Cortona, the exact date uncertain, he trained, according to his cousin Vasari, in Piero della Francesca's Florentine workshop, from whom he took a rigorous sense of construction before hardening the forms and heightening the anatomical power of his figures, also influenced by Perugian painters such as Benedetto Bonfigli, Fiorenzo di Lorenzo and Pinturicchio. Documented in Arezzo from 1472 and then in Città di Castello in 1474, he was active in Rome from 1478 to 1484.

Summoned by Pope Sixtus IV to complete the team of painters for the Sistine Chapel, he painted there between 1481 and 1482 the Testament and Death of Moses, a fresco measuring 350 by 572 centimetres, working alongside Perugino, their scenes on the chapel walls echoing one another. Pope Julius II recalled him to Rome in 1508 along with other prominent artists, though their work at the Vatican Palace was subsequently removed to make room for Raphael's commissions.

It was in the frescoes of the San Brizio Chapel in Orvieto Cathedral, contracted on 5 April 1499 for two hundred ducats for the ceiling and six hundred for the walls, plus provisions and wine, that his art reached its fullest expression between 1499 and 1503, with apocalyptic scenes and a Last Judgment whose anatomical violence anticipates Michelangelo; he reportedly based one crucifixion figure on his own son Antonio, who died of the plague during the cycle's execution. He died on 16 October 1523 in his native Cortona, where he had established his workshop, aged about eighty-one or eighty-two, having remained professionally active into his final year, completing a church altarpiece at Foiano.
