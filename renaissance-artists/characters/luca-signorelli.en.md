Born in 1441, he trained under Piero della Francesca, from whom he took a rigorous sense of construction before hardening the forms and heightening the anatomical power of his figures.

Summoned by Pope Sixtus IV to complete the team of painters for the Sistine Chapel, he worked there alongside Perugino, their scenes on the chapel walls echoing one another. It was nonetheless in the frescoes of the San Brizio Chapel in Orvieto Cathedral that his art reached its fullest expression, with scenes of the Last Judgment whose anatomical violence anticipates Michelangelo.

He died in 1523 in Cortona, Tuscany, where he had established his workshop.
