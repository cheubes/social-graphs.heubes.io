Born in 1399 or 1400 in Florence, the son of a member of the wool-workers' guild, he may have trained as a goldsmith under Leonardo di Ser Giovanni before collaborating on Ghiberti's bronze doors. Trained as a stone sculptor, he made his name between 1431 and 1438 with the marble cantoria of Florence Cathedral, made as a counterpart to Donatello's, whose naturalism left a lasting mark on his own work, and joined the guild of stone and wood masters in 1432.

He went on to perfect a tin-glazed terracotta technique, in blue and white, whose earliest surviving example, a Visitation in Pistoia, dates from 1445; the process, more durable and more expressive than bare stone, allowed him to produce weather-resistant religious reliefs at lower cost. He purchased a large house with a workshop in 1446 that remained the family's base until the 1520s, and in 1471 declined, on account of his age, election as president of the Florentine sculptors' guild.

This invention gave its name to an entire family workshop, which he passed on to his nephew Andrea and his great-nephews Giovanni and Girolamo, trained under him in his production methods, for both mass-cast pieces and individually modelled works. Luca della Robbia died in Florence in February 1482, taking with him, according to tradition, most of the secrets of his tin-based glaze, and leaving an abundant body of work in Tuscan churches and convents.
