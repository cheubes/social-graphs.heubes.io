Trained as a stone sculptor, he made his name in the 1430s with the marble cantoria of Florence Cathedral, made as a counterpart to Donatello's, whose naturalism left a lasting mark on his own work. He went on to perfect a glazed terracotta technique, in blue and white, which allowed him to produce weather-resistant religious reliefs at lower cost. This invention gave its name to an entire family workshop, which he passed on to his nephew Andrea, trained under him. Luca della Robbia died in Florence in 1482, leaving an abundant body of work in Tuscan churches and convents.
