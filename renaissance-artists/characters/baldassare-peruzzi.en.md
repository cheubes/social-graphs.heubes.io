Born in Siena, he trained there before moving to Rome in the early 1500s, where he assisted Bramante on the construction of the new St Peter's Basilica. Both a painter and an architect, he designed the Villa Farnesina for the banker Agostino Chigi, decorating some of its rooms himself with illusionistic frescoes.

Appointed architect of St Peter's in 1520 alongside Antonio da Sangallo the Younger, he was captured during the Sack of Rome in 1527, took refuge in Siena for a time, then returned to Rome to build the Palazzo Massimo alle Colonne.

He died in 1536 and was buried in the Pantheon, near Raphael's tomb.
