Born on 7 March 1481 in Ancaiano, a hamlet of Sovicille, near Siena, he trains there as a painter under Pinturicchio and Francesco di Giorgio Martini before turning to architecture from the age of twenty. Encouraged by the banker Agostino Chigi, he moves to Rome in 1503, where he assists Bramante on the construction of the new St Peter's Basilica.

Both a painter and an architect, he designs for Chigi the Villa Farnesina, begun in 1506, a pleasure residence that establishes his reputation and some of whose rooms he decorates himself with illusionistic frescoes. He succeeds Raphael in 1520 as coadjutor on the St Peter's project, alongside Antonio da Sangallo the Younger.

Taken prisoner during the Sack of Rome in 1527, he manages to free himself by paying a ransom and takes refuge in Siena, where he becomes architect of the Republic and works on the city's fortifications, before returning to Rome around 1535 to build the Palazzo Massimo alle Colonne. He dies on 6 January 1536, aged fifty-four, in relative poverty despite his artistic importance, and is buried in the Pantheon, near Raphael's tomb.
