Born around 1497 in Ferrara, he began his career in Bologna around 1519, producing painted terracottas for the Palazzo d'Accursio. He specialised in sculpted portraiture in terracotta and wax, a genre in which his skill earned him a strong reputation across Emilia, and in 1522 received the commission for a group of fourteen statues of the Transito della Vergine for the oratory of Santa Maria della Vita.

In the 1520s he collaborated with the sculptor Properzia de' Rossi on the portal reliefs of the Basilica of San Petronio, one of the city's great decorative projects, and presented Charles V with a bust of himself at the emperor's coronation on 22 February 1522, strengthening his standing at court. His major works include the Lamentation of Christ (c. 1522-1526) in Bologna Cathedral and marble bas-reliefs for the Arca di San Domenico in 1531, along with portrait busts of figures such as Pope Clement VII, now in the Palazzo Vecchio in Florence.

That reputation also drew the attention of Alfonso I d'Este, who brought him to the Ferrara court, where he became one of the duke's official sculptors. Cardinal Ippolito de' Medici then became his patron and brought him to Rome to design Pope Clement VII's funerary monument, a project that came to nothing after the cardinal's death in 1535. Back in Bologna, he died prematurely in 1537, having divided his career between the two cities.
