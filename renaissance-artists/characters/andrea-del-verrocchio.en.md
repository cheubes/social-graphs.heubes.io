Born around 1435 in Florence, he takes his name from his first master, the goldsmith Giuliano Verrocchi, under whom he trains before establishing himself as a sculptor and painter; he joins the sculptors' guild in 1469 and the painters' guild in 1472. After Donatello's death, he becomes the principal sculptor in Medici service, particularly for Lorenzo the Magnificent, and runs one of the busiest workshops in Florence, rivalling that of the Pollaiolo brothers.

There he trains several major artists, including Francesco Botticini, Perugino, Lorenzo di Credi and Leonardo da Vinci, who collaborates on his Baptism of Christ, painting one of the angels - a result said to have led Verrocchio to give up painting altogether to focus exclusively on sculpture. He produces his bronze David (1472-1475) and, for the Medici, the tomb of Piero and Giovanni de' Medici in San Lorenzo.

Summoned to Venice to cast the equestrian statue of the condottiero Bartolomeo Colleoni (1483-1488), he died on 7 October 1488 in Venice without seeing the bronze completed, a task finished by Alessandro Leopardi.
