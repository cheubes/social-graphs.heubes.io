According to tradition recorded by Vasari, the young Giotto, tending sheep in the Tuscan countryside, was discovered sketching by Cimabue, who took him on as his pupil.

He broke with his master's Byzantine stiffness, giving his figures volume, weight and human feeling, notably in the frescoes of the Scrovegni Chapel in Padua and the Bardi and Peruzzi chapels in Santa Croce, Florence. In 1334 he was appointed master builder of Florence Cathedral and designed the campanile that bears his name, which he did not live to see completed.

He died in 1337, a century before Masaccio rediscovered and extended his sense of volume and naturalism.
