Born around 1267, likely in Florence itself according to recent research, the son of the blacksmith Bondone, he is said, according to tradition recorded by Vasari, to have been discovered sketching his sheep in the Tuscan countryside by Cimabue, who supposedly took him on as his pupil, though this anecdote is now widely regarded as legendary. He married around 1290 Ricevuta di Lapo del Pela, known as Ciuta, with whom he had eight children; his son Francesco went on to become a painter as well.

He broke with his supposed master's Byzantine stiffness, giving his figures volume, weight and human feeling, painting frescoes at Santa Maria Novella in the 1290s, working in Rome from 1297 to 1300, and then completing around 1305 his masterwork, the frescoes of the Scrovegni Chapel in Padua. He went on to paint between 1306 and 1311 in the Lower Church of Assisi and, between 1329 and 1333, for King Robert of Anjou in Naples.

In 1334 he was appointed master builder of Florence Cathedral and designed the campanile that bears his name, which he did not live to see completed. He died in Florence on 8 January 1337, aged sixty-nine or seventy, a century before Masaccio rediscovered and extended his sense of volume and naturalism.
