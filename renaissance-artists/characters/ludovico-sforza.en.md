Born on 3 August 1452 in Milan, the fourth son of Francesco Sforza and Bianca Maria Visconti, initially excluded from the succession but given an extensive humanist education in classical studies, languages and the arts, he held various diplomatic roles after his father's death in 1466. After the assassination of Duke Galeazzo Maria in 1476, he first opposed the regency of Bona of Savoy and went into exile, before returning by force in 1479 and securing, on 3 November 1480, Bona's cession of the regency to him, which made him tutor to the young Duke Gian Galeazzo, his nephew.

Married to Beatrice d'Este on 18 January 1491 in Pavia, a marriage initially delayed by his relationship with Cecilia Gallerani but which grew deeply affectionate, he drew Leonardo da Vinci to his court, employing him as both artist and engineer, notably for irrigation projects, and the artist painted The Last Supper there and worked on the unfinished project for a monumental bronze horse honouring his father; the Milanese court became renowned for its artistic patronage and splendid festivals. On the mysterious death of Gian Galeazzo on 22 October 1494, which some contemporaries suspected was poisoning, he became duke in his own right.

In 1494, his initial alliance with King Charles VIII of France against Naples turned against him as French ambitions grew alarming; Beatrice, who died in childbirth on 2-3 January 1497, had firmly supported him through the crisis, notably when he suffered what may have been a stroke, and her loss devastated him, leaving him dressed in black and visiting her tomb twice a day. Without her support, he proved unable to resist Louis XII, Charles VIII's successor, and fled Milan with his sons on 1 September 1499; identified by a Swiss mercenary during the siege of Novara, he was captured in April 1500, imprisoned in France, first at Pierre-Scize and then at Loches from 1504, and died in his dungeon on 27 May 1508, aged fifty-five, following a failed escape attempt.
