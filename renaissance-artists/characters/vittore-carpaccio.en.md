Born Vittore Scarpaza between 1460 and 1465 in Venice, the son of the furrier Pietro Scarpaza, from a family originating on the island of Mazzorbo in the diocese of Torcello, documented since at least the thirteenth century, he was long thought to have first trained under Lazzaro Bastiani, though the most recent research favours an apprenticeship in the Bellini family workshop, under the guidance of Gentile and Giovanni Bellini, from whom he absorbed a sense of colour and light. His earliest known solo works, the Virgin and Child and a Salvator Mundi with Four Saints, date from around 1488-1490.

He became one of the leading narrative painters in Venice at the turn of the sixteenth century. For several Venetian confraternities, or scuole, he produced large narrative cycles: one devoted to the legend of Saint Ursula, begun in 1490 for the Scuola di Sant'Orsola and comprising nine large-scale paintings tracing the saint's legendary journey and martyrdom, and then, between 1502 and 1507, one for the Scuola di San Giorgio degli Schiavoni, centred on episodes from the lives of three patron saints and marked by a pronounced taste for Eastern Mediterranean imagery. He also collaborated between 1501 and 1507 with Giovanni Bellini on frescoes for the Doge's Palace, since lost in the 1577 fire, painted a Saint Augustine in His Study in 1502, and sat in 1508 on a committee assessing Giorgione's frescoes.

This attention to urban setting makes his work a valuable record of the Venice of his time. Settled in Capo d'Istria from 1516, where he painted a Sacra Conversazione, he decorated the organ shutters of its Duomo there in 1523, his last known independent work, and died in that same city between 1525 and 1526, both his birth and death dates remaining uncertain.
