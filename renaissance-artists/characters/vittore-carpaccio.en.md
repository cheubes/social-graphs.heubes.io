Working in the orbit of Giovanni Bellini, from whom he absorbed a sense of colour and light, he became one of the leading narrative painters in Venice at the turn of the sixteenth century. For several Venetian confraternities, or scuole, he produced large narrative cycles, including one devoted to the legend of Saint Ursula and another painted for the Scuola di San Giorgio degli Schiavoni, in which sacred episodes unfold against detailed views of Venice and its monuments.

This attention to urban setting makes his work a valuable record of the Venice of his time. Both his birth and death dates remain uncertain, placed around 1465 and 1525 respectively.
