Born on 5 December 1443 in Albisola, near Savona, in Liguria, Giuliano della Rovere, the son of Raffaello della Rovere and Theodora Manerola, of Greek descent, and one of three brothers with a sister, owed his ecclesiastical career to his uncle, Pope Sixtus IV, who made him Bishop of Carpentras on 16 October 1471 and then cardinal on 16 December that year. He held several bishoprics simultaneously between 1472 and 1477 through simony and pluralism, became Archbishop of Avignon in 1475, chamberlain of the College of Cardinals in 1479, and then Bishop of Ostia and papal legate to Bologna in 1483, the year his illegitimate daughter, Felice della Rovere, was also born; he fiercely opposed Alexander VI throughout his pontificate.

Elected pope on 1 November 1503 as Julius II, after the brief pontificate of Pius III, he launched the rebuilding of St Peter's Basilica, entrusted to Bramante, laying its foundation stone on 18 April 1506, and established the Swiss Guard that same year. He personally led military campaigns that earned him the nickname of the Warrior Pope: he expelled Giovanni Bentivoglio from Bologna in 1506-1507, formed the League of Cambrai with France and the Emperor in 1508, won the Battle of Agnadello against Venice in May 1509, and then formed the Holy League against France in November 1511, suffering defeat at Ravenna in April 1512 before forcing, with the help of Swiss mercenaries, the French retreat back across the Alps.

A demanding patron, he commissioned Michelangelo to paint the Sistine Chapel ceiling from 1508 and had Raphael decorate the papal apartments, including the Stanza della Segnatura, while also founding the Vatican Museums in 1506. Julius II died in Rome on 21 February 1513, aged sixty-nine, likely of malaria, and was buried at St Peter's, though the famous monument Michelangelo carved for him stands in San Pietro in Vincoli, leaving his successors two of the greatest artistic undertakings of the Renaissance.
