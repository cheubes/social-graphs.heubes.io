Born in 1443 near Savona, in Liguria, Giuliano della Rovere owed his ecclesiastical career to his uncle, Pope Sixtus IV, who made him a cardinal, before fiercely opposing Alexander VI throughout his pontificate.

Elected pope in 1503 as Julius II, he launched the rebuilding of St Peter's Basilica, entrusted to Bramante, and personally led military campaigns that earned him the nickname of the Warrior Pope. A demanding patron, he commissioned Michelangelo to paint the Sistine Chapel ceiling and had Raphael decorate the papal apartments, including the Stanza della Segnatura.

Julius II died in Rome in 1513, leaving his successors two of the greatest artistic undertakings of the Renaissance.
