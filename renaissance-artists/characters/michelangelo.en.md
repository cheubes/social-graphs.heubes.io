Born on 6 March 1475 in Caprese, Tuscany, the son of Ludovico Buonarroti, a judicial administrator and podestà of Chiusi della Verna, and Francesca di Neri del Miniato di Siena, who died in 1481 when he was only six, he studied grammar in Florence under the humanist Francesco da Urbino before briefly training, from 1488, in Domenico Ghirlandaio's workshop. Noticed by Lorenzo de' Medici, who took him into his Florentine palace alongside his own children, he attended the Platonic Academy between 1490 and 1492, where he encountered Marsilio Ficino and Pico della Mirandola, and studied anatomy through the dissection of cadavers at the church of Santo Spirito.

He carved a life-size Hercules in 1494 and arrived in Rome in 1496, where he executed the Pietà between 1498 and 1499, before returning to Florence to carve the David between 1501 and 1504. Pope Julius II commissioned his own tomb from him in 1505, a project that would stretch over forty years, and then the Sistine Chapel ceiling, painted between 1508 and 1512; Pope Leo X later commissioned him to design a façade for the church of San Lorenzo, a project that was never carried out, before he painted The Last Judgment on the chapel's altar wall between 1536 and 1541.

His rivalry with Leonardo da Vinci, and later with Raphael, active in papal Rome at the same time, defined much of the age; nicknamed "Il Divino" by his contemporaries, he was the first Western artist whose biography was published during his lifetime. He formed a deep late-life friendship with the poet Vittoria Colonna, met in Rome around 1536-1538, and developed an affection for the young Roman patrician Tommaso dei Cavalieri, met at fifty-seven, while composing more than three hundred poems. Appointed architect of St Peter's Basilica in 1546, he died in Rome on 18 February 1564, aged eighty-eight, leaving unfinished the dome that he had designed.
