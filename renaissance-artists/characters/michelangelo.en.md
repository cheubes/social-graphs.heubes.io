Born in 1475 in Caprese, Tuscany, Michelangelo briefly trained in Domenico Ghirlandaio's workshop before catching the eye of Lorenzo de' Medici, who took him into his Florentine palace alongside his own children.

Settled in Rome, he carved the Pietà and painted the Sistine Chapel ceiling for Pope Julius II, before Pope Leo X commissioned him to design a façade for the church of San Lorenzo, a project that was never carried out. His rivalry with Leonardo da Vinci, and later with Raphael, active in papal Rome at the same time, defined much of the age, even as he formed a deep late-life friendship with the poet Vittoria Colonna.

He died in Rome in 1564, leaving unfinished the dome of St Peter's Basilica that he had designed.
