Born on 1 January 1449 in Florence, the son of Piero de' Medici and Lucrezia Tornabuoni, considered the most promising of their five children, he received an elite education under the diplomat-bishop Gentile de' Becchi, the humanist philosopher Marsilio Ficino, and the Greek scholar John Argyropoulos. Cosimo's grandson, he took charge of the Florentine government in 1469, at the age of twenty, alongside his brother Giuliano, the same year he married Clarice Orsini on 7 February, with whom he would have ten children, including the future Popes Leo X and Clement VII.

On 26 April 1478, he survived the Pazzi conspiracy, which struck him and Giuliano in Florence Cathedral during mass: Giuliano was killed, while Lorenzo, only slightly wounded, escaped, the attack triggering papal retaliation and then war. A skilled diplomat, he maintained the balance of power established by the Peace of Lodi among the Italian states and travelled to Naples in person to negotiate peace in the aftermath of the Pazzi war.

A poet in his own right, he turned his court into a gathering place for humanists such as Marsilio Ficino, Angelo Poliziano and Pico della Mirandola, while patronizing painters including Botticelli, Ghirlandaio and the young Michelangelo, whom he welcomed into his own household; in 1471, he calculated that his family had spent some six hundred and sixty-three thousand florins on charity, buildings and taxes. He died peacefully on 8 April 1492 at his villa in Careggi, aged forty-three, while listening to the Gospel of the day, leaving his son Piero a position the Medici would lose by 1494.
