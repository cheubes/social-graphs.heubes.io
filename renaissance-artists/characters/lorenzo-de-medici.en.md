Cosimo's grandson, he took charge of the Florentine government in 1469 alongside his brother Giuliano, and was the sole survivor when the Pazzi conspiracy struck in 1478, killing Giuliano. A poet in his own right, he turned his court into a gathering place for humanists such as Marsilio Ficino, Angelo Poliziano and Pico della Mirandola, while patronizing painters including Botticelli, Ghirlandaio and the young Michelangelo, whom he welcomed into his own household. A skilled diplomat, he travelled to Naples in person to negotiate peace in the aftermath of the Pazzi war. He died in 1492, leaving his son Piero a position the Medici would lose by 1494.
