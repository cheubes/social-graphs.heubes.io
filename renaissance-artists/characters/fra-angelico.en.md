Born Guido di Pietro around 1395 in Vicchio, in the Mugello, he was first trained as a manuscript illuminator and worked as an assistant to Gherardo Starnina on the frescoes of the Dominican friary of Cortona between 1408 and 1418, before being documented as a painter in his own right in Florence from January 1418. He entered the Dominican order at the convent of Fiesole around 1423 under the name Fra Giovanni, alongside his younger brother Benedetto, also a Dominican, and absorbed from Masaccio a sense of volume and space that he put to the service of devotional painting.

Cosimo de' Medici funded the rebuilding of the convent of San Marco in Florence from 1436, where he painted, for the friars' cells, a vast fresco cycle conceived as aids to meditation, completing the San Marco Altarpiece in 1439 under his patronage. In his workshop he trained the young Benozzo Gozzoli, who assisted him before developing his own colourful, narrative style.

Summoned to Rome in 1445 by Pope Eugene IV, he painted there between 1447 and 1449 the frescoes of the Niccoline Chapel for Nicholas V, devoted to the lives of Saint Stephen and Saint Lawrence, before returning from 1449 to 1452 to lead the Fiesole convent as prior. Fra Angelico died in Rome on 18 February 1455, aged about sixty, and was buried at Santa Maria sopra Minerva; he was beatified by John Paul II in 1982 and declared patron saint of artists in 1984.
