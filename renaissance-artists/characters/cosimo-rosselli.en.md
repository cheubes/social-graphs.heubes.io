Born in 1439 in Florence, he ran an active workshop, though one whose standing never matched that of his more celebrated contemporaries. Sixtus IV nonetheless included him in the team of Tuscan painters assembled to decorate the walls of the Sistine Chapel, where he worked alongside Perugino. According to Vasari, aware that his drawing could not compete with his rivals', he loaded his frescoes with gold and ultramarine to catch the pope's eye, to the amusement of his fellow painters. He died in Florence in 1507, his career having remained in the shadow of the more gifted painters he worked alongside throughout his life.
