Born in 1439 in Florence, he became at fourteen, in 1453, a pupil of Neri di Bicci, who also trained his cousin Bernardo di Stefano Rosselli. He then opened his own workshop, active but never matching the standing of his more celebrated contemporaries.

Among his notable early works is a panel depicting Saints Barbara, Matthias and John the Baptist, completed in 1469 for the German confraternity chapel at Santissima Annunziata, where he also painted frescoes in the forecourt and adjoining convent. Sixtus IV included him in 1481-1482 in the team of Tuscan painters assembled to decorate the walls of the Sistine Chapel, where he worked alongside Perugino, Botticelli and Ghirlandaio, executing notably the Descent from Mount Sinai, the Last Supper and the Sermon on the Mount.

According to Vasari, aware that his drawing could not compete with his rivals', he loaded his frescoes with gold and ultramarine to catch the pope's eye, to the amusement of his fellow painters. Back in Florence, he continued an active career, notably at Sant'Ambrogio, and trained several pupils including Piero di Cosimo, Fra Bartolomeo and Mariotto Albertinelli; still alive on 25 November 1506, contrary to Vasari's claim, he died around 1507, aged about sixty-eight, his career having remained in the shadow of the more gifted painters he worked alongside throughout his life.
