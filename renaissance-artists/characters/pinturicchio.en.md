Born Bernardino di Betto in 1454 in Perugia, he likely trained under the Perugian painters Bonfigli and Fiorenzo di Lorenzo, before becoming, according to Vasari, a paid assistant of Perugino, alongside whom he worked in 1481 on the wall frescoes of the Sistine Chapel, painting an Assumption of the Virgin on the altar wall, later destroyed to make way for Michelangelo's Last Judgement in 1535.

Employed by the Della Rovere family between 1484 and 1492 to decorate chapels at Santa Maria del Popolo in Rome, he was then commissioned by Pope Alexander VI to lavishly decorate his Vatican apartment, an extensive fresco cycle painted between 1492 and 1494 mixing portraits, allegories and antique-inspired motifs. He painted the Doctors of the Latin Church at Orvieto Cathedral in 1496.

He closed his career in Siena between 1502 and 1507, where Cardinal Francesco Piccolomini, the future Pope Pius III, commissioned him to decorate the Piccolomini Library in the cathedral, devoted to the life of Pope Pius II; his Baglioni Chapel in Spello is often regarded as his masterpiece. He died in Siena in 1513, aged fifty-eight or fifty-nine, leaving behind a body of work marked by a bright palette richly heightened with gold.
