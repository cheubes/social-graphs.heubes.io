A native of Perugia, he trained in Perugino's circle, working alongside him in the 1480s on the wall frescoes of the Sistine Chapel. Pope Alexander VI later commissioned him to lavishly decorate his Vatican apartment, an extensive fresco cycle mixing portraits, allegories and antique-inspired motifs. He closed his career in Siena, where Cardinal Francesco Piccolomini, the future Pope Pius III, commissioned him to decorate the Piccolomini Library in the cathedral, devoted to the life of Pope Pius II. He died in Siena in 1513, leaving behind a body of work marked by a bright palette richly heightened with gold.
