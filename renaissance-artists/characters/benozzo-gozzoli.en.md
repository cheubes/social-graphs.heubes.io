Born in 1420 or 1424 near Florence, where he arrives in 1427, he trains under Fra Angelico at the San Marco convent, then works as a goldsmith's apprentice in Lorenzo Ghiberti's workshop between 1444 and 1447, contributing to the doors of the Florence Baptistery. He then turned to painting as an assistant to Fra Angelico, accompanying him to Rome from 1447, gaining recognition through frescoes in various churches, then remains in Umbria until 1456, producing important works at Montefalco.

Back in Florence in 1459, he developed a colourful, narrative style of his own, displayed to great effect in his masterpiece, the Procession of the Magi (1459-1460), painted for the Medici palace chapel, which includes portraits of his patrons and a self-portrait wearing a cap inscribed with the Latin words "opus benotii."

He then sets up a workshop in Pisa from 1468 to 1484, where he covered the walls of the Camposanto with a vast cycle of Old Testament frescoes, largely destroyed in the 1944 bombing. Back in Florence in 1495 following political upheaval, he dies of the plague on 4 October 1497 in Pistoia, his final works showing a more austere style, influenced by Savonarola's preaching.
