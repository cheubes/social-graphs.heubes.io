Born around 1406 in Florence, the son of a butcher, he was orphaned at the age of two and raised by his aunt Mona Lapaccia, who placed him at eight, for lack of means, in the Carmelite convent of Santa Maria del Carmine in Florence. Admitted to the novitiate in 1420, he took his vows at sixteen in 1421 and was ordained priest around 1425, remaining attached to the priory until 1432; it was there that he could study Masaccio's frescoes in the Brancacci Chapel, whose influence lastingly shaped his painting.

Leaving the convent in 1432 without being released from his vows, he returned to Florence, where he won the protection of Cosimo de' Medici despite a turbulent life: in 1458, while painting in Prato, he met the young novice Lucrezia Buti, whom he took as a model for his Madonnas before eloping with her; the couple had a son, Filippino, born in 1457, and a daughter, Alessandra, in 1465. He executed fresco cycles for his Florentine patrons in the cathedrals of Prato, between 1456 and 1465, and Spoleto.

Sandro Botticelli trained in his workshop, inheriting his sense of line and grace. Fra Filippo Lippi died in Spoleto on 8 October 1469, while still at work on the cathedral's decoration, in disputed circumstances, some suggesting poisoning by relatives of Lucrezia; Lorenzo de' Medici commissioned his tomb monument in the cathedral.
