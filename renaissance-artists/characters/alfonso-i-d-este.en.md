Born on 21 July 1476 in Ferrara, the son of Ercole I d'Este and Eleonora of Naples, he first married Anna Sforza in 1491, at fifteen, who died in 1497, and then, in a second marriage, Lucrezia Borgia, daughter of Pope Alexander VI, in 1501. He succeeded his father as Duke of Ferrara, Modena and Reggio in 1505, facing violent succession disputes with his brothers Ferrante and Giulio, whom he had imprisoned.

An enthusiast of artillery, he equipped his duchy with a renowned cannon foundry and clashed with the Papal States during the Italian Wars, first allied with Pope Julius II against Venice and then excommunicated in 1510 for refusing to acknowledge a papal peace treaty. He fought at the Battle of Ravenna in 1512 and eventually recovered his territories through skilful alliances.

A generous patron, he commissioned from Titian a series of mythological paintings for his camerino d'alabastro, continuing a cycle begun by Giovanni Bellini, and in 1529 opened one of the finest art galleries of his day. Close to his sister Isabella d'Este, with whom he remained a political ally, he died of indigestion on 31 October 1534 in Ferrara, aged fifty-eight, and was buried alongside Lucrezia Borgia at the Corpus Domini monastery.
