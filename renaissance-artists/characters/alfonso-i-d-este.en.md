The son of Ercole I d'Este, he succeeded his father as Duke of Ferrara in 1505, having first married Anna Sforza and then, in a second marriage, Lucrezia Borgia, daughter of Pope Alexander VI. An enthusiast of artillery, he equipped his duchy with a renowned cannon foundry and clashed with the Papal States during the Italian Wars, even incurring excommunication under Pope Julius II. A generous patron, he commissioned from Titian a series of mythological paintings for his camerino d'alabastro, continuing a cycle begun by Giovanni Bellini. Close to his sister Isabella d'Este, with whom he remained a political ally, he died in Ferrara in 1534.
