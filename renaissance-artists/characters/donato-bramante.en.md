Born near Urbino, he may have trained under Piero della Francesca before spending more than two decades at the Sforza court in Milan, where he designed, among other works, the tribune of Santa Maria delle Grazie. After the fall of Ludovico il Moro in 1499, he moved to Rome, where he built the small circular Tempietto of San Pietro in Montorio, a model of emerging High Renaissance classical architecture. In 1506 Julius II commissioned him to rebuild St Peter's Basilica, making him its first chief architect, assisted on site by the young Baldassare Peruzzi. He died in Rome in 1514, leaving the unfinished basilica to his successors, including Antonio da Sangallo the Younger.
