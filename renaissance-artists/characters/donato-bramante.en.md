Born Donato di Pascuccio d'Antonio in 1444 in Fermignano, near Urbino, he trained in that city, where around 1467 he encountered the painters Melozzo da Forlì and Piero della Francesca, who lastingly shaped his understanding of perspective. He moved to Milan around 1474, becoming from 1476 the virtual court architect of Duke Ludovico Sforza.

There he designed, among other works, the Casa Fontana-Silvestri around 1480, the church of Santa Maria presso San Satiro between 1482 and 1486, famous for its illusionistic apse that simulates depth in bas-relief where the site lacked space, and then the tribune of Santa Maria delle Grazie between 1492 and 1499. After the fall of Ludovico il Moro in 1499, he moved to Rome, where Julius II became his principal patron.

There he built in 1502 the small circular Tempietto of San Pietro in Montorio, a model of emerging High Renaissance classical architecture, before Julius II commissioned him in 1506 to rebuild St Peter's Basilica, making him its first chief architect, assisted on site by the young Baldassare Peruzzi; he also designed the Vatican's Cortile del Belvedere from 1505. He died in Rome on 11 April 1514, aged about seventy, leaving the unfinished basilica to his successors, including Antonio da Sangallo the Younger.
