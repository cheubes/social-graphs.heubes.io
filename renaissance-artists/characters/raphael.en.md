Born in 1483 in Urbino, Raphael trained in Perugino's workshop, absorbing his master's clarity and balance before surpassing them.

Settled in Rome, he was entrusted by Pope Julius II with decorating the papal apartments, where he painted The School of Athens, a role that Leo X, once elected pope, renewed at the head of the Vatican's major artistic projects.

His workshop, which trained Giulio Romano, grew into the largest in the city, even as he painted one of his most celebrated portraits of his friend Baldassare Castiglione, whom he had met at the court of Urbino, and carried on a famously bitter rivalry with Michelangelo. Raphael died in Rome in 1520, reportedly on his thirty-seventh birthday.
