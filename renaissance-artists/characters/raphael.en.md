Born on 28 March or 6 April 1483 in Urbino, the son of the court painter Giovanni Santi and Màgia Ciarla, he lost his mother in 1491 and then his father on 1 August 1494, at eleven, becoming the ward of his uncle Bartolomeo while managing the family workshop from a young age. Likely trained under Perugino around 1500, absorbing his master's clarity and balance before surpassing them, he was already regarded as a fully trained master by December 1500; his first documented work, the Baronci Altarpiece, dates from 1500-1501.

Invited to Siena by Pinturicchio in 1502 for cartoons, he spent a Florentine period between 1504 and 1508 absorbing Leonardo da Vinci's influence, before settling in Rome in 1508. Settled in Rome, he was entrusted by Pope Julius II with decorating the papal apartments, painting the Stanza della Segnatura between 1508 and 1511, which includes The School of Athens (1511), his masterpiece, as well as The Parnassus (1511); named architect of St Peter's Basilica in 1514 on Bramante's death, he was given full powers as Prefect over Roman antiquities in 1515. This role was renewed by Leo X, once elected pope, at the head of the Vatican's major artistic projects.

His workshop, which trained around fifty pupils and assistants, including Giulio Romano, Gianfrancesco Penni and Perino del Vaga, grew into the largest in the city, producing with the printmaker Marcantonio Raimondi around fifty engravings, even as he painted one of his most celebrated portraits of his friend Baldassare Castiglione, whom he had met at the court of Urbino, and carried on a famously bitter rivalry with Michelangelo. Raphael died in Rome on 6 April 1520, aged thirty-seven, reportedly on his birthday, leaving his Transfiguration unfinished, and was buried in the Pantheon.
