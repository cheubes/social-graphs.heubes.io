Trained as a goldsmith, Brunelleschi competed against Lorenzo Ghiberti in 1401 for the commission of the Florence Baptistery doors, narrowly losing. He then travelled to Rome with his friend Donatello to study ancient ruins, a trip that fed his research into the mathematical rules of linear perspective. Back in Florence, he won the commission for the dome of Santa Maria del Fiore in 1420, raising it without scaffolding through a self-supporting double-shell structure, with Cosimo de' Medici backing several of his projects. His friend Leon Battista Alberti dedicated his treatise Della pittura to him in tribute to his architectural feats; he died in 1446, before the lantern crowning his dome was completed.
