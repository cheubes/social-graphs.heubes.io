Born in 1377 in Florence, the son of the notary and civil servant Brunellesco di Lippo Lapi and Giuliana Spini, he trained from the age of fifteen as a goldsmith and bronze sculptor, becoming a master and joining the Arte della Seta guild in December 1398. He competed against Lorenzo Ghiberti in 1401 for the commission of the Florence Baptistery's bronze doors, where the two finalists were each required to depict the Sacrifice of Isaac; refusing the collaboration the jury proposed for want of full control over the work, he left the project to Ghiberti.

He then travelled to Rome with his friend Donatello to study ancient ruins, a trip that fed his research into the mathematical rules of linear perspective. Back in Florence, he designed the Ospedale degli Innocenti in 1419 and received in 1421 one of the very first patents in modern history for a river transport vessel, before leading the construction of the Basilica of San Lorenzo from 1421 to 1442 and of the Pazzi Chapel from 1430 to 1444.

He won the competition for the dome of Santa Maria del Fiore in 1420, raising it without scaffolding until 1436 through a self-supporting double-shell structure, a feat that occupied most of the last decades of his life and rose higher than the ancient Pantheon's. His friend Leon Battista Alberti dedicated his treatise Della pittura to him in tribute to his architectural feats; he died on 15 April 1446, aged sixty-eight or sixty-nine, before the lantern crowning his dome was completed, and was granted the exceptional honour of burial in the cathedral crypt.
