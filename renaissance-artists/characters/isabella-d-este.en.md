Born on 19 May 1474 in Ferrara, the daughter of Ercole I d'Este, Duke of Ferrara, and Eleonora of Naples, and regarded as her parents' favourite child among a family that included her sister Beatrice and her brother Alfonso I, with whom she remained politically allied throughout her life, she received a careful humanist education: she could recite Virgil and Terence by heart, mastered several languages, and studied the lute under Giovanni Angelo Testagrossa as well as dance under the Jewish dancing master Ambrogio. Betrothed at six, in 1480, to Francesco II Gonzaga, Marquess of Mantua, she married him by proxy on 11 February 1490, the celebration taking place on 15 February in Mantua, with a dowry of twenty-five thousand ducats.

The couple, whose relationship remained tense over political disagreements and a delayed motherhood before genuine affection developed, had eight children, including Federico II, later Duke of Mantua. She acted as regent of the marquisate when Venice took Francesco prisoner in 1509, retaining military control until his release in 1512 and hosting the Congress of Mantua that same year; on Francesco's death on 19 March 1519, she again ruled as regent for her son Federico, securing Mantua's elevation to a duchy.

An insatiable collector, she commissioned works from Leonardo da Vinci, Raphael, Titian, Michelangelo and Mantegna, whom she retained in her service, and filled the studiolo of the ducal palace with allegories painted by several masters, while running a vast network of correspondents, as attested by the roughly twenty-eight thousand letters she received and twelve thousand copies of sent letters that survive; she also patronised the composers Bartolomeo Tromboncino and Marco Cara. She died in Mantua on 13 February 1539, aged sixty-four, and was buried beside Francesco in the Church of Santa Paola, from which her remains would later be stolen.
