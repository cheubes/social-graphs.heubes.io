The son of a Venetian dyer, or tintore, from which he took his nickname, he is said by tradition to have spent a brief spell in Titian's workshop before continuing his training largely on his own. He reportedly displayed in his own studio the motto "the drawing of Michelangelo and the colour of Titian", a programme he carried out through a fiery manner and dramatic lighting effects. He devoted much of his career to decorating the Scuola Grande di San Rocco, where he painted several dozen canvases, before competing with Veronese for the major commissions of the Doge's Palace. He died in Venice in 1594.
