Born Antonio Benci on 17 January 1429 in Florence, trained as a goldsmith under Lorenzo Ghiberti in the family workshop he shared with his brother Piero, he then studied sculpture with Donatello and painting with Paolo Uccello, as well as architecture and engraving. He established himself in Florence as a painter and sculptor from his renowned workshop on via Vacchereccia, where the future masters Botticelli and Filippino Lippi trained, studying the human body in motion with near-scientific precision that extended Donatello's innovations.

His engraving Battle of the Ten Nudes became a touchstone for an entire generation, studied and copied by the young Michelangelo. He also painted a cycle devoted to the Labours of Hercules for the Medici, and likely sculpted the twins added to the Capitoline Wolf in Rome.

Settling in Rome around 1492 in the last years of his life, he cast the bronze tombs of Popes Sixtus IV and Innocent VIII there, and died in the city on 4 February 1498, aged sixty-nine.
