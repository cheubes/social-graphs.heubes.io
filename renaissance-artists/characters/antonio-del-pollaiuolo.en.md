Trained as a goldsmith in the family workshop he shared with his brother Piero, he established himself in Florence as a painter and sculptor, studying the human body in motion with near-scientific precision that extended Donatello's innovations. His engraving Battle of the Ten Nudes became a touchstone for an entire generation, studied and copied by the young Michelangelo. He also painted a cycle devoted to the Labours of Hercules for the Medici. Summoned to Rome in the last years of his life, he cast the bronze tombs of Popes Sixtus IV and Innocent VIII there, and died in the city in 1498.
