Trained as a goldsmith and medal engraver, work he continued at the Bologna mint even after taking up painting, he became official painter to Giovanni II Bentivoglio's court in Bologna from the 1490s onward. With Lorenzo Costa, he founded the Bolognese school of painting, marked by gentle devotional subjects and a refined feeling for landscape. His late works show a more classical grace shaped by Raphael's influence: Vasari claims, likely with some exaggeration, that a painting Raphael sent to Bologna so overwhelmed him that he died of the shock. He died in 1517, the very year that painting reached the city.
