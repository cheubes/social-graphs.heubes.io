Born Francesco Raibolini in 1447, likely in Bologna, he may have trained under Marco Zoppo and then worked as a goldsmith and medal engraver, a trade he continued as director of the Bologna mint even after taking up painting, first mentioned in 1486; his earliest documented work, the Felicini Madonna, is signed and dated 1494.

He became official painter to Giovanni II Bentivoglio's court in Bologna from the 1490s onward. With Lorenzo Costa, he founded the Bolognese school of painting there, marked by gentle devotional subjects and a refined feeling for landscape, shaped by Ercole de' Roberti; his workshop trained notably the engraver Marcantonio Raimondi.

After 1505, his late works show a more classical grace shaped by the influence of Perugino and of Raphael, with whom he was friends. Vasari claims, likely with some exaggeration, that a painting Raphael sent to Bologna, the Saint Cecilia, so overwhelmed him that he died of the shock; that legend, now discredited given their documented friendship, nonetheless coincides with his death in Bologna on 5 January 1517.
