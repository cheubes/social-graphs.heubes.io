Born on 24 February 1463 in Mirandola, near Modena, into the family that ruled the small lordship, the son of Gianfrancesco I Pico and Giulia Boiardo, and a cousin of the poet Matteo Maria Boiardo, he was named a papal protonotary at the age of ten and quickly mastered Latin, Greek and Hebrew. He studied canon law at the University of Bologna from 1477, philosophy at Ferrara, and then Hebrew, Arabic and Aramaic at Padua between 1480 and 1482 under Elia del Medigo, before joining the University of Paris in 1485 for theology and scholastic philosophy.

He met Lorenzo de' Medici and Marsilio Ficino in Florence in 1484, then proposed in Rome, in December 1486, nine hundred theses for public debate, blending Platonism, Neoplatonism, Aristotelianism, Hermeticism and Kabbalah, prefaced by his Oration on the Dignity of Man. Pope Innocent VIII halted the planned debate in February 1487, condemned thirteen of the theses, and made the work the first printed book to be universally banned by the Church; prosecuted, Pico fled to France in 1488, where he was arrested and imprisoned at Vincennes, before finally being cleared of the papal censures only in 1493, under Alexander VI.

He owed his initial freedom solely to the intervention of Lorenzo de' Medici, who protected him and gave him refuge in Florence, where he published an apology for his theses in 1489. Within the Medici circle, he studied under Marsilio Ficino and formed a lasting friendship with Angelo Poliziano; on Lorenzo's death in 1492, he moved to a villa near Fiesole. He died in Florence on 17 November 1494, aged thirty-one, the same year as his friend Poliziano and in circumstances that long remained unclear, rumour first pointing to poisoning by his own secretary; a 2007 exhumation confirmed arsenic in both men, possibly ordered by Piero de' Medici, Lorenzo's successor.
