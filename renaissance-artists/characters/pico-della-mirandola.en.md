Born in 1463 in Mirandola, where his family ruled a small lordship, he quickly mastered Latin, Greek and Hebrew and gained an early reputation for erudition.

In 1486 he proposed nine hundred theses for public debate in Rome, prefaced by his Oration on the Dignity of Man; several were condemned by the papacy, and only Lorenzo de' Medici's intervention secured his freedom and a refuge in Florence. Within the Medici circle, he studied under Marsilio Ficino and formed a lasting friendship with Angelo Poliziano.

He died in 1494, the same year as his friend, in circumstances that remain unclear and that tradition attributes to poisoning.
