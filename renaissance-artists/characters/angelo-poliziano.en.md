Born Angelo Ambrogini in 1454 in Montepulciano, which gave him his name, he lost his father young, moved to Florence, and studied Greek and Latin there in humanist circles, under John Argyropoulos, before befriending Marsilio Ficino.

Taken into the Medici household around 1473, he became tutor to Lorenzo de' Medici's children, including the future Pope Leo X, and wrote major works such as the Stanze per la giostra (1475-1478), celebrating Giuliano de' Medici's tournament victory, and the dramatic fable Orfeo, written in 1480 during a stay in Mantua. Present in the cathedral during the Pazzi conspiracy of 1478, an episode that interrupted work on the Stanze and prompted his temporary departure from Florence, he helped Lorenzo take refuge in the sacristy while Giuliano, Lorenzo's brother, was murdered.

Reconciled with Lorenzo, he obtained a chair of rhetoric at the University of Florence, which he held until his death. His philological work, particularly the Miscellanea (1489), grounded in rigorous textual criticism, established him as one of the founders of modern philology. He died in 1494, the same year as his friend Pico della Mirandola, with whom he was buried at the San Marco convent.
