Born on 29 June 1475 at the ducal palace of Ferrara, the daughter of Ercole I d'Este and Eleonora of Naples, Isabella d'Este's younger sister, her birth first disappoints her father, who had hoped for a male heir. Sent at two to the court of her grandfather King Ferdinand of Naples, where she is cherished as his own daughter under the guardianship of her aunt Ippolita Maria Sforza, she grows up there for eight years in a refined aristocratic setting before returning to Ferrara at ten, in 1485, the year her betrothal to Ludovico Sforza, regent and later Duke of Milan, is arranged.

The marriage is celebrated on 17 January 1491 in Pavia. At the Milanese court, where Leonardo da Vinci was then in her husband's service, she emerged as an active political figure, leading a diplomatic mission to Venice in May 1493 to strengthen Ludovico's alliance with the Republic, and gives birth to two sons, Ercole Massimiliano on 23 January 1493 and Francesco on 4 February 1495. Fond of festivities and fine dress, she helped make Milan one of the most brilliant artistic centres of northern Italy.

During the French invasion of 1494-1495, she distinguishes herself through her courage, named governor of Milan alongside her brother, and travels alone to the military camp at Vigevano in June 1495 to rally the Milanese troops against the Duke of Orléans. Pregnant a third time, she died in the night of 2-3 January 1497, aged twenty-one, along with the child; Ludovico Sforza, overcome with grief, withdraws for a time from political life. She is buried at the church of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan.
