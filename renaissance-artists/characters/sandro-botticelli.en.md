Born Alessandro di Mariano di Vanni Filipepi around 1445 in Florence, the youngest of four children who survived to adulthood, the son of the tanner Mariano di Vanni d'Amedeo Filipepi and Smeralda Filipepi, he owed his nickname Botticelli to that of his brother Giovanni, "little barrel." Apprenticed around 1461-1462 to Fra Filippo Lippi, inheriting his master's sense of line and grace, he likely left the workshop by April 1467, when Lippi moved to Spoleto, and may also have briefly trained with the Pollaiuolo brothers or Verrocchio.

He opened his own workshop by 1469, receiving in June his first documented commission, a panel of Fortitude for the Merchants' Guild, and took on as an apprentice in 1472 Filippino Lippi, his former master's son. He became one of Lorenzo de' Medici's favoured painters, who likely commissioned Primavera from him around 1482, while Marsilio Ficino's Neoplatonic thought and Angelo Poliziano's poetry shaped the iconography of The Birth of Venus around 1485; he also painted a Saint Augustine for the church of Ognissanti in 1480 and illustrated a luxury edition of the Divine Comedy in 1481.

Summoned to Rome by Pope Sixtus IV, he painted in 1481-1482 alongside Ghirlandaio and Perugino three major scenes on the walls of the Sistine Chapel, before sitting in 1491 on the committee for the façade project of Florence Cathedral. His style grew more personal and mannered in the 1490s, turning toward a more archaic look that ran against the classicism of Leonardo da Vinci's generation. He died in Florence on 17 May 1510, aged about sixty-four or sixty-five, his painting by then out of fashion, and was buried in his parish church of Ognissanti, where he had lived his entire life.
