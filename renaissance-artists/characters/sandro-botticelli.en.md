Born in 1445 in Florence, he trained in Fra Filippo Lippi's workshop, inheriting his master's sense of line and grace. He became one of Lorenzo de' Medici's favoured painters, who likely commissioned Primavera from him, while Marsilio Ficino's Neoplatonic thought and Angelo Poliziano's poetry shaped the iconography of The Birth of Venus.

Summoned to Rome by Pope Sixtus IV, he painted alongside Ghirlandaio and Perugino on the walls of the Sistine Chapel, before in turn training the young Filippino Lippi in his own workshop. He died in Florence in 1510, his painting by then out of fashion against the classicism of the following generation.
