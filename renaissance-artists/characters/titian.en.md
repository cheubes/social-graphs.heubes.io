Born around 1488-1490 in Pieve di Cadore, near Belluno, the exact date uncertain despite a late-life claim of 1474 now dismissed by historians, the son of the timber merchant Gregorio Vecellio, associated with the Venetian Arsenal, and Lucia, grandson of the noted notary Conte Vecellio and brother of the lesser-known painter Francesco Vecellio, he was sent, according to Ludovico Dolce, to Venice at the age of nine. He trained there in the workshop of Giovanni Bellini, where he worked alongside Giorgione, some sources also suggesting a spell with Sebastiano Zuccato and Gentile Bellini; no documented work survives, however, before 1510.

He worked between 1507 and 1509 alongside Giorgione on the now-lost façade frescoes of the Fondaco dei Tedeschi, then painted frescoes in Padua in 1511 for the Carmelite church and the Scuola del Santo. On Bellini's death in 1516 he became official painter to the Republic, completing that same year his Assumption of the Virgin for Santa Maria Gloriosa dei Frari, a masterpiece that established his reputation, then worked for Alfonso I d'Este, who commissioned from him between 1520 and 1523 Bacchus and Ariadne and a mythological series for his camerino d'alabastro in Ferrara.

A sought-after portraitist of the powerful, he painted Charles V in Bologna in 1533, who made him Count Palatine and Knight of the Golden Spur, then granted him an annual pension of a hundred scudi in 1541; a stay in Rome in 1545-1546, where he was offered, but declined, the position of keeper of the papal seal, brought him face to face with Michelangelo, while his intimate friend Pietro Aretino helped spread his fame across Europe from Venice. He produced from the 1550s onward his Poesie series for Philip II, who reaffirmed his pension in 1571, transferable to his son and assistant Orazio. He died in Venice on 27 August 1576, carried off by an outbreak of plague, aged about eighty-six to ninety, and was buried at the Frari, beneath the Altar of the Crucifix.
