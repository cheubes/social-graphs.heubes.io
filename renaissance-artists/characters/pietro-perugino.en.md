Born Pietro Vannucci around 1446-1452, the exact date uncertain, in Città della Pieve, Umbria, he likely trained first in the Perugian workshops of Bartolomeo Caporali or Fiorenzo di Lorenzo, before joining Verrocchio's Florentine workshop alongside the young Leonardo da Vinci; Piero della Francesca is also said to have taught him perspective. Enrolled as a master in the Confraternity of St Luke by 1472, he painted his Adoration of the Magi for Perugia around 1476.

Summoned to Rome by Pope Sixtus IV around 1480, he led the team of painters assembled in 1481-1482 to decorate the walls of the Sistine Chapel, alongside Sandro Botticelli, Domenico Ghirlandaio and Luca Signorelli, painting among other scenes the Delivery of the Keys. He maintained active studios in both Florence and Perugia from 1486 to 1499, married Chiara, daughter of the architect Luca Fancelli, in September 1493, with whom he would have three sons, and decorated the audience hall of the Collegio del Cambio in Perugia between 1496 and 1500.

His clear, balanced style won him a prestigious clientele, including Isabella d'Este, for whom he painted notably an altarpiece for the church of the Annunziata in Florence between 1504 and 1507, and left a lasting mark on his pupil Raphael, who trained in his workshop. Retiring to Perugia in 1512 after losing favour in Rome to Raphael, he died of the plague in 1523 at Fontignano, near Perugia, and, like other plague victims, was hastily buried in an unconsecrated field, his reputation by then diminished by the harsher judgment of the following generation.
