Born around 1431 in Isola di Carturo, near Padua, the son of a modest carpenter named Biagio, who died between 1449 and 1451, he enters, before the age of eleven, an apprenticeship with Francesco Squarcione, an influential Paduan painter whose renowned workshop and collection of antiquities, statues, bronzes and Roman reliefs lastingly shape his aesthetic sensibility; by 1445 he is already designated "Andrea pictore" in legal documents. He married Nicolosia Bellini in 1453, daughter of the painter Iacopo Bellini and sister of the painter Giovanni Bellini, who initially followed his compositions before developing a colour sense of his own.

After the altarpiece for Padua's Santa Sofia, his first documented work, he produces the Ovetari Chapel frescoes between 1450 and 1457, where his style, blending classical references and innovations in perspective, takes shape, then completes the San Zeno altarpiece in Verona in 1460. Called that same year to the court of Mantua by Marquis Ludovico III Gonzaga, for seventy-five lire a month plus housing and provisions, he became the Gonzaga family's official painter for more than forty years, receiving land in 1476 to build his residence and workshop.

He produces for their ducal palace the Camera degli Sposi (1465-1474) with its celebrated illusionistic oculus, then the great cycle of the Triumphs of Caesar (c. 1486-1492), nine large tempera canvases celebrating military glory. Between 1488 and 1500, he also works for Pope Innocent VIII, decorating Vatican galleries since destroyed. Isabella d'Este, who arrived in Mantua by marriage, made him the official painter of her studiolo, for which he painted Parnassus. He died on 13 September 1506 in Mantua, after more than four decades in the service of the same dynasty, and was buried in the Basilica of Sant'Andrea, whose funerary chapel was not completed until 1516.
