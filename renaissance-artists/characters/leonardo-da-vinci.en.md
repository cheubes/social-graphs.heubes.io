Born on 15 April 1452 in Vinci, near Florence, the illegitimate son of the Florentine notary Piero da Vinci and Caterina di Meo Lippi, a woman of modest standing, he had little contact with the sixteen half-siblings born of his father's successive remarriages. He entered Andrea del Verrocchio's Florentine workshop around the age of fourteen, training there for seven years in drawing, chemistry, metallurgy and metalworking, and qualifying as a master in the Guild of Saint Luke by 1472; his contribution to the Baptism of Christ, painted with Verrocchio around 1472-1475, reportedly convinced his master to give up painting.

Sent as an ambassador to Milan by Lorenzo de' Medici in 1482, he painted the Virgin of the Rocks there and then, between 1495 and 1498, The Last Supper for Santa Maria delle Grazie, worked on an unfinished bronze horse project for Francesco Sforza that was never cast, and decorated the Sala delle Asse of the Sforza Castle around 1498. Back in Tuscany after the fall of Ludovico il Moro in 1500, he entered the service of Cesare Borgia as a military architect in 1502, drawing a map of Imola, before returning to Florence the following year to begin, in October 1503, the portrait of Lisa del Giocondo, the Mona Lisa, and spend two years on the Battle of Anghiari, never completed.

His rivalry with Michelangelo became public when Florence commissioned each of them to paint a battle scene for the Palazzo Vecchio. Summoned to Rome in 1513 by Pope Leo X, he lodged at the Belvedere and then, in 1516, accepted the invitation of King Francis I of France and settled at Clos Lucé, near Amboise, where he designed, among other things, a mechanical lion for royal pageants. He died on 2 May 1519 at Clos Lucé, aged sixty-seven, likely from a stroke, leaving his pupil Francesco Melzi his paintings, instruments and library, and behind him thousands of pages of scientific notebooks.
