Born in 1452 in Vinci, he entered Andrea del Verrocchio's Florentine workshop as a young man, where his contribution to the Baptism of Christ reportedly convinced his master to give up painting.

Drawn to Milan by Ludovico Sforza, he painted The Last Supper there and worked on an unfinished bronze horse project, before returning to Tuscany to paint the Mona Lisa. His rivalry with Michelangelo became public when Florence commissioned each of them to paint a battle scene for the Palazzo Vecchio, never completed.

He died in 1519 in France, welcomed in his final years by King Francis I, leaving behind thousands of pages of scientific notebooks.
