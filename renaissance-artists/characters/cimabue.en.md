Born Cenni di Pepo around 1240 in Florence, the exact date uncertain, he owes his nickname Cimabue to a disputed origin, either "ox-head" or "proud man," depending on interpretations of the Italian verb cimare. Little is known of his training, likely acquired in Pisa, where the Byzantine tradition still permeates emerging Italian painting, from which the motif of the Christus patiens, the suffering Christ, emerges.

On 8 June 1272, he appears as a witness in Rome in a notarial act, identified as "Cimabove, pictore de Florencia," evidence that he is already an established artist in his thirties. Active in Florence in the second half of the thirteenth century, his works include the Santa Trinita Maestà and the Santa Croce Crucifix, now badly damaged, and he contributed to the fresco cycle in the Upper Basilica of Assisi.

According to the legend recorded by Vasari, he discovered the young Giotto drawing his sheep and took him on as a pupil, only for his student to soon surpass him. On 5 November 1301, he receives a commission for a polyptych with predella for the Santa Chiara hospital in Pisa, then works from September 1301 to February 1302 on the apse mosaic of Pisa Cathedral, where his documented contribution is the figure of Saint John. He died in 1302 in Pisa, shortly after the last recorded payment for that work.
