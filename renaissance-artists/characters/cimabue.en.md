Little is known of Cimabue's training; he was active in Florence in the second half of the thirteenth century, working in a manner still shaped by the Byzantine tradition of his time. His works include the Santa Trinita Maestà and the Santa Croce Crucifix, now badly damaged, and he contributed to the fresco cycle in the Upper Basilica of Assisi. According to the legend recorded by Vasari, he discovered the young Giotto drawing his sheep and took him on as a pupil, only for his student to soon surpass him. Cimabue died around 1302, while at work on the apse mosaic of Pisa Cathedral.
