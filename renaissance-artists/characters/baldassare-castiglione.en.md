Born in 1478 at Casatico, near Mantua, he served the courts of Milan and Mantua before joining that of Urbino in 1504, then among the most refined in Italy, where he formed a friendship with Raphael, who later painted one of his most celebrated portraits of him.

A diplomat, he drew on his years as a courtier to write The Book of the Courtier, a dialogue published in 1528 that set out the Renaissance ideal of the perfect courtier through fictional conversations at the Urbino court.

He ended his career as papal nuncio to Spain, dying in Toledo in 1529.
