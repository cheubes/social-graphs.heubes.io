Born on 6 December 1478 at Casatico, near Mantua, he studies the humanities in Venice and Milan under the scholars Giorgio Merula and Demetrios Chalcondyles, then serves at the court of Ludovico Sforza. On his father's death in 1495, he joins the Gonzaga court at Mantua, before joining that of Urbino in 1504 under Duke Guidobaldo, then among the most refined in Italy, where he forms a friendship with Raphael, who later paints one of his most celebrated portraits of him, and where he also mixes with Pietro Bembo.

He marries Ippolita Torelli in 1516, a noblewoman who dies four years later while he is serving as ambassador in Rome. In 1521, Pope Leo X grants him ecclesiastical status, opening his religious career. A diplomat, he also serves as ambassador to England and Rome, and draws on his years as a courtier to write The Book of the Courtier, a dialogue published in 1528 that set out the Renaissance ideal of the perfect courtier through fictional conversations at the Urbino court, becoming an influential manual of manners across Europe.

Pope Clement VII names him papal nuncio to Spain in 1524, where he accompanies Emperor Charles V across Spanish cities. After the Sack of Rome in May 1527, he faces accusations from which he is eventually cleared. He ends his career in Spain, dying in Toledo on 8 February 1529, aged fifty.
