As a young man, Paolo di Dono, known as Paolo Uccello for his love of birds, trained in Lorenzo Ghiberti's workshop, then busy with the Florence Baptistery doors. Brunelleschi's research into linear perspective fed a near-obsessive fascination in him, which he applied both to the equestrian fresco of John Hawkwood in Florence Cathedral and to the three panels of The Battle of San Romano, painted for the Medici. According to Vasari, this passion for perspective gradually isolated him from his contemporaries and from public favour, which had turned toward other pictorial modes. Paolo Uccello died in Florence in 1475, leaving behind a body of work that long stood apart in the painting of the Quattrocento.
