Born Paolo di Dono in 1397, likely in Pratovecchio near Arezzo, the son of the barber-surgeon Dono di Paolo and Antonia, a high-born Florentine, he apprenticed under the sculptor Lorenzo Ghiberti from 1412 to 1416, then busy with the Florence Baptistery doors, and joined the Compagnia di San Luca as early as 1414 and then, in 1415, the official Florentine painters' guild. He was earning an independent living as a painter by 1424, travelled to Venice in 1425 for the San Marco mosaics, and returned to Florence in 1431.

Brunelleschi's research into linear perspective fed a near-obsessive fascination in him, which he applied in 1436 to the commemorative equestrian fresco of John Hawkwood in Florence Cathedral, and then, in the 1450s, to the three panels of The Battle of San Romano, painted for the Medici, besides the clock figures and stained glass windows he made for the Duomo in 1443. Married around 1453 to Tommasa Malifici, with whom he had a son, Donato, born that same year and named after Donatello, and a daughter, Antonia, born in 1456, who became a Carmelite nun and a painter in her own right, he worked in Urbino between 1465 and 1469 for the Confraternity of Corpus Domini.

According to Vasari, this passion for perspective gradually isolated him from his contemporaries and from public favour, which had turned toward other pictorial modes; his 1469 tax return, in which he described himself as "old and ailing," depicts him living out his final years as a lonely, forgotten figure. Paolo Uccello died on 10 December 1475 at the Hospital of Florence, aged seventy-seven or seventy-eight, and was buried in his father's tomb at the church of Santo Spirito, leaving behind a body of work that long stood apart in the painting of the Quattrocento.
