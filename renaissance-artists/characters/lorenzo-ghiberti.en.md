Born Lorenzo di Bartolo in 1378 in Pelago, some twenty kilometres from Florence, he trained as a goldsmith under his stepfather Bartolo di Michele, who was close to his mother Fiore, and also received training as a painter under Gherardo Starnina, working around 1400 on frescoes for Carlo I Malatesta in Rimini. A sculptor and goldsmith, he won at twenty-one, in 1401, the competition for the Florence Baptistery doors, narrowly beating Brunelleschi, who abandoned the project to study architecture in Rome, leaving Ghiberti sole master of the commission thanks to a casting method that was more efficient, lighter and less costly.

He devoted most of the following decades to the project: the formal contract was signed in 1403 with Bartolo's workshop, Ghiberti legally took over the commission in 1407, earning two hundred florins a year, and the first doors, known as the North Doors, were completed and installed on 19 April 1424. He married Marsila, daughter of Bartolommeo di Lucca, in 1417 at sixteen, and had two sons, Tommaso, born in 1417, and Vittorio, born in 1418.

In 1425 he received the commission for a second set of doors, the East Doors, that Michelangelo would later call the "Gates of Paradise": their creation, spread over twenty-seven years, abandoned the traditional frame of twenty-eight quatrefoils for ten large rectangular panels applying the newly discovered principles of perspective to Old Testament scenes. His workshop became a training ground for a generation of Florentine artists, including the young Paolo Uccello and Benozzo Gozzoli, then a goldsmith's apprentice there; late in life he wrote his Commentarii, considered the first artist's autobiography. He died in Florence on 1 December 1455, aged seventy-five, of a fever of unknown cause, and was buried at Santa Croce, three years after seeing the Gates of Paradise installed on the Baptistery.
