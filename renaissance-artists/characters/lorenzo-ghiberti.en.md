A sculptor and goldsmith, Lorenzo Ghiberti won the competition for the Florence Baptistery doors in 1401, narrowly beating Brunelleschi, and devoted most of the following decades to the project: first the North Doors, then the East Doors that Michelangelo would later call the "Gates of Paradise".

His workshop became a training ground for a generation of Florentine artists, including the young Paolo Uccello and Benozzo Gozzoli, then a goldsmith's apprentice there. Late in life he wrote his Commentarii, tracing his own career and that of his contemporaries in what is considered the first artist's autobiography.

He died in Florence in 1455, three years after seeing the Gates of Paradise installed on the Baptistery.
