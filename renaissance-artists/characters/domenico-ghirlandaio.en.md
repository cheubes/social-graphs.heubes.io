Born in 1448 in Florence, the son of a goldsmith who specialized in garlands, from which his nickname is said to derive, he ran one of the city's busiest workshops together with his brothers. He painted for several merchant families, notably the Tornabuoni Chapel cycle at Santa Maria Novella and the Sassetti Chapel cycle at Santa Trinita, both populated with portraits of contemporary patrons. Summoned to Rome by Pope Sixtus IV, he took part in the Sistine Chapel wall frescoes of 1481-1482 alongside Sandro Botticelli and Perugino, while also working regularly for the Medici. The young Michelangelo briefly passed through his workshop before being noticed by Lorenzo de' Medici; Ghirlandaio died in 1494, probably carried off by an epidemic.
