Born Domenico di Tommaso Curradi di Doffo Bigordi on 2 June 1448 in Florence, the eldest of six children of whom only he and his brothers Davide and Benedetto survived to adulthood, he was the son of a goldsmith and silk merchant who specialized in the metal garland headdresses from which the family nickname Ghirlandaio is said to derive. Initially trained by his father, he then studied painting and mosaic under Alesso Baldovinetti, some sources also suggesting a spell in Verrocchio's workshop, and soon ran one of the city's busiest workshops together with his brothers.

He painted for several merchant families, notably the Sassetti Chapel cycle at Santa Trinita between 1482 and 1485 and then the more ambitious Tornabuoni Chapel cycle at Santa Maria Novella between 1485 and 1490, devoted to the Virgin and John the Baptist and populated with portraits of contemporary patrons. Summoned to Rome by Pope Sixtus IV, he took part in 1481 in the Sistine Chapel wall frescoes alongside Sandro Botticelli and Perugino, painting the Calling of the Apostles and a now-lost Resurrection of Christ, while also working regularly for the Medici.

His workshop, one of the most productive in Florence, took in the young Michelangelo, whose two best pupils, according to Vasari, Lorenzo de' Medici requested for his academy. Married twice and father of six children, Ghirlandaio died on 11 January 1494 aged forty-five, carried off by a pestilential fever, and was buried in Santa Maria Novella; his son Ridolfo carried on the family workshop.
