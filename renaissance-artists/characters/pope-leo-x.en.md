Born Giovanni de' Medici on 11 December 1475 in Florence, the second son of Lorenzo the Magnificent and Clarice Orsini, he received the tonsure at seven, destined from infancy for an ecclesiastical career, and held numerous benefices providing a steady income by the age of ten. Educated by Florence's greatest humanists, Angelo Poliziano, Pico della Mirandola, Marsilio Ficino and Bernardo Dovizio Bibbiena, he studied theology and canon law at Pisa from 1489 to 1491, and his father secured him a cardinal's hat in pectore as early as 9 March 1489, at thirteen, a nomination formalised before the College of Cardinals on 23 March 1492.

Appointed papal legate of Bologna and Romagna on 1 October 1511, he was ordained priest on 15 March 1513, consecrated bishop two days later, and then elected pope on 19 March 1513, at thirty-seven, prevailing over his rival Raffaele Riario with the backing of the younger cardinals. He continued his Florentine family's tradition of artistic patronage in Rome, keeping Raphael at the head of the Vatican's major projects and the rebuilding of St Peter's, commissioning Michelangelo to design a façade for the church of San Lorenzo that was never carried out, reorganising the University of Rome, and founding the city's first Greek printing press in 1515.

A cultured and lavish-spending pope, an accomplished musician, he multiplied feasts and commissions until, within two years, he had emptied the papal treasury his predecessor Julius II had carefully rebuilt, waging in 1517 the costly War of Urbino to secure his nephew Lorenzo's ambitions, and then turning to the sale of indulgences tied to funding St Peter's, which helped spark Luther's revolt in 1517; he excommunicated Luther on 3 January 1521 through the bull Decet Romanum Pontificem. Leo X died suddenly in Rome on 1 December 1521, aged forty-five, of bronchopneumonia, barely ten months after that excommunication, and was buried at Santa Maria sopra Minerva, leaving the papacy financially exhausted.
