Born in Florence in 1475, Giovanni de' Medici was the second son of Lorenzo the Magnificent, who secured him a cardinal's hat while still a teenager.

Elected pope in 1513 as Leo X, he continued his Florentine family's tradition of artistic patronage in Rome, keeping Raphael at the head of the Vatican's major projects and commissioning Michelangelo to design a façade for the church of San Lorenzo, a project that was never carried out. A cultured and lavish-spending pope, he multiplied feasts and commissions until the papal treasury ran dry, turning to the sale of indulgences in a move that helped spark Luther's revolt.

Leo X died in Rome in 1521, leaving the papacy financially exhausted.
