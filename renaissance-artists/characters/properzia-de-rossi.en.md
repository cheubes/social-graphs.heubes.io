Born around 1490 in Bologna, the daughter of the notary Giovanni Martino Rossi da Modena rather than an artist, unusual for a woman artist of her time, she received a broad education in painting, music, dance, poetry and classical literature, studying according to some sources under a sculptor at the University of Bologna; Vasari also notes that she "played and sang better than any other woman of her city." She first attracted attention for miniature reliefs carved into fruit stones, as well as copies of drawings by Raphael, a virtuoso exercise that revealed her technical skill early on.

She later turned to monumental marble sculpture: commissioned in 1525 by Bologna's Cathedral of San Petronio for Genesis relief scenes, she produced between 1525 and 1526 three sibyls, two angels and two bas-relief panels, including a Joseph and Potiphar's Wife now held in the Museum of San Petronio, alongside the sculptor Alfonso Lombardi, and likely a panel depicting the Queen of Sheba visiting Solomon. She also executed an engraved marble piece for the church of Madonna del Baraccano in 1526; according to Vasari, she was paid inadequately for this project due to sabotage by her colleague Amico Aspertini.

This recognition, rare for a woman in the field, made her the only female sculptor Giorgio Vasari included among the artists of his time in his Lives. She died in Bologna in 1530, during the same week as Charles V's papal coronation, and was buried, per her will, at the Della Morte hospital, a few years after working on that project, which remains her best-documented achievement.
