Born into the powerful Colonna family, she was betrothed in childhood, as was customary, to Ferrante d'Avalos, Marquess of Pescara, whom she married in 1509. Widowed in 1525 after her husband died from wounds received at the Battle of Pavia, she devoted herself to poetry and a demanding spiritual life, drawing close to Italian reformist circles associated with Juan de Valdés. A celebrated Petrarchan poet throughout Italy, she formed a deep friendship with Michelangelo in her later years, nourished by an exchange of letters, poems and drawings, including several religious sheets he made specifically for her. She died in Rome in 1547, leaving behind a body of poetry that ranked among the most widely read of her time.
