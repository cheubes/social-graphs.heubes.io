Born in April 1492 in Marino, in Lazio, the daughter of Fabrizio Colonna, grand constable of the Kingdom of Naples, and Agnese da Montefeltro, niece of the Duke of Urbino, she was betrothed at three, as was customary, to Ferrante d'Avalos, Marquess of Pescara. When her family's lands were confiscated by Pope Alexander VI in 1501, the Colonnas took refuge on Ischia, where she received from Costanza d'Avalos a typical humanist education in literature and the arts.

She married Ferrante d'Avalos on 27 December 1509 on Ischia; the couple lived together only briefly, until 1511, when her husband joined the military campaigns of the Italian Wars. Taken prisoner in 1512 at the Battle of Ravenna, he then spent years shifting between captivity and campaigning, while she wrote him passionate verse correspondence; her poetry began attracting attention as early as the late 1510s.

Widowed on 3 December 1525 after her husband died from wounds received at the Battle of Pavia, she first sought to enter a convent, a request refused by Pope Clement VII and her own brother, and remained on Ischia until 1529 before returning to Rome, devoting herself to poetry and a demanding spiritual life, drawing close to Italian reformist circles associated with Juan de Valdés; she published her Rime collections from 1538 onward, reprinted several times during her life and after her death. A celebrated Petrarchan poet throughout Italy, she met Michelangelo in 1536, at forty-six, when he was sixty-one, and formed a deep friendship with him in her later years, nourished by an exchange of letters, poems and drawings, including several religious sheets he made specifically for her. She died in Rome on 25 February 1547, aged fifty-six, at the convent of San Silvestro in Capite, leaving behind a body of poetry that ranked among the most widely read of her time.
