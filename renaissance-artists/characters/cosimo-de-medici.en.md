Born on 27 September 1389 in Florence, the son of Giovanni di Bicci de' Medici, founder of the Medici Bank in 1397, and Piccarda Bueri, he and his brother Lorenzo the Elder inherited the family bank and built it into the most powerful in Europe, with branches in Rome, Venice, Naples, London, Bruges and Geneva, managing much of the papacy's finances. He married Contessina de' Bardi around 1415, with whom he had two sons, Piero the Gouty, born in 1416, and Giovanni, born in 1421.

Imprisoned in the Palazzo Vecchio in September 1433 and then exiled by rivals led by Rinaldo degli Albizzi, he took refuge in Venice with his bank; the resulting flight of capital forced Florence to rescind his banishment within a year, and he returned in triumph in 1434 to rule the city from behind the scenes for thirty years, without ever holding a permanent official post. In 1450, he engineered Francesco Sforza's accession to the duchy of Milan, establishing a balance of power that secured nearly fifty years of relative peace in Italy.

A tireless patron, he spent over six hundred thousand gold florins on the arts and letters: he funded the rebuilding of the San Marco convent, where Fra Angelico painted and where he founded Florence's first public library in 1444, commissioned Donatello's David and Judith, backed several of Filippo Brunelleschi's building projects including the dome of Santa Maria del Fiore, made Michelozzo his house architect, and commissioned Marsilio Ficino's complete Latin translation of Plato's works. He died on 1 August 1464 at Careggi, aged seventy-four; Florence posthumously granted him the title pater patriae, inscribed on his tomb in San Lorenzo, and his grandson Lorenzo carried his legacy forward.
