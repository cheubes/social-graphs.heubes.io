Born Giorgio Barbarelli, probably between 1477 and 1478 (other sources suggest 1473-1474), in Castelfranco Veneto, he trained, like Titian, in the workshop of Giovanni Bellini in Venice, then the official painter of the Republic, and was likely influenced by Leonardo da Vinci's visit to the city in 1500.

While still in his twenties, in 1500, he painted portraits of Doge Agostino Barbarigo and the condottiere Consalvo Ferrante; in 1504 he received the commission for the altarpiece of Castelfranco cathedral. He went on to develop an enigmatic style of painting in which landscape carries as much weight as the figures, as in The Tempest around 1508 or the Sleeping Venus around 1510, left unfinished at his death. In 1507-1508 he worked alongside the younger Titian on the now-lost façade frescoes of the Fondaco dei Tedeschi in Venice, having received a first payment in 1507 for work at the Doge's Palace.

He died of the plague on 17 September 1510, aged somewhere between thirty-one and thirty-seven depending on which birth date is accepted, leaving a small and disputed body of work, whose burial site remains uncertain between the islands of Poveglia and Lazzaretto Nuovo, and whose influence shaped Venetian painting for decades, Titian's above all.
