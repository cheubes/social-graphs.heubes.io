Born in Verona, from which he took his name, he trained there before settling in Venice in the early 1550s. On arriving in the city, he drew on the brilliant colour of Titian, then the dominant painter of the lagoon, to develop a taste for large-scale decorative compositions, such as The Wedding at Cana, painted for the refectory of San Giorgio Maggiore. Summoned before the Inquisition in 1573 over the liberties taken in The Feast in the House of Levi, he simply renamed the painting rather than alter it. A rival of Tintoretto for the major commissions of the Doge's Palace, he died in Venice in 1588.
