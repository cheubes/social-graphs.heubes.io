A Florentine sculptor, Donatello travelled to Rome with his friend Brunelleschi to study ancient ruins, a trip that fed his reinvention of statuary.

He revived the classical nude and perspectival relief, most famously with his bronze David, the statue of St George at Orsanmichele, and the equestrian monument to Gattamelata in Padua, the first Renaissance bronze of its kind. He partnered with Michelozzo for over a decade in a joint workshop and enjoyed Cosimo de' Medici's support throughout his career, with Cosimo continuing to provide for him in old age.

Donatello died in 1466 and, at his own request, was buried near Cosimo in the basilica of San Lorenzo.
