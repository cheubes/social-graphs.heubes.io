Born in Arezzo to a family of modest means, he spent a turbulent youth between Perugia and Rome before settling permanently in Venice in 1527.

His biting pen, put to work in pamphlets and satirical sonnets, earned him the nickname "scourge of princes", as he praised or attacked the powerful depending on how well they paid him. Having become Titian's intimate friend and sitting for several of his portraits, he put his writing to work spreading the painter's fame across Europe.

He was less kind to Michelangelo, whom he publicly attacked in a now-famous letter criticising the nudity of the Last Judgement, before dying in Venice in 1556.
