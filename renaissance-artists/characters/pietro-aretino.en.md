Born on 19 or 20 April 1492 in Arezzo, the son of Luca Del Tura, a shoemaker who abandoned the family to join the militia, he was raised by his mother, Margherita, known as Tita Bonci, who entered into a relationship with the local nobleman Luigi Bacci, who provided for the family. He spent a formative decade in Perugia before moving to Rome, where the banker Agostino Chigi became his patron; his turbulent youth thus took him between Perugia and Rome before he settled permanently in Venice in 1527.

He launched his career as a pamphleteer in 1516 with The Last Will and Testament of the Elephant Hanno, a satire written after the death of Pope Leo X's favourite pet, then found in 1521, on Leo's death, a new patron in Cardinal Giulio de' Medici. His biting pen, put to work in pamphlets and satirical sonnets, earned him the nickname "scourge of princes," as he praised or attacked the powerful depending on how well they paid him; in 1525, erotic sonnets accompanying drawings by Giulio Romano provoked assassination attempts against him that forced him to flee Rome.

Having become Titian's intimate friend in Venice, who painted his portrait three times, in 1527, 1537 and 1545, and depicted him in several other works, he maintained a close relationship with him spanning nearly thirty years, acting as his publicist, adviser and closest companion, putting his writing to work spreading the painter's fame across Europe. He was less kind to Michelangelo, whom he publicly attacked in a now-famous letter criticising the nudity of the Last Judgement. He died in Venice on 21 October 1556, aged sixty-four, popular legend claiming he died from laughing too much, though the available evidence points instead to a stroke or heart attack.
