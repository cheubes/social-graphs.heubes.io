Luca della Robbia's nephew, he trained under him and inherited his glazed terracotta technique, which he refined and spread on a far larger scale.

His roundels of swaddled infants, in blue and white, adorn the façade of the Ospedale degli Innocenti, built by Brunelleschi, and rank among the most recognisable images of Florentine sculpture. He also supplied terracotta altarpieces for the Franciscan sanctuary of La Verna and for numerous Tuscan churches, heading a thriving family workshop.

Andrea della Robbia died in Florence in 1525, having in turn passed the tradition on to his own sons.
