Born on 20 November 1435 in Florence, Luca della Robbia's nephew through his brother Marco, he trained under him and inherited his glazed terracotta technique, which he refined and spread on a far larger scale, turning his uncle's workshop into a major artistic enterprise. Working mainly in blue and white, he did not consider himself strictly a sculptor, drawing his inspiration from the painting of his time rather than from sculpture.

His roundels of swaddled infants adorn the façade of the Ospedale degli Innocenti, built by Brunelleschi, and rank among the most recognisable images of Florentine sculpture. He also supplied large terracotta altarpieces for the Franciscan sanctuary of La Verna and for numerous Tuscan churches and palaces, heading a thriving family workshop.

Andrea della Robbia welcomed and trained his five sons in his workshop, ensuring the family tradition continued into the next generation, particularly through his son Giovanni. He died on 4 August 1525 in Florence, aged eighty-nine.
