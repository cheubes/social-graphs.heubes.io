Born in 1404 in Genoa, the illegitimate son of an exiled Florentine family, he studied law in Bologna before entering the service of the Roman Curia as an apostolic abbreviator.

A humanist theorist, he wrote the treatise De pictura, dedicating its Italian version, Della pittura, to his friend Filippo Brunelleschi, and codified linear perspective for an entire generation of painters. As an architect, he designed the façade of the Palazzo Rucellai and that of Santa Maria Novella in Florence, as well as the Tempio Malatestiano in Rimini for Sigismondo Malatesta and the church of Sant'Andrea in Mantua for the Gonzaga family.

He died in Rome in 1472, leaving a body of theoretical work that shaped architects and painters for generations after him.
