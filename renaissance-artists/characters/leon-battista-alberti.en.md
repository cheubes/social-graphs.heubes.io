Born on 14 February 1404 in Genoa, the illegitimate son of the wealthy exiled Florentine merchant Lorenzo di Benedetto Alberti and Bianca Fieschi, he attended a boarding school in Padua and then studied law in Bologna, before living in Florence, where his family was allowed to return from exile in 1428, and then settling in Rome in 1431. There he took holy orders and entered the service of the Roman Curia as an apostolic abbreviator, becoming architectural advisor to Pope Nicholas V for the Vatican projects in 1447.

A humanist theorist, he began the treatise De pictura in 1435, dedicating its Italian version, Della pittura, to his friend Filippo Brunelleschi, and codified linear perspective for an entire generation of painters, before turning from 1438, encouraged by Leonello d'Este, to architecture, designing a triumphal arch in Ferrara. He completed the treatise De re aedificatoria in 1452, published in 1485, and secured in 1446 his first major architectural commission, the façade of the Palazzo Rucellai in Florence.

As an architect, he designed from 1450 the Tempio Malatestiano in Rimini for Sigismondo Malatesta, between 1456 and 1470 the upper façade of Santa Maria Novella in Florence, and from 1471 the church of Sant'Andrea in Mantua for the Gonzaga family, regarded as his most accomplished work, alongside work on San Sebastiano in Mantua and the urban redesign of Pienza. Never married and a priest throughout his life, he died in Rome on 25 April 1472, aged sixty-eight, leaving a body of theoretical work that shaped architects and painters for generations after him.
