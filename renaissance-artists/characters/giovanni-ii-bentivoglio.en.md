Born in 1443, he took control of Bologna in 1463 and ruled the city as de facto lord for more than forty years, never holding formal title to it, as the city officially remained under papal sovereignty.

His court welcomed Francesco Francia as its official painter, and its active patronage made Bologna a recognised artistic centre in Emilia.

His drive for independence from the papacy ultimately collided with Pope Julius II, who drove him out of the city in 1506 to bring it under direct papal rule. He died in exile in Milan in 1508, two years after that fall from power.
