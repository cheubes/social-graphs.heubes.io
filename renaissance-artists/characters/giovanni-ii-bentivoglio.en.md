Born on 12 February 1443 in Bologna, the son of Annibale I Bentivoglio, head of the city's magistracy, murdered by his rival Battista Canneschi in June 1445 while Giovanni was still a small child, he grew up under the de facto regency of a cousin, Sante I Bentivoglio, before claiming lordship of Bologna on the latter's death in 1463. He married Ginevra Sforza, widow of his predecessor, on 2 May 1464, consolidating his position, and that same year Pope Paul II granted him the privilege of being considered perpetual head of the city's Senate.

He ruled the city as de facto lord for more than forty years, never holding formal title to it, as the city officially remained under papal sovereignty, while also occasionally serving as a condottiero, notably from 1477 during the siege of Faenza for the Sforza, and ruthlessly suppressing internal threats, such as the execution or exile of the Malvezzi family in 1488 and the Marescotti in 1501. His court welcomed Francesco Francia as its official painter, who executed with Lorenzo Costa and Amico Aspertini a cycle devoted to Saint Cecilia; he also began construction of the Palazzo Bentivoglio in 1498.

His drive for independence from the papacy ultimately collided with Pope Julius II, who excommunicated him and placed Bologna under interdict on 7 October 1506; as papal and French troops advanced, Giovanni and his family fled the city, which Julius II entered in triumph on 10 November, an attempted reconquest by his sons failing in 1507. He died on 15 February 1508, imprisoned by Louis XII of France in Milan's Castello Sforzesco, leaving eleven of the sixteen children he had with Ginevra who survived to adulthood.
