Born in 1431 in Xàtiva, in the Kingdom of Valencia, Rodrigo Borgia owed his rise to his uncle, Pope Callixtus III, who made him a cardinal as early as 1456. Elected pope in 1492 after a conclave tainted by accusations of simony, Alexander VI had his Vatican apartment decorated by Pinturicchio in a lavish style that matched his own, and advanced the political fortunes of his children, among them Cesare and Lucrezia Borgia. His pontificate met with the constant opposition of Cardinal della Rovere, the future Julius II, who succeeded him after his death. Alexander VI died in Rome in 1503, leaving the papacy with a reputation long tarnished by the conduct of his court.
