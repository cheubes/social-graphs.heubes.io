Born in Rome, he entered Raphael's workshop at a young age and became his chief assistant, likely contributing to some of the frescoes in the Villa Farnesina and the Vatican Stanze. On his master's death in 1520, he inherited the Roman workshop and helped complete several of Raphael's unfinished commissions, including The Transfiguration.

Summoned to Mantua in 1524 by Marquess Federico II Gonzaga, he became the court's painter and architect, designing for him the Palazzo del Te, whose Sala dei Giganti frescoes rank among the high points of early Mannerism.

He died in Mantua in 1546, having turned the city into one of northern Italy's leading artistic centres.
