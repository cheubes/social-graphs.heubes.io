Born Giulio Pippi around 1499 in Rome, he entered Raphael's workshop at a young age and became an important member of the team working on the Vatican frescoes and the Stanze, painting notably figures in the Fire in the Borgo, and likely contributing to some of the frescoes in the Villa Farnesina. His nickname "Giulio Romano" came from his geographical origin, the only member of the workshop not native to Rome.

On his master's death in 1520, he took a leading role in completing the Vatican commissions, designing the Constantine frescoes and finishing the Coronation of the Virgin and The Transfiguration left unfinished by Raphael, while also decorating the Villa Madama for Cardinal Giulio de' Medici. Courted by Federico II Gonzaga from 1522, he agreed in late 1524 to move to Mantua, where he became the court's painter and architect, earning more than a thousand ducats a year.

There he designed for Federico II the Palazzo del Te, built roughly between 1525 and 1535, whose Sala dei Giganti frescoes rank among the high points of early Mannerism, while also helping rebuild the ducal palace and the cathedral. He died in Mantua on 1 November 1546, aged forty-six or forty-seven, having turned the city into one of northern Italy's leading artistic centres.
