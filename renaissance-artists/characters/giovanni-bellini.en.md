Born around 1430 in Venice, raised in the household of the painter Jacopo Bellini, whose exact family relationship to him remains disputed among historians, elder brother of Gentile Bellini and brother-in-law of Andrea Mantegna, he began in the family workshop by following the latter's compositions, before developing a light and colour sense of his own under the influence of the oil technique introduced to Venice around 1473 by Antonello da Messina.

He received his first commission in 1470, alongside Gentile, for the Scuola di San Marco, then was appointed conservator of paintings at the Doge's Palace in 1479-1480, a post carrying an annual pension of eighty ducats, before being officially named painter to the Venetian Republic in 1483. He painted major altarpieces, such as the one for San Giobbe or the San Zaccaria altarpiece of 1505, using oil paint to achieve new atmospheric effects; Dürer still praised him in 1506 as the best painter in the city.

Giorgione and Titian trained in his workshop, later competing with him for commissions in the 1510s even as he still painted The Feast of the Gods around 1514, while Vittore Carpaccio worked in his orbit. He died in Venice on 29 December 1516, still active, and was buried at the Basilica di San Giovanni e Paolo, having shaped an entire generation of Venetian painters.
