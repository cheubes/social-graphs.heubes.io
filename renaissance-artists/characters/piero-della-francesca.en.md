Born Piero di Benedetto de' Franceschi around 1415 in Sansepolcro, Tuscany, he lost his father Benedetto before his birth and took the name by which he is known, della Francesca, from his mother, Romana di Perino da Monterchi, who supported his education in mathematics and art; he likely apprenticed around 1432-1438 under the local painter Antonio di Giovanni d'Anghiari. He trained in Florence as an assistant to Domenico Veneziano, absorbing his sense of colour, encountering there Donatello, Brunelleschi and Fra Angelico and drawing inspiration from Masaccio's frescoes, before developing a mathematical approach to perspective that he set out in several treatises, including On Perspective in Painting, completed between the mid-1470s and the 1480s, and a treatise on the five regular solids in 1485.

Documented in 1439 for fresco work at the church of Sant'Egidio in Florence, he returned to Sansepolcro in 1442, joining the City Council, before receiving the commission for the Madonna della Misericordia altarpiece in 1445 and completing The Baptism of Christ in 1450. He executed the fresco of St Sigismund in the Tempio Malatestiano in Rimini in 1451, then painted between 1452 and 1464 the History of the True Cross fresco cycle in the Basilica of San Francesco in Arezzo, regarded as his masterpiece.

Active at the Urbino court under Duke Federico da Montefeltro from the 1450s to the 1470s, he painted there the double portrait diptych of Federico and his wife, Battista Sforza, which fixed the duke's famous profile for posterity, as well as the Montefeltro Altarpiece, or Brera Madonna; working for a time at that court, he passed his geometric rigour on to pupils such as Luca Signorelli. According to Vasari he went blind in his final years, made his will in 1487, and died in Sansepolcro on 12 October 1492, aged seventy-six or seventy-seven, leaving his possessions to his family and the Church.
