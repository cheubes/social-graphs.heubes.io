A native of Sansepolcro, in Tuscany, he trained in Florence as an assistant to Domenico Veneziano, absorbing his sense of colour before developing a mathematical approach to perspective that he set out in several treatises. His major works include the True Cross fresco cycle in Arezzo and the double portrait diptych of Federico da Montefeltro and his wife, Battista Sforza, which fixed the duke's famous profile for posterity. Working for a time at the Urbino court, he passed his geometric rigour on to pupils such as Luca Signorelli. According to Vasari he went blind in his final years, and he died in Sansepolcro in 1492.
