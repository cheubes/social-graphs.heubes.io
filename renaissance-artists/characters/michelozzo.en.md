Born around 1396 in Florence, the son of the tailor of French origin Bartolomeo di Gherardo Borgognone and Antonia, he had three brothers, Leonardo, Zanobi and Giovanni. Trained as a sculptor, he began in 1410 as a die-engraver for the Florentine mint, learning casting, chasing and gilding, joined the guild of stone and wood masters in the early 1420s, and trained from 1420 under Lorenzo Ghiberti, working on the Baptistery's North Door between 1417 and 1423-1424, before declaring himself, in tax records of 1427, Ghiberti's partner.

He then partnered with Donatello for over a decade in a shared workshop, assisting him on the sacristy of Santa Trinita and the tabernacle of St Louis of Toulouse, before the two of them erected in 1428 an open-air pulpit for Prato Cathedral. He accompanied Cosimo de' Medici into exile in Venice in 1433-1434, then became, on their return and for nearly forty years, the family's house architect.

He built Cosimo's Florentine palace, now the Palazzo Medici Riccardi, from 1444, chosen over Brunelleschi by Cosimo himself, and remodelled the convent of San Marco for him from 1437-1438, fitting its library with an elegant colonnaded hall, besides designing the villas at Careggi, Cafaggiolo and Fiesole. Married in January or February 1441, at forty-five, to Francesca di Piero Galligari, then twenty, with whom he had seven children, he died in Florence on 7 October 1472, aged seventy-five or seventy-six, and was buried at the Monastery of San Marco, leaving a lasting architectural mark on the city.
