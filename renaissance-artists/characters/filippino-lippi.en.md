Probably born in 1457 in Prato, the illegitimate son of the painter Fra Filippo Lippi and Lucrezia Buti, a former nun, whose marriage, arranged by Lorenzo de' Medici after papal dispensation, was, according to Vasari, never actually celebrated, he was introduced to painting at an early age by his father and assisted him in Prato and then at Spoleto Cathedral. He lost his father in 1469, aged just twelve, and completed the unfinished frescoes of the Life of the Virgin at Spoleto before finishing his training in Botticelli's workshop, becoming his sole recorded assistant by 1472.

He made his name completing, between 1483 and 1484, the frescoes Masaccio had left unfinished decades earlier in Florence's Brancacci Chapel, before developing a personal style marked by great narrative freedom and rich ornamentation, visible notably in his Apparition of the Virgin to St Bernard (1485-1487). His reputation brought him commissions as far as Rome, where he painted the Carafa Chapel in Santa Maria sopra Minerva between 1488 and 1493 for Cardinal Oliviero Carafa, before returning to Florence, where he worked from 1487 on the Strozzi Chapel, completed in 1503.

He died in Florence on 18 April 1504, aged forty-seven, leaving unfinished a Deposition; all the workshops of the city closed on the day of his funeral as a mark of tribute, after a career devoted entirely to Florentine painting at the close of the Quattrocento.
