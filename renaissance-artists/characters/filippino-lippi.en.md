The son of the painter Fra Filippo Lippi, who introduced him to painting at an early age, he lost his father while still a child and completed his training in Botticelli's workshop. He made his name completing the frescoes Masaccio had left unfinished decades earlier in Florence's Brancacci Chapel, before developing a personal style marked by great narrative freedom and rich ornamentation. His reputation brought him commissions as far as Rome, where he painted the Carafa Chapel in Santa Maria sopra Minerva for Cardinal Oliviero Carafa. He died in Florence in 1504, after a career devoted entirely to Florentine painting at the close of the Quattrocento.
