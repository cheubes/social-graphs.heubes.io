Born in 1433 near Figline Valdarno, he came to Cosimo de' Medici's attention as a child, and Cosimo entrusted him with translating the whole of Plato's work into Latin. Ordained a priest in 1473, he led the Platonic Academy the Medici established at their villa in Careggi, where he was joined by Angelo Poliziano and the young Pico della Mirandola, whom he introduced to Neoplatonic thought. His Christianized reading of Plato also fed into contemporary painting, notably inspiring Sandro Botticelli's mythological compositions. He died at Careggi in 1499, leaving a body of work that shaped Florentine humanism for generations.
