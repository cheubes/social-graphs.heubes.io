Born on 19 October 1433 in Figline Valdarno, the son of Diotifeci d'Agnolo, a physician in the service of Cosimo de' Medici, he came to Cosimo's attention as a child, and Cosimo took him into his household and became his patron for life. He studied Greek under John Argyropoulos, who lectured in Florence from 1459 onward, and became versed in classical texts.

In 1462, Cosimo supplied him with Greek manuscripts of Plato's works and entrusted him with translating the entire corpus into Latin, a first draft of which he completed between 1468 and 1469 before its publication in 1484, the same year as his treatise De amore. Ordained a priest in 1473, he led the Platonic Academy the Medici established at their villa in Careggi, where he was joined by Angelo Poliziano and the young Pico della Mirandola, whom he introduced to Neoplatonic thought.

He completed in 1474 his treatise on the immortality of the soul, the Theologia Platonica, as well as De Christiana Religione, then published in 1489 De vita libri tres, a medical and astrological work that led to an accusation of heresy before Pope Innocent VIII, of which he was ultimately acquitted. His Christianized reading of Plato also fed into contemporary painting, notably inspiring Sandro Botticelli's mythological compositions. He died at Careggi on 1 October 1499, aged sixty-five, leaving a body of work that shaped Florentine humanism for generations; a commemorative bust by Andrea Ferrucci was placed in Florence Cathedral in 1521.
