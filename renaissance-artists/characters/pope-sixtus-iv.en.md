Born Francesco della Rovere on 21 July 1414 in Celle Ligure, near Savona, the son of Leonardo Beltramo di Savona della Rovere and Luchina Monteleoni, of modest social origins according to some historians, he entered the Franciscan order at nine and studied philosophy and theology at the Universities of Bologna and then Padua, where he obtained his licentiate on 19 January 1434 and his doctorate in 1444, before lecturing at several Italian universities. Appointed assistant to the Franciscan minister general in 1446, personal confessor to Cardinal Bessarion in 1459, he was elected minister general of his order at fifty in 1464, then made cardinal by Pope Paul II in 1467, with the titular church of San Pietro in Vincoli.

Elected pope on 9 August 1471 and consecrated on the 25th of that month, he took the name Sixtus, unused since the fifth century. A builder pope, he had the chapel that bears his name constructed and gathered a team of Tuscan and Umbrian painters to decorate it, among them Botticelli, Ghirlandaio and Perugino, while considerably expanding the Vatican Library, restoring more than thirty churches, and having the Ponte Sisto built.

He also raised his nephew Giuliano della Rovere, the future Julius II, to the cardinalate, among thirty-four appointments including six nephews, and sealed a close alliance with Federico da Montefeltro, whom he made Duke of Urbino in 1474; in 1478, he established the Spanish Inquisition by papal bull under pressure from Ferdinand of Aragon, and was personally implicated in the Pazzi conspiracy against the Medici. Sixtus IV fell ill on 8 August 1484 and died on the 12th, in Rome, aged seventy, leaving the papacy the Sistine Chapel and a legacy of nepotism that would long mark the Curia.
