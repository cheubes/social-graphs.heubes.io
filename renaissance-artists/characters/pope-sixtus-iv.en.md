Born in 1414 near Savona, in Liguria, Francesco della Rovere entered the Franciscan order before rising rapidly through the Church hierarchy to his election as pope in 1471. A builder pope, he had the chapel that bears his name constructed and gathered a team of Tuscan and Umbrian painters to decorate it, among them Botticelli, Ghirlandaio and Perugino. He also raised his nephew Giuliano della Rovere, the future Julius II, to the cardinalate, and sealed a close alliance with Federico da Montefeltro, whom he made Duke of Urbino in 1474. Sixtus IV died in Rome in 1484, leaving the papacy the Sistine Chapel and a legacy of nepotism that would long mark the Curia.
