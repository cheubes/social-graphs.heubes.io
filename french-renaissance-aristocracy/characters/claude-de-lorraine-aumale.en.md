Born on 18 August 1526 in Joinville, the third son of Antoinette of Bourbon and brother of François de Guise and the Cardinal of Lorraine, he grew up in the shadow of his elder brothers within a family that dominated the kingdom's Catholic politics, and married Louise de Brézé, Diane de Poitiers's daughter, on 1 August 1547, tightening the ties between the Guise family and the king's favourite.

Entering military service in 1544 under the Duke of Lorraine, he took part in the Siege of Boulogne that same year, the Piedmont campaign in 1551, and fighting against imperial forces in 1552, where he was captured and held until 1555. Becoming Duke of Aumale on his father's death in 1550, he was named Grand Huntsman of France under Henry II and governor of Burgundy, and fought alongside his brother François in the recapture of Calais on 8 January 1558.

After his brother François's assassination in 1563, he emerged as one of the leaders of the Catholic party and played a decisive role in the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre, before commanding troops during the second and third Wars of Religion. Engaged in the siege of Protestant La Rochelle alongside the Duke of Anjou, he died there on 3 March 1573, aged forty-six, like several of his brothers claimed by the fighting of this generation of Guise; he left eleven children, including Charles I, Duke of Aumale.
