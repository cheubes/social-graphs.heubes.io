The third son of Antoinette de Bourbon and brother of François de Guise and the Cardinal of Lorraine, he grew up in the shadow of his elder brothers within a family that dominated the kingdom's Catholic politics.

Grand Huntsman of France, he fought alongside his brother François in the recapture of Calais in 1558, then took part in the early Wars of Religion as a Catholic military commander.

Engaged in the siege of Protestant La Rochelle, he died there in 1573, like several of his brothers claimed by the fighting of this generation of Guise.
