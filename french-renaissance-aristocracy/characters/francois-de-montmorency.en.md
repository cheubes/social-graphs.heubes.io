Born on 17 July 1530 in Chantilly, the eldest son of Anne de Montmorency and Madeleine de Savoie, he sees his first military action at the Siege of Lenz, in Piedmont, in 1551, then takes part in the German campaigns and the Siege of Metz in 1552 under Henry II. Captured on 30 May 1553 while defending Thérouanne against the forces of Charles V, he remains a prisoner for three years.

Released, he receives the Order of Saint Michael and becomes Governor of Paris and the Île-de-France in 1556, then Marshal of France in 1559, before undertaking several diplomatic missions to England. Marshal of France and Governor of Paris, he pursued, unlike many members of the high Catholic nobility, a policy of religious moderation and civil tolerance toward the Protestants during the Wars of Religion, which earned him growing unpopularity in Paris for strictly enforcing royal edicts.

Close to the moderate Politiques faction that his brother Henri de Montmorency-Damville also joined, he was for a time suspected of complicity with the Malcontents conspiracy and imprisoned in the Bastille from 1574 to 1575 on Catherine de' Medici's orders. Released after the king acknowledged his innocence, he died on 6 May 1579 at the Château d'Écouen, aged forty-eight, without ever fully regaining the influence he had held before his disgrace.
