Born Charles-Maximilien on 27 June 1550 at the royal château of Saint-Germain-en-Laye, the third son of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici, he came to the throne at age ten upon the death of his brother Francis II, under his mother's regency until he came of age, proclaimed in August 1563; he was crowned at Reims Cathedral on 5 May 1561. During his grand tour of France (1564-1566), he had the Edict of Roussillon adopted, fixing 1 January as the start of the year.

Married to Elisabeth of Austria, daughter of Emperor Maximilian II, on 27 November 1570, by whom he had only one daughter, who died in infancy, he also fathered an illegitimate son with his mistress Marie Touchet. He ruled through the early Wars of Religion, torn between Michel de l'Hôpital's counsel of conciliation and pressure from the Catholic faction.

In August 1572, under his mother's influence, he authorised the killing of the Protestant leaders gathered in Paris for Henry of Navarre's wedding, triggering the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre on 24 August, which caused thousands of deaths. Wasted by tuberculosis, he died on 30 May 1574 at the Château de Vincennes of pleurisy, at twenty-three, after a thirteen-year reign, leaving the throne to his brother Henry III.
