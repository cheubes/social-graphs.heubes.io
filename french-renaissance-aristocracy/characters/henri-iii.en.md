Born on 19 September 1551 at the château de Fontainebleau, the fourth son of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici, he is not initially destined for the throne and receives a humanist education from Jacques Amyot and François de Carnavalet. Catherine de' Medici's favourite son, he distinguished himself as a military commander at just eighteen, winning the battles of Jarnac and Moncontour against the Protestants in 1569, which earns him the title of lieutenant-general of the kingdom.

Elected King of Poland-Lithuania in May 1573, at twenty-one, he is crowned on 21 February 1574 but secretly flees Kraków that June on hearing of his brother Charles IX's death to claim the French throne, where he is proclaimed king on 30 May 1574 and crowned at Reims Cathedral on 13 February 1575. Married to Louise of Lorraine on 15 February 1575, he ruled a kingdom torn between the Catholic League and the Protestants, facing four successive Wars of Religion, the Edict of Beaulieu attempting in 1576 to ease tensions, and had Henri de Guise and the Cardinal of Lorraine assassinated at the Château de Blois in December 1588 to reassert his authority, triggering a massive uprising.

Allied with Henri de Navarre against the League, he is stabbed on 1 August 1589 at Saint-Cloud by the Dominican friar Jacques Clément and dies the next day of peritonitis from his abdominal wound, the last king of the Valois dynasty.
