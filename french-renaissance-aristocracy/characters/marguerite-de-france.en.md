Born on 14 May 1553 in Saint-Germain-en-Laye, the seventh child of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici, sister to three kings of France, she receives a refined education at the royal court. She married Henri de Navarre on 18 August 1572, a match meant to reconcile Catholics and Protestants, but whose "crimson wedding" became, just six days later, the pretext for the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre.

Kept apart from her husband through the Wars of Religion, she takes part in the Malcontents conspiracy and shifts political allegiance several times, running at Nérac, from 1579 to 1582, a genuine literary academy that welcomes major poets. Having sided with the League, she is forced into exile in 1585 and remains a prisoner at the Château d'Usson in Auvergne from 1586 to 1605, nineteen years of isolation during which she writes her memoirs. She became Queen of France in 1589 when Henri de Navarre acceded as Henry IV, and consents on 17 December 1599 to the annulment of their childless marriage in exchange for substantial financial compensation.

Returning to Paris in 1605, she creates a prestigious literary salon in her Left Bank mansion, surrounding herself with writers and philosophers and becoming a major figure of Parisian cultural life, all while remaining on good terms with her former husband. She dies on 27 March 1615 in Paris, aged sixty-one, and is buried at the Basilica of Saint-Denis; her memoirs, published after her death, rank among the earliest such works by a Frenchwoman.
