Born on 17 February 1519 in Bar-le-Duc, the son of Claude of Lorraine, first Duke of Guise, and Antoinette of Bourbon, he is nicknamed "le Balafré" (the Scarred) for a serious facial wound received at the Siege of Boulogne in 1544.

As governor of Metz, he successfully defended the city from October 1552 to January 1553 against an imperial siege of fifty-five thousand men led by Charles V, then recaptured Calais from the English in January 1558, moving French artillery across the frozen marshes surrounding the city to catch the enemy by surprise.

Uncle of Mary, Queen of Scots through his sister Marie de Guise, he dominated the kingdom's government during the reign of Francis II alongside his brother, the Cardinal of Lorraine, notably suppressing the Amboise conspiracy in 1560. Second Duke of Guise and father of Henri de Guise and Charles de Mayenne, he commanded the Catholic armies at the outset of the Wars of Religion and won the Battle of Dreux in 1562, taking Condé prisoner. He was shot by the Protestant Jean de Poltrot de Méré in an ambush at the Siege of Orléans on 18 February 1563 and died of his wounds on 24 February at Saint-Hilaire-Saint-Mesmin, a killing the Guise family attributed to the instigation of Gaspard de Coligny.
