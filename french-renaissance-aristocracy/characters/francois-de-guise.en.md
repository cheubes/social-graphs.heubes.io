Nicknamed "le Balafré" (the Scarred) for a wound received at the Siege of Boulogne, he distinguished himself by successfully defending Metz against Charles V in 1552 and recapturing Calais from the English in 1558.

Uncle of Mary, Queen of Scots through his sister Marie de Guise, he dominated the kingdom's government during the reign of Francis II alongside his brother, the Cardinal of Lorraine.

Second Duke of Guise and father of Henri de Guise and Charles de Mayenne, he commanded the Catholic armies at the outset of the Wars of Religion and won the Battle of Dreux in 1562, taking Condé prisoner. He was assassinated in 1563 at the Siege of Orléans by the Protestant Poltrot de Méré, a killing the Guise family attributed to the instigation of Gaspard de Coligny.
