Born on 12 November 1547 at the Château de Fontainebleau, the second daughter of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici, she grows up at court alongside her sister Elisabeth and Mary, Queen of Scots, and married Charles III of Lorraine in 1559 at the age of eleven, becoming Duchess of Lorraine and Bar and strengthening ties between the French crown and the Duchy of Lorraine.

Described by contemporaries as notably discreet and retiring, unlike several of her siblings, she led a quiet court life in Nancy while keeping ties to the French court, where she witnessed major events such as the Amboise conspiracy of 1560 and the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre of 1572.

The mother of nine children, seven of whom reached adulthood, including the future Duke Henry II of Lorraine, she died in childbirth on 21 February 1575 in Nancy, at the age of twenty-seven; her mother Catherine de' Medici subsequently raised her daughter Christine.
