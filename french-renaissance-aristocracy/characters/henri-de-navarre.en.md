Raised partly in the Protestant faith by his mother Jeanne d'Albret, he became King of Navarre on her death in 1572, the same year his marriage to Marguerite de France served as the pretext for the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre, which he survived by abjuring under duress.

Leader of the Protestants after fleeing the court in 1576, he became heir presumptive to the French throne in 1584 on the death of Francis, Duke of Anjou, precipitating the formation of the Catholic League and the War of the Three Henrys. Victorious at Ivry in 1590, he laid siege to Paris before converting to Catholicism in 1593, a move tradition credits with the remark "Paris is well worth a Mass."

As King of France under the name Henry IV, he ended the Wars of Religion with the Edict of Nantes in 1598, before being assassinated by Ravaillac in 1610.
