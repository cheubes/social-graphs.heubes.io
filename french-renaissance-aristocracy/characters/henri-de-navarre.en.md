Born on 13 December 1553 at the château de Pau, in Béarn, tradition holds that he received a "Béarnaise baptism" from his grandfather Henri d'Albret, who rubbed garlic on his lips and had him breathe in Jurançon wine; his Catholic baptism took place on 6 March 1554, with Henry II of France and Henry II of Navarre as godfathers. Raised partly in the Protestant faith by his mother Jeanne d'Albret, who imposed a strict Calvinist education on him, he grew up in the Béarnaise countryside at Coarraze, mixing with peasants during hunting trips. Brought to the French court by his father in 1561, he received military training under Admiral Coligny and fought for the first time at the Battle of Arnay-le-Duc in 1570.

He became King of Navarre on his mother's death in 1572, the same year his marriage to Marguerite de France served as the pretext for the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre, which he survived by abjuring under duress. Leader of the Protestants after fleeing the court in 1576, he became heir presumptive to the French throne in 1584 on the death of Francis, Duke of Anjou, precipitating the formation of the Catholic League and the War of the Three Henrys. Victorious at Ivry in 1590, he laid siege to Paris before converting to Catholicism in 1593, a move tradition credits with the remark "Paris is well worth a Mass."

As King of France under the name Henry IV, he ended the Wars of Religion with the Edict of Nantes in 1598, before being assassinated on 14 May 1610 by the Catholic fanatic Ravaillac, who stabbed him as his carriage stood stopped on rue de la Ferronnerie in Paris.
