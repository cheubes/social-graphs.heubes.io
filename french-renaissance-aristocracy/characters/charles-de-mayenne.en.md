The second son of François de Guise and younger brother of Henri de Guise, he took command of the Catholic League and proclaimed himself Lieutenant-General of the Kingdom after his brother's assassination at Blois in December 1588. He fought Henry IV in the field, who defeated him successively at Arques in 1589 and Ivry in 1590, yet without ever fully subduing him. After several years of resistance, he negotiated his surrender in 1595-1596, securing governorships and compensation in exchange for submission, before withdrawing from political life until his death in 1611.
