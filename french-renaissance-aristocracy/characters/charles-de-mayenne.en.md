Born on 26 March 1554 in Alençon, the second son of François I de Guise and Anne d'Este, he leaves at eighteen to fight the Ottomans in the ranks of the Holy League, arriving too late to take part in the Battle of Lepanto; he later becomes governor of Burgundy and then Grand Chamberlain of France in 1573.

He took command of the Catholic League and proclaimed himself Lieutenant-General of the State and Crown of France after his brothers' assassination at Blois in December 1588, an unprecedented title that lets him set up a rival government to the monarchy in Paris.

He fought Henry IV in the field, who defeated him successively at Arques in 1589 and Ivry in 1590, yet without ever fully subduing him; after losing Burgundy at the Battle of Fontaine-Française in 1595, he submitted to the king that November, an agreement formalised by the Edict of Folembray in January 1596. He then withdrew from political life and died on 3 October 1611 in Soissons, aged fifty-seven.
