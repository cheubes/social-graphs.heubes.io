Born Hercule on 18 March 1555 at Fontainebleau, the youngest of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici's children, he takes the name François at his confirmation in 1565. His face permanently scarred by smallpox contracted in childhood, he nursed a lasting rivalry with his elder brother Henry III.

As Duke of Alençon and later Duke of Anjou, he became, after the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre of 1572, the leader of the Malcontents faction, a hub of opposition at court; after a dramatic escape from the Louvre in 1575, he negotiated favourable terms that won him the duchies of Anjou and Touraine. From 1572 he courted Queen Elizabeth I of England, without any marriage ever coming of it, the negotiations finally collapsing in 1582.

Becoming protector of the liberties of the Netherlands against Spain that same year, he was crowned Duke of Brabant in February 1582, but his rule there proved disastrous, culminating in a failed attack on Antwerp in January 1583. He died on 10 June 1584 at Château-Thierry, of tuberculosis and malaria, aged twenty-nine. His death, leaving Henry III without a direct heir, opened the dynastic path to Henri de Navarre and precipitated the formation of the Catholic League.
