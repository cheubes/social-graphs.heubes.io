The youngest of Catherine de' Medici's children, his face scarred by smallpox, he nursed a lasting rivalry with his elder brother Henry III.

As Duke of Alençon and later Duke of Anjou, he led the Malcontents faction against the king during the Wars of Religion, before spending several fruitless years courting Queen Elizabeth I of England.

He later intervened in the Netherlands against Spain, where he was briefly proclaimed Duke of Brabant, before dying of illness at Château-Thierry in 1584. His death, leaving Henry III without a direct heir, opened the dynastic path to Henri de Navarre and precipitated the formation of the Catholic League.
