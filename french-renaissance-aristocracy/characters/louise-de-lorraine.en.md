Born on 30 April 1553 at the château de Nomény, descended from a cadet branch of the House of Lorraine, that of Vaudémont, a first cousin of Duke Charles III of Lorraine, she was chosen by Henry III, reportedly out of genuine affection rather than political calculation: the king sent emissaries to ask for her hand in January 1575, a proposal she at first doubted was genuine. The wedding took place on 15 February 1575 at Reims Cathedral, and she became Queen of France.

Also titular Queen of Poland, she remained deeply attached to her husband despite his infidelities; their marriage, marked by several miscarriages from May 1575 and numerous pilgrimages and spa treatments through 1586, remained childless, which drew tension at court.

After Henry III's assassination by Jacques Clément on 1 August 1589, she adopted the white mourning of queens and wore it for the rest of her life, earning the nickname "the White Queen," and retired in piety, first spending eleven years at the Château de Chenonceau, received as her dower, and then at the convent she founded at Moulins, where she died on 29 January 1601. Her remains were transferred in 1817 to the Basilica of Saint-Denis, where she remains the only pre-Revolutionary queen to actually rest there.
