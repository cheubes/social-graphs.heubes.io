Born on 29 December 1552 in La Ferté-sous-Jouarre, the son of Louis I of Condé, he is raised in a Calvinist family and became Prince of Condé at seventeen after his father's execution following the Battle of Jarnac in 1569, immediately taking command of the Protestant armies alongside Gaspard de Coligny.

Militant Protestant convictions set him apart from his cousin Henri de Navarre; he fights at the Siege of La Rochelle in 1573 and becomes governor of Picardy. Cousin and companion in arms of Henri de Navarre, he jointly commanded the Huguenot forces with him at the victory of Coutras in 1587.

He died suddenly on 5 March 1588 in Saint-Jean-d'Angély, aged thirty-five, a death shrouded in suspicions of poisoning, voiced notably by physicians and by Henri de Navarre himself, that briefly implicated his second wife, Charlotte-Catherine de La Trémoïlle, who was arrested and interrogated before finally being acquitted in 1596. She gave birth after his death, in September 1588, to a son, the future Henry II of Condé.
