The son of Louis I of Condé, he became Prince of Condé at seventeen after his father's execution following the Battle of Jarnac in 1569, immediately taking command of the Protestant armies alongside Gaspard de Coligny. Cousin and companion in arms of Henri de Navarre, he jointly commanded the Huguenot forces with him at the victory of Coutras in 1587. He died suddenly the following year at Saint-Jean-d'Angély, a death shrouded in suspicions of poisoning that briefly implicated his pregnant wife, who gave birth after his death to a son, the future Henry II of Condé.
