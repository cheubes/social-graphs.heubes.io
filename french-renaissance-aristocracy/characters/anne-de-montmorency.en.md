A childhood companion of Francis I, he built a distinguished military career under that king and later Henry II, who made him Constable of France in 1538, the kingdom's highest military office. A long-standing rival of the Guise family for influence over Henry II, he nonetheless allied with them in 1561 to form the Catholic triumvirate opposed to the Protestants. Taken prisoner at the Battle of Dreux in 1562, like his adversary Condé that same day, he died five years later from wounds received at the Battle of Saint-Denis, at seventy-four, one of the most powerful figures of the French Renaissance.
