Born on 15 March 1493 in Chantilly, the son of Guillaume de Montmorency and Anne Pot, he is the godson of Queen Anne of Brittany, who gives him his first name. A childhood companion of Francis I, he distinguishes himself at the Battle of Marignano in 1515 and becomes Marshal of France in 1522.

Taken prisoner alongside the king at the Battle of Pavia in 1525, he helps negotiate the Treaty of Madrid in 1526, the year he is named Grand Master of France; he becomes Constable of France on 10 February 1538, the kingdom's highest military office, before falling from favour in 1541 after diplomatic setbacks. He regains his full influence when Henry II comes to the throne in 1547.

A long-standing rival of the Guise family for influence over Henry II, he nonetheless allied with them in 1561 to form the Catholic triumvirate opposed to the Protestants. Taken prisoner at the Battle of Dreux in 1562, like his adversary Condé that same day, he dies on 10 November 1567 from wounds received at the Battle of Saint-Denis, for refusing to surrender while surrounded, at seventy-four. A Renaissance patron, he has the châteaux of Chantilly and Écouen built and protects artists such as Bernard Palissy, leaving the image of one of the most powerful figures of the French Renaissance.
