The second son of Francis I, he spent part of his childhood as a hostage in Spain after his father's defeat at Pavia, before becoming King of France in 1547.

He continued the Italian Wars until the Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis in 1559, which ended the conflict and sealed the peace through the marriage of his daughter Elisabeth to Philip II of Spain. Married to Catherine de' Medici but devoted for decades to his favourite, Diane de Poitiers, he also worked to suppress the spread of Protestantism in the kingdom.

He died ten days after being struck in the eye by a lance splinter during a tournament held to celebrate the peace, Gabriel de Montgomery unable to pull his blow in time.
