Born on 16 November 1528 at the château de Saint-Germain-en-Laye, the only child of Henry II, King of Navarre, and Marguerite d'Angoulême, sister of Francis I, she receives an exceptional humanist education under Nicolas Bourbon. Forced into marriage at twelve to William of Cleves in 1541, a union she resisted with unwavering vigour, she obtains its annulment from Pope Paul III in October 1545 after the duke's diplomatic reversal. She then marries Antoine de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme and First Prince of the Blood, on 20 October 1548, by whom she has five children, only two of whom reach adulthood, the future Henry IV and Catherine de Bourbon.

She inherited the throne of Navarre on her father's death on 25 May 1555, at first ruling jointly with Antoine, then openly converted to Calvinism around August 1560, becoming France's highest-ranking Protestant, and imposed Calvinism by ordinance in July 1561 as the official religion in her lands of Béarn.

Becoming sole regent after Antoine's death in 1562, she took refuge in La Rochelle during the third religious conflict in 1568, where she administered the Huguenot movement, managing finances, fortifications and international diplomacy, and negotiated the Peace of Saint-Germain-en-Laye in August 1570. It was she who negotiated her son's marriage to Marguerite de France, but she died suddenly in Paris on 9 June 1572, months before that wedding and the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre that followed it; an autopsy concluded natural causes, despite rumours of poisoning that would circulate for centuries.
