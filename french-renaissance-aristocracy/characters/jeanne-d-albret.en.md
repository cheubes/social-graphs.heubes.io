The daughter of Marguerite de Navarre and niece of Francis I, she inherited the throne of Navarre in 1555 and openly converted to Calvinism in 1560, which she imposed as the official religion in her lands of Béarn.

Queen of Navarre and mother of Henri de Navarre, she became, after her husband's death, the principal political and financial leader of the Huguenot movement, notably supporting the cause of Louis I of Condé.

It was she who negotiated her son's marriage to Marguerite de France, but she died suddenly in Paris in 1572, months before that wedding and the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre that followed it.
