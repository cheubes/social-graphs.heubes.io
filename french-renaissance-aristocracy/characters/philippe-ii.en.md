Born on 21 May 1527 in Valladolid, the son of Charles V and Isabella of Portugal, he receives a careful education from eminent tutors, including the mathematician Pedro Sanchez Ciruelo and the humanist Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda, and masters Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French and Latin. At sixteen, in 1543, he becomes regent of Spain in his father's absence, assisted by a council of regency, the same year he marries, in a first union, Infanta Maria Manuela of Portugal, who dies two years later giving birth to Don Carlos.

The son of Charles V, he inherited a vast empire spanning Spain, the Netherlands, and territories in Italy and the New World after his father's gradual abdication, becoming King of Spain on 16 January 1556, and cast himself as the champion of Catholic Counter-Reformation in Europe, promoting the reforms of the Council of Trent.

A former King consort of England through his 1554 marriage to Mary Tudor, until her death in 1558, he married Elisabeth of Valois as his third wife in 1559, sealing the Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis. In the 1580s and 1590s, he actively backed, both financially and militarily, the French Catholic League against the accession of the Protestant Henri de Navarre to the throne. His intervention ultimately failed after Navarre's conversion to Catholicism, and the Peace of Vervins ended the Franco-Spanish conflict the very year of his death: he died on 13 September 1598 at the Escorial palace, aged seventy-one, after a turbulent reign of more than forty years.
