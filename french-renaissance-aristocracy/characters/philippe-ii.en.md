The son of Charles V, he inherited a vast empire spanning Spain, the Netherlands, and territories in Italy and the New World, and cast himself as the champion of Catholic Counter-Reformation in Europe.

A former King consort of England through his marriage to Mary Tudor, he married Elisabeth of Valois as his third wife in 1559, sealing the Treaty of Cateau-Cambrésis.

In the 1580s and 1590s, he actively backed, both financially and militarily, the French Catholic League against the accession of the Protestant Henri de Navarre to the throne. His intervention ultimately failed after Navarre's conversion to Catholicism, and the Peace of Vervins ended the Franco-Spanish conflict the very year of his death, in 1598.
