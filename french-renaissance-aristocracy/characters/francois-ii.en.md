Born on 19 January 1544 at the Château de Fontainebleau, the eldest son of Catherine de' Medici and Henry II, frail since childhood, he nonetheless receives a careful education entrusted to several tutors. Betrothed at four to Mary, Queen of Scots, he marries her on 24 April 1558 at Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris.

He comes to the throne of France on 10 July 1559, at fifteen, after his father's accidental death; nominally of age, he quickly delegates power to his young wife's uncles, François de Guise and the Cardinal of Lorraine, whose dominance provokes the Amboise conspiracy in March 1560, a failed attempt to overthrow the Guise family, while Protestant unrest breaks out in several provinces.

His reign, dominated by the House of Guise, lasted less than eighteen months and ended with his premature death on 5 December 1560, at sixteen, probably from mastoiditis or an ear infection, leaving the throne to his younger brother Charles IX.
