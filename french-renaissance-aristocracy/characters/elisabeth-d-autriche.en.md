Born on 5 July 1554 in Vienna, the daughter of Emperor Maximilian II and Maria of Austria, she is educated under the charge of Ogier Ghislain de Busbecq and speaks German, Spanish, Latin and Italian, but not French. A Habsburg archduchess, she marries Charles IX by proxy on 22 October 1570 in Speyer, and then formally on 26 November at Mézières, at fifteen, he being nineteen, a match that strengthens ties between France and Austria in the midst of the Wars of Religion; her state entry into Paris that following 29 March is a grand affair.

Crowned queen at Saint-Denis on 25 March 1571, she bears a single daughter, Marie-Elisabeth of France, on 27 October 1572, who dies in infancy. Queen of France, noted for her gentleness and piety, she remains largely apart from political affairs, confining herself to her role as sovereign and royal wife without ever favouring any faction.

After Charles IX's death in 1574, she declines several remarriage proposals and returns to Vienna in December 1575, aged twenty, where she founds a convent of Poor Clares and never sets aside mourning for her husband, devoting herself to religious life until her death on 22 January 1592, aged thirty-seven; she is buried at St Stephen's Cathedral in Vienna.
