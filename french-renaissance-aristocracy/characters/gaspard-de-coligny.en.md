A nephew of Anne de Montmorency, he converted to Protestantism in the late 1550s and became, alongside Louis I of Condé, the principal military and political leader of the Huguenots.

Admiral of France, he gained the ear of the young Charles IX in the early 1570s, pushing him toward war with Spain in the Netherlands, a prospect that alarmed Catherine de' Medici and the Guise family.

The target of a first assassination attempt on 22 August 1572, he was finished off two days later, on the 24th, on the orders of Henri de Guise, triggering the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre.
