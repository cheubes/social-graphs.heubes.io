Born on 16 February 1519 in Châtillon-sur-Loing, the son of Marshal Gaspard I de Coligny and Louise de Montmorency, a nephew of Anne de Montmorency, he receives a humanist education and joins the court of Francis I as early as 1530, where he studies alongside the royal children. From 1542, he takes part in the wars against Charles V in Italy, Flanders and Luxembourg; colonel general of the French infantry in 1547, the year he marries Charlotte de Laval, he is named Admiral of France in 1552 and becomes governor of Picardy.

Moderate at first, he gradually converted to Protestantism in the 1560s, particularly during his retreats at Châtillon-sur-Loing, a shift that accelerates after the Edict of January 1562. He then becomes, alongside Louis I of Condé, the principal military and political leader of the Huguenots, fighting at Dreux, Saint-Denis, Jarnac in 1569, where Condé is killed, and then Moncontour that same year, before winning the Battle of Arnay-le-Duc in 1570 and negotiating the Peace of Saint-Germain-en-Laye.

Admiral of France, he gained the ear of the young Charles IX in the early 1570s, pushing him toward war with Spain in the Netherlands, a prospect that alarmed Catherine de' Medici and the Guise family. The target of a first assassination attempt on 22 August 1572, wounded in the arm and hand by a gunman posted in a house belonging to the Guise family, he was finished off two days later, in the night of 23-24 August, at his home on rue de Béthisy, by the Bohemian captain Charles Danowitz on the orders of Henri de Guise, his body thrown out the window, dragged through the streets and displayed at the Montfaucon gibbet, triggering the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre.
