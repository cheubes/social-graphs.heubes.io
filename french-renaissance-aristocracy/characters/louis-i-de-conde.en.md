The younger brother of Antoine de Bourbon, he was implicated in the 1560 Amboise conspiracy against Guise influence, a plot that nearly cost him his life before he was saved by the death of Francis II. Founder of the Bourbon-Condé branch, he became, alongside Gaspard de Coligny, the principal military and political leader of the Protestants, fighting notably at the Battle of Dreux in 1562, where he was taken prisoner by François de Guise. Taking up arms again in the third War of Religion, he was wounded and captured at the Battle of Jarnac in 1569, then shot dead after his surrender.
