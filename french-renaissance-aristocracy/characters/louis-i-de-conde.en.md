Born on 7 May 1530 at the château de Vendôme, the youngest surviving son of Charles IV de Bourbon and Françoise d'Alençon, the younger brother of Antoine de Bourbon and uncle of the future Henry IV, he distinguishes himself under Marshal de Brissac in Piedmont and then takes part in the conquest of the Three Bishoprics and the defence of Metz in 1552, before receiving the Order of Saint Michael in 1553.

Secretly converted to Protestantism as early as the summer of 1558, under the influence of his wife Éléonore de Roye, he became the natural leader of the Huguenot movement. He was implicated in the 1560 Amboise conspiracy against Guise influence, a plot that nearly cost him his life before he was saved by the death of Francis II.

Founder of the Bourbon-Condé branch, he became, alongside Gaspard de Coligny, the principal military and political leader of the Protestants, losing the Battle of Dreux in 1562, where he was taken prisoner by François de Guise, before being freed by the Peace of Amboise in 1563. He led the Surprise of Meaux in 1567 and then the Battle of Saint-Denis during the second War of Religion, before falling back on La Rochelle in 1568. Taking up arms again in the third War of Religion, he was wounded and captured at the Battle of Jarnac on 13 March 1569; as he tried to surrender, he was shot dead by Joseph-François de Montesquiou.
