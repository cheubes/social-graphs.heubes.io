Born on 25 December 1494 in Ham, Picardy, descended from the House of Bourbon-Vendôme through her father François de Bourbon-Vendôme and her mother Marie de Luxembourg-Saint-Pol, whose line traced back to Saint Louis through Robert de Clermont, she married Claude of Lorraine, first Duke of Guise, in Paris on 9 June 1513.

She bore about a dozen children, including Marie de Guise, future queen regent of Scotland, François de Guise, second Duke of Guise, and two cardinals, Charles and Louis de Lorraine, seeing several of them rise to the highest offices of the kingdom. The first Duchess of Guise, she passed her taste for ambition on to her children and oversaw her family's interests and marriage alliances with lasting authority; her granddaughter Mary, Queen of Scots, born of Marie de Guise, became Queen of Scotland and briefly Queen of France.

Widowed in 1550, she retired to the château of Joinville, where she lived another thirty-three years, watching over her family's Catholic interests throughout the Wars of Religion. The matriarch of her house, she died on 22 January 1583 in Joinville, at eighty-eight, having witnessed both the rise and the first setbacks of the House of Guise.
