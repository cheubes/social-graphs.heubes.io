Born on 31 December 1550 in Joinville, the son of François de Guise, he receives, after his father's assassination in 1563, an education entrusted to his uncle the Cardinal of Lorraine, rounded out by military training in Hungary against the Ottomans; that same year he becomes Grand Master of France and Duke of Guise.

He avenged his father's assassination by leading, on 24 August 1572, the troops that killed Admiral Coligny, whom he held responsible for his father's death, before hunting down fleeing Protestant leaders on the Left Bank of Paris. Wounded in the face at the Battle of Dormans in October 1575, he inherited his father's nickname "le Balafré" and founded the Catholic League that same year, becoming its undisputed leader and a popular hero in Paris.

From 1584, he made himself the champion of the Catholic League against Henri de Navarre's claims to the throne, signing the Treaty of Joinville with King Philip II of Spain, which secured him financial support. In May 1588, during the Day of the Barricades, he forced Henry III to flee his own capital, nursing ambitions that reached toward the throne itself. Leader of the Catholic League and a central figure of the French Wars of Religion, he embodied the opposition to Henry III until his execution on the king's orders on 23 December 1588 at the Château de Blois, where eight guards attacked him as he entered the royal chambers; his body was burned with quicklime and his ashes scattered in the Loire.
