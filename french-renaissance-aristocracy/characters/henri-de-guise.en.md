The son of François de Guise, he avenged his father's assassination by having Gaspard de Coligny killed during the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre in 1572.

Wounded in the face at the Battle of Dormans in 1575, he inherited his father's nickname "le Balafré" and founded the Catholic League that same year, becoming its undisputed leader and a popular hero in Paris.

In May 1588, during the Day of the Barricades, he forced Henry III to flee his own capital, nursing ambitions that reached toward the throne itself. Leader of the Catholic League and a central figure of the French Wars of Religion, he embodied the opposition to Henry III until his assassination on the king's orders at the Château de Blois in December 1588.
