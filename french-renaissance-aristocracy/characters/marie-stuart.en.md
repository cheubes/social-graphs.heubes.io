Born on 8 December 1542 at Linlithgow Palace, Scotland, the daughter of King James V and Marie de Guise, she was made Queen of Scots at six days old on her father's death from cholera; legend has it that, on hearing he had a daughter, he remarked, "It came with a lass, it will go with a lass." James Hamilton, Earl of Arran, becomes regent while Cardinal David Beaton leads the Catholic faction opposed to English influence; the Treaty of Greenwich of 1 July 1543 betroths her for a time to Prince Edward of England, an alliance broken off when Arran reconciles with Beaton.

A Franco-Scottish treaty of 7 July 1548 promises her marriage to the French Dauphin, Francis; she sails for France in August 1548 and lands at Carrières-sur-Seine on 16 October. Sent to the French court at the age of five, she grew up under the guardianship of her grandmother Antoinette de Bourbon and her Guise uncles, receiving instruction in languages, rhetoric and poetry from noted tutors, including Ronsard. She married Francis on 24 April 1558 and became Queen of France in July 1559 when he came to the throne, only to be widowed in December 1560 when her young husband died.

Back in Scotland from August 1561 to rule, as a Catholic, a kingdom divided by the Reformation, she married Henry Stuart, Lord Darnley, in 1565, by whom she had a son, the future James VI of Scotland and I of England. After Darnley's murder in 1567, her scandalous marriage to James Hepburn, Earl of Bothwell, forced her to abdicate and flee to England to seek the protection of her cousin Elizabeth I. Regarded as a threat to the English throne, she was imprisoned there for nineteen years before being implicated in the Babington Plot against Elizabeth and executed by beheading on 8 February 1587 at Fotheringhay Castle.
