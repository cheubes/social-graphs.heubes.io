Born on 17 February 1524 in Joinville, the younger son of Claude of Lorraine, first Duke of Guise, and Antoinette of Bourbon, he is named Archbishop of Reims as early as 1538, at just thirteen, and made a cardinal in 1547 by Pope Paul III; he also serves as Bishop of Metz from 1550 to 1551 and holds numerous abbatial commendations.

Under the reign of François II (1559-1560), he and his brother François wield considerable power over the kingdom's finances and administration. He becomes one of the chief architects of the kingdom's Catholic policy, taking part in the Colloquy of Poissy in 1561 and then representing France at the Council of Trent in 1563, where he champions the Catholic Reformation.

A celebrated orator and patron of letters, he embodied, in opposition to Michel de l'Hôpital, the hardline stance toward the Protestants, and secured the latter's fall from favour in 1568. Head of the House of Guise and of the Catholic party after his brother's assassination in 1563, he continues to oppose Catherine de' Medici's policy of tolerance, but his own influence wanes in his final years. He dies on 26 December 1574 in Avignon of a lung infection, at fifty, months before Charles IX, and is buried in Reims Cathedral.
