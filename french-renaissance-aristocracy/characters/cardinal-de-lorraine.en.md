The younger brother of François de Guise, Archbishop of Reims from his teenage years and later a cardinal, he became one of the chief architects of the kingdom's Catholic policy and represented France at the Council of Trent. A celebrated orator and patron of letters, he embodied, in opposition to Michel de l'Hôpital, the hardline stance toward the Protestants, and secured the latter's fall from favour in 1568. Head of the House of Guise after his brother's assassination in 1563, his own influence nonetheless waned in his final years, and he died in 1574, months before Charles IX.
