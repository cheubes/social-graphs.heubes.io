A Baptist deacon and the youngest person ever registered to vote in his county, he took part on February 18, 1965, in a night march in Marion, Alabama, held in support of a jailed activist. State troopers violently charged the demonstrators; trying to protect his mother and grandfather, Jackson was shot by a trooper inside a nearby café.

He died of his wounds eight days later. His death pushed James Bevel and the SCLC to conceive the Selma to Montgomery march, originally meant to carry his coffin to Governor Wallace's doorstep; the idea grew into the historic march that helped speed passage of the Voting Rights Act.
