Born on 27 April 1927 in Marion, Alabama, she grows up on her parents' farm and studies at Antioch College in Ohio, earning her degree in 1951, before training in voice and violin at the New England Conservatory of Music in Boston, graduating in 1954.

She marries Martin Luther King on 18 June 1953 in her parents' garden and becomes involved in the movement early on, notably during the Montgomery bus boycott; the couple has four children, Yolanda, Martin Luther III, Dexter and Bernice. Far from confined to the role of a leader's wife, she speaks at numerous rallies herself, undertakes advocacy trips to Ghana in 1957 and India in 1959, and weaves feminist and pacifist concerns into her activism, notably against the Vietnam War and nuclear weapons.

After her husband's assassination on 4 April 1968, she leads the campaign for a federal holiday in his honor, achieved in 1986, and founds the King Center for Nonviolent Social Change in Atlanta to carry on his legacy, while campaigning for women's and LGBT rights. She dies on 31 January 2006 in Rosarito, Mexico, of ovarian cancer, aged seventy-eight.
