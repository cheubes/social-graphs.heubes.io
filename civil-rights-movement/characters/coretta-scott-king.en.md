A trained musician, she married Martin Luther King in 1953 and became involved in the movement early on, notably during the Montgomery bus boycott. Far from confined to the role of a leader's wife, she spoke at numerous rallies herself and wove feminist and pacifist concerns into her activism.

After her husband's assassination in 1968, she led the campaign for a federal holiday in his honor, achieved in 1986, and founded the King Center for Nonviolent Social Change in Atlanta to carry on his legacy.
