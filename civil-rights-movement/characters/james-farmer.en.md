Co-founder in 1942 of the Congress of Racial Equality (CORE), one of the movement's oldest organizations devoted to nonviolent civil disobedience, he became its national director in 1961. Under his leadership CORE organized the Freedom Rides that year, interracial bus journeys meant to test enforcement of a Supreme Court ruling banning segregation in interstate travel.

The buses were attacked and burned in Alabama, but the campaign, later taken up by the SNCC, forced the federal government to enforce desegregation. He is counted among the "Big Six" who organized the 1963 March on Washington, though he was in jail in Louisiana at the time of the event.
