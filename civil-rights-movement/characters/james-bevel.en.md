Born on 19 October 1936 in Itta Bena, Mississippi, he grows up on a cotton plantation, serves in the US Navy, then studies from 1957 to 1961 at the American Baptist Theological Seminary in Nashville alongside John Lewis, where he discovers Tolstoy's writing and the Gandhian nonviolence taught by his mentor James Lawson.

He became one of the SCLC's boldest strategists. It was his idea, controversial even within the movement, to involve children and high-schoolers in the 1963 Birmingham campaign, which magnified the media impact of the police violence. He also conceived the 1965 Selma to Montgomery march in response to the killing of activist Jimmie Lee Jackson, then the 1966 Chicago campaign, and married SNCC activist Diane Nash, with whom he drafts the Alabama Voting Rights Project proposal in 1963.

His later life is marred by an incest charge involving his own daughter, for which he is convicted in April 2008 and sentenced to fifteen years in prison and a fifty-thousand-dollar fine. Released on appeal after seven months in custody, he dies of pancreatic cancer on 19 December 2008, aged seventy-two.
