A journalist and then a NAACP administrator, he became its executive secretary in 1955 and led the organization for more than two decades, favoring institutional lobbying and litigation over mass direct action.

Wary of the more radical methods championed by the SNCC and the Nation of Islam, he nonetheless helped organize the 1963 March on Washington alongside the movement's other major figures, while keeping his distance from the Black Power turn that followed.
