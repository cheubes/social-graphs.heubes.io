Born Malcolm Little on 19 May 1925 in Omaha, Nebraska, the son of Reverend Earl Little, a Baptist preacher influenced by Marcus Garvey's Pan-Africanist movement, he loses his father in 1931, killed under suspicious circumstances by a streetcar, while his mother, institutionalized, sinks into depression. A bright student at a mostly white school, he abandons his dream of becoming a lawyer after a teacher tells him it is no realistic goal for a Black man, using a racial slur, then drifts in Boston into drug dealing, gambling and burglary.

Arrested in 1946 and sentenced to ten years in prison, of which he serves seven, he discovers reading and, corresponding with his brother, begins taking an interest in the Nation of Islam. Converted to Islam in prison, he is released on parole in 1952, adopts the surname "X" to reject his "slave name" and rises rapidly through the organization's ranks, becoming minister of the Harlem temple as early as 1954 and then the Nation of Islam's most charismatic minister and public spokesman, preaching Black separatism and the right to armed self-defense, in open break with Martin Luther King's nonviolent, integrationist strategy. His national prominence, gained after the 1959 television documentary The Hate That Hate Produced, helps grow the Nation of Islam's membership from roughly five hundred to thirty thousand by 1963.

After a pilgrimage to Mecca in April 1964, where he encounters Muslims of every background, a shock that shifts his views toward a more universalist message, he breaks that same month with Elijah Muhammad and founds Muslim Mosque Inc. and then the Organization of Afro-American Unity. He is assassinated on 21 February 1965 at the Audubon Ballroom in Manhattan, as he is about to speak, by Nation of Islam members, at a time when his relationship with the organization had deeply deteriorated; three of them are convicted in 1966, but two are exonerated in 2021, authorities acknowledging that crucial evidence had been withheld by the FBI and the NYPD.
