Converted to Islam in prison, he became the Nation of Islam's most charismatic minister and public spokesman, preaching Black separatism and the right to armed self-defense, in open break with Martin Luther King's nonviolent, integrationist strategy.

After a pilgrimage to Mecca that shifted his views toward a more universalist message, he broke with Elijah Muhammad in 1964 and founded the Organization of Afro-American Unity. He was assassinated in February 1965 in Harlem by Nation of Islam members, at a time when his relationship with the organization had deeply deteriorated.
