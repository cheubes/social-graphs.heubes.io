Born on 30 July 1924 in Boonville, Missouri, he grows up in Macomb, Illinois, where his family settles when he is six, and takes part in his first sit-in as early as 1947, successfully integrating a cafeteria in Peoria. He moves to Nashville in 1959 to study ministry at American Baptist College, where he meets James Lawson, who teaches Gandhian nonviolent civil disobedience there.

A minister and early activist, he helps found the Nashville Christian Leadership Conference and takes part in Nashville's first sit-ins in 1960, then the 1961 Freedom Rides, before joining the SCLC leadership as director of national affiliates and direct action. Known for his eloquence, he trains many activists in nonviolent technique.

During the 1965 Selma campaign, an image of him being struck in the face by Sheriff Jim Clark on the courthouse steps, while trying to accompany Black residents seeking to register to vote, is seen across the country and helps speed passage of the Voting Rights Act. He dies on 17 July 2020 in Atlanta, aged ninety-five.
