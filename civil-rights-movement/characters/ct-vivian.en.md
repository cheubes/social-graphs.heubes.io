A minister and early activist, he took part in the Nashville sit-ins and then the 1961 Freedom Rides before joining the SCLC leadership as director of national affiliates and direct action. Known for his eloquence, he trained many activists in nonviolent technique.

During the 1965 Selma campaign, an image of him being struck in the face by Sheriff Jim Clark on the courthouse steps, while trying to accompany Black residents seeking to register to vote, was seen across the country.
