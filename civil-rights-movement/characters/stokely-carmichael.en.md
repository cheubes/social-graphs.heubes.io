A Caribbean-American student, he joined the SNCC and took part in Freedom Summer in 1964, then in founding the Lowndes County Freedom Organization, whose black panther emblem later gave its name to a more famous organization. Elected SNCC chairman in 1966 in place of John Lewis, he marked a turn toward a more separatist message.

It was during the "Meredith Against Fear" march in June 1966 that he publicly launched the slogan "Black Power," breaking with the strictly integrationist, nonviolent rhetoric Martin Luther King had championed until then. He later took the name Kwame Ture.
