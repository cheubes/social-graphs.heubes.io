A former teacher, he became the SNCC's executive secretary in 1961, a key administrative post he held through the organization's most intense years. It was he who built the logistics of Freedom Summer in 1964, the Mississippi voter registration campaign that cost three activists their lives.

Older and more experienced than most SNCC students, he was also one of its sharpest critics of the SCLC's more moderate strategy, before later joining more radical movements.
