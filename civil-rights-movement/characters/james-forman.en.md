Born on 4 October 1928 in Chicago, he serves in the US Air Force until 1952, including during the Korean War, before earning a degree from Roosevelt University in 1957.

A former teacher, he became the SNCC's executive secretary in 1961, a key administrative post he held through the organization's most intense years. It was he who built the logistics of Freedom Summer in 1964, the Mississippi voter registration campaign that cost three activists their lives. Older and more experienced than most SNCC students, he was also one of its sharpest critics of the SCLC's more moderate strategy, before later joining more radical movements, including the Black Panther Party and the International Black Workers Congress.

Married three times, to Mary Sears (1950-1956), Mildred Thompson (1959-1965) and then Constancia Romilly from 1965, with whom he has two children, he goes on to a career as a journalist, teacher and writer, notably authoring The Making of Black Revolutionaries. He dies on 10 January 2005 in Washington, aged seventy-six.
