Born on 5 January 1926 in Attapulgus, Georgia, he serves in the US Army during the Second World War, receiving the Purple Heart. A decorated veteran, he joined the SCLC and became one of its most determined field organizers, notably in Georgia, one of Martin Luther King's principal lieutenants charged with nonviolent mobilization.

On 7 March 1965, in Martin Luther King's absence, he led the march of some six hundred demonstrators with John Lewis that attempted to cross the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma. That day, remembered as "Bloody Sunday," saw the marchers violently attacked by state troopers and white supremacists, injuring nearly seventy people, images that helped speed passage of the Voting Rights Act a few months later.

He goes on to a political career, serving in the Georgia State Senate, and remains politically active until his death. He dies on 16 November 2000 in Atlanta, aged seventy-four; his funeral is held at Ebenezer Baptist Church, where Martin Luther King had preached.
