A decorated World War II veteran, he joined the SCLC and became one of its most determined field organizers, notably in Georgia. On March 7, 1965, in Martin Luther King's absence, he led the march with John Lewis that attempted to cross the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma.

That day, remembered as "Bloody Sunday," saw the marchers violently attacked by state troopers on the edge of town, images that helped speed passage of the Voting Rights Act a few months later.
