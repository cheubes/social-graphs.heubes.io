Born on 25 June 1933 in Kosciusko, Mississippi, he serves nine years in the US Air Force (1951-1960), including a posting in Japan, before applying to the University of Mississippi in 1961 with NAACP backing.

His admission, ordered by federal courts despite Governor Ross Barnett's initial refusal, triggered riots on the Oxford campus in 1962 that left two dead and forced President Kennedy to send thousands of federal troops to protect him and enforce the court order; he begins classes on 1 October 1962 and completes his degree on 18 August 1963, under constant military protection.

In 1966, he undertook a solo march across Mississippi to encourage Black voter registration and denounce the persistence of fear; shot on 6 June by white gunman Aubrey James Norvell on the second day, he survives his wounds and is visited in hospital by Martin Luther King, while the march is taken up by the movement's major organizations, including Stokely Carmichael's SNCC. He publishes an account of his university experience, Three Years in Mississippi, in 1966, before later joining the Republican Party and taking controversial positions, including opposing affirmative action and, in 1991, backing former Ku Klux Klan leader David Duke's campaign for governor of Louisiana.
