Founder and leader of the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters, the first major Black labor union in the United States, he secured from President Roosevelt as early as 1941 a ban on racial discrimination in the defense industry, by threatening to organize a march on Washington.

A respected elder statesman of the movement in the 1960s, he was the original initiator of the 1963 March on Washington, entrusting its logistical organization to his protégé Bayard Rustin, and delivered one of the opening speeches at the gathering before the Lincoln Memorial.
