Born on 25 July 1941 in Chicago, he is fourteen when he is accused of whistling at Carolyn Bryant, a white woman, in a Mississippi grocery store while staying with an uncle in August 1955; she claims at the time that he made improper advances toward her, before recanting in 2017.

Abducted from his uncle's home by Roy Bryant and J.W. Milam, he is tortured, shot, and his body dumped in the Tallahatchie River, weighted down with a cotton gin fan tied on with barbed wire. A jury of twelve white men acquits the two killers after just sixty-seven minutes of deliberation, on 23 September 1955; they confess to the crime the following year in Look magazine, shielded by double jeopardy from further prosecution.

His murder, and above all his mother's decision to display his mutilated body in an open casket, sent shockwaves across the nation widely credited as one of the sparks of the civil rights movement. Congress names its 2022 federal anti-lynching law the Emmett Till Antilynching Act in his memory.
