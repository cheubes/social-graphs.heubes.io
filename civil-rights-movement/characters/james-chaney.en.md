Born James Earl Chaney on 30 May 1943 in Meridian, Mississippi, the second of five children of Ben, a plasterer, and Fannie Lee Chaney, he attends Catholic school through ninth grade and becomes an early supporter of the civil rights cause, to the point of being suspended from school for wearing an NAACP badge. After leaving Harris Junior College, he works as an apprentice plasterer alongside his father.

He begins volunteering in late 1963 and joins CORE in Meridian, where he helps organize voter education classes, introduces CORE staff to local church leaders and helps them get around the surrounding counties. He helps organize Freedom Summer in 1964, the Black voter registration campaign jointly run by CORE and the SNCC under the COFO umbrella.

On 21 June 1964, he traveled with Michael Schwerner and Andrew Goodman to investigate the burning of a Black church in Neshoba County. Arrested and then released at night by a deputy sheriff, the three men were intercepted and killed by Ku Klux Klan members; their bodies were not found until six weeks later. The three activists are posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2014.
