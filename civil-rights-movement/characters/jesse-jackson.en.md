A young seminary student, he joined the SCLC in the mid-1960s and headed its Chicago branch of Operation Breadbasket, a program that used economic boycotts to secure the hiring of Black workers by local businesses. He grew close to Martin Luther King, whom he accompanied on several campaigns.

He was at the Lorraine Motel in Memphis at the moment of King's assassination in April 1968, an episode central to his personal legend, before going on to a long career as a civil rights leader and later a Democratic presidential candidate.
