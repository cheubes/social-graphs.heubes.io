Born on 8 October 1941 in Greenville, South Carolina, to a sixteen-year-old mother and a married biological father who worked in a cotton mill, he is raised by Charles Henry Jackson, a postal worker who gives him his surname. He attends Sterling High School in Greenville and then the University of Illinois on a football scholarship, before transferring to North Carolina A&T State University, where he earns a sociology degree in 1964.

A young seminary student, he joined the SCLC in the mid-1960s under Martin Luther King's influence and headed its Chicago branch of Operation Breadbasket, a program that used economic boycotts to secure the hiring of Black workers by local businesses. Ordained a Baptist minister in June 1968, he was at the Lorraine Motel in Memphis at the moment of King's assassination on 4 April 1968, an episode central to his personal legend.

He founds Operation PUSH in 1971 to carry on this fight, then leads a long career as a civil rights leader, marked by two runs for the Democratic presidential nomination, in 1984 (third place, 18.09% of the vote) and 1988 (second place, 29.12% of the vote). Diagnosed with Parkinson's disease in November 2017, he dies on 17 February 2026 in Chicago, aged eighty-four.
