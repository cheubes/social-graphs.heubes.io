Elected Governor of Alabama in 1962, he promised in his inaugural address "segregation now, segregation tomorrow, segregation forever." The following year, he symbolically stood in the doorway of the University of Alabama in a failed attempt to block the enrollment of two Black students.

A national figure of segregationism, he ran for president several times on a platform opposing integration, before declaring himself repentant in his final years and seeking forgiveness from the Black leaders he had fought.
