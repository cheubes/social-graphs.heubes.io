A mathematics teacher trained at Harvard, he joined the SNCC in 1961 to organize Black voter registration in rural Mississippi, one of the movement's most dangerous fronts. Quiet and thoughtful, he became a respected moral figure within the organization.

He was one of the chief architects of Freedom Summer in 1964, which brought hundreds of volunteers, often white and from the North, to draw national attention to the repression in the state. Marked by the violence of that campaign, he later stepped back from it to devote himself to mathematics education.
