Born Robert Parris Moses on 23 January 1935 in Harlem, New York, he graduates from Stuyvesant High School in 1952 and then, on a scholarship, from Hamilton College in 1956, before earning a master's degree in philosophy at Harvard. He teaches mathematics at New York's Horace Mann School for two years after leaving Harvard in 1958.

He joined the SNCC in 1961 as a field secretary and headed its Mississippi Project to organize Black voter registration in the rural state, one of the movement's most dangerous fronts. Quiet and thoughtful, he became a respected moral figure within the organization, and one of the chief architects of Freedom Summer in 1964, which brought hundreds of volunteers, often white and from the North, to draw national attention to the repression in the state.

Marked by the violence of that campaign, he later stepped back from it to devote himself to mathematics education, founding the Algebra Project with a MacArthur Fellowship received in 1982, a program devoted to improving minority education in math. He dies on 25 July 2021, aged eighty-six.
