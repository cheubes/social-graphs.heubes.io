Born on 20 November 1925 in Brookline, Massachusetts, the seventh child of Joseph Patrick Kennedy and Rose Fitzgerald Kennedy, he serves in the Navy at seventeen before studying at Harvard and then the University of Virginia School of Law. A legal counsel to Senate committees in the 1950s, he investigates organized crime, denouncing the gangsters' grip on certain industries.

Appointed Attorney General at thirty-five when his brother John becomes president in 1961, he plays a crucial role in the Cuban Missile Crisis by secretly negotiating with Soviet ambassador Anatoly Dobrynin, while aggressively pursuing organized-crime figures such as Jimmy Hoffa. He sent federal marshals to protect the Freedom Riders attacked in Alabama in 1961, while also authorizing, under pressure from J. Edgar Hoover, the wiretapping of Martin Luther King in the name of national security.

Resigning after his brother's assassination in 1963, he is elected US Senator from New York in 1964, then declares his candidacy for the Democratic nomination in March 1968, opposing the Vietnam War and building a coalition with minorities and the poor. After winning the California primary on 5 June 1968, he is shot by Sirhan Sirhan at the Ambassador Hotel in Los Angeles and dies the next day, aged forty-two, just two months after King; he is buried at Arlington National Cemetery, near his brother, and is posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2025.
