Born on 14 September 1921 in New Haven, Connecticut, the daughter of McCullough Baker, she earns her degree from New York University in 1943 and then her law degree from Columbia Law School in 1946, where future Supreme Court Justice Thurgood Marshall hires her as a legal assistant in her second year.

An attorney at the NAACP Legal Defense Fund led by Marshall, she helped draft the briefs for Brown v. Board of Education and personally argued numerous school and university integration cases, winning nine victories before the Supreme Court, including James Meredith's admission to the University of Mississippi in 1962. The first African American woman to argue before the Supreme Court, she also serves in the New York State Senate (1964-1965) and as Manhattan Borough President (1965-1966).

She became in 1966 the first Black woman appointed as a federal judge in the United States, a post she holds until her death, awarded the Spingarn Medal in 2003. She dies of heart failure on 28 September 2005 in Manhattan, aged eighty-four.
