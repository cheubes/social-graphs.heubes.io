A longtime pacifist and socialist activist, he introduced Martin Luther King to Gandhian civil disobedience as early as the Montgomery boycott, but his past membership in the Communist Party and his openly gay identity, at a time when this exposed him to persecution, made him a figure some in the movement kept at arm's length.

A gifted organizer, he was nonetheless entrusted in 1963 with the logistics of the March on Washington, which he orchestrated in a matter of weeks into the success of a gathering of more than two hundred thousand people.
