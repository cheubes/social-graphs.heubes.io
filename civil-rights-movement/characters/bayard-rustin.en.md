Born on 17 March 1912 in West Chester, Pennsylvania, raised by Quaker grandparents, he studies at Wilberforce University (1932-1936) and then City College of New York in 1937. He first joins the Young Communist League in 1937, leaving in 1941 when the party abandons civil rights advocacy, before joining the Fellowship of Reconciliation that same year and helping found CORE, working with A. Philip Randolph against discrimination in the military.

Imprisoned from 1944 to 1946 for refusing military conscription in 1943, he travels between 1947 and 1952 to India and Africa to study nonviolent independence movements, then becomes secretary of the War Resisters League in 1950, a post he holds until 1965. A longtime pacifist and socialist activist, he introduces Martin Luther King to Gandhian civil disobedience as early as the Montgomery boycott and helps found the SCLC in 1957; but his past Communist Party membership and his openly gay identity, at a time when this exposed him to persecution, make him a figure some in the movement keep at arm's length.

A gifted organizer, he is nonetheless entrusted in 1963 with the logistics of the 28 August March on Washington, which he orchestrates in a matter of weeks into the success of a gathering of more than two hundred and fifty thousand people. He goes on to chair the A. Philip Randolph Institute from 1965 to 1979 and dies on 24 August 1987 of peritonitis at Manhattan's Lenox Hill Hospital; President Obama posthumously awards him the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2013.
