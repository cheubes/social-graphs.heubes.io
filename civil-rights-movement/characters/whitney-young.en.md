A social worker trained at Harvard and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, he became executive director of the National Urban League in 1961 and steered it toward a more activist role, building ties with major corporations and the federal government to secure the hiring and training of African Americans.

Counted among the "Big Six" who organized the 1963 March on Washington, he embodied a more institutional, negotiated path than civil disobedience, without ever breaking from the movement's other leaders. He drowned accidentally in 1971 during a trip to Lagos, Nigeria.
