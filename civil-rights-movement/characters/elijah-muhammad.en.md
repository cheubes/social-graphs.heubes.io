Born Elijah Robert Poole on 7 October 1897 in Sandersville, Georgia, the son of a Baptist preacher who had worked on cotton plantations, he marries Clara Evans in 1917 and moves to Detroit in 1923, where he works on a Chevrolet assembly line.

He joins the Nation of Islam, founded by Wallace Fard Muhammad, around 1930 and, after Fard's mysterious disappearance in 1934, establishes himself as the organization's leader, relocating to Chicago, where he builds it into a developed religious and economic structure with temples, businesses and schools, preaching racial separatism and Black economic self-reliance rather than integration into an American society he considered irredeemably racist. He proclaims Fard the earthly incarnation of Allah and positions himself as the divinely appointed prophet.

Imprisoned for four years from 1942 for sedition and draft resistance during the Second World War, he trained and promoted Malcolm X, who became the movement's public face, before the latter publicly accused him of fathering children with six secretaries, contradicting the organization's strict moral code; their relationship severely deteriorated from 1963 onward, culminating in Malcolm's break with the organization and assassination in 1965. He led the organization until his death on 25 February 1975 in Chicago, aged seventy-seven; his son Warith Deen Muhammad succeeds him and gradually steers the Nation toward orthodox Sunni Islam, rejecting its racial ideology.
