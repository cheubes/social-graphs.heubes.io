Successor to Wallace Fard Muhammad as leader of the Nation of Islam in 1934, he built it into a structured religious and economic organization, preaching racial separatism and Black economic self-reliance rather than integration into an American society he considered irredeemably racist.

He trained and promoted Malcolm X, who became the movement's public face, before their relationship severely deteriorated from 1963 onward, culminating in Malcolm's break with the organization and assassination in 1965. He led the organization until his death in 1975.
