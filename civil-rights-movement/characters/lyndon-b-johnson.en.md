Born on 27 August 1908 in Stonewall, Texas, the eldest of five children, he earns a teaching degree from Texas State University in 1930 and briefly teaches public speaking before entering politics as secretary to Congressman Richard M. Kleberg. Elected to the House of Representatives in 1937, he serves briefly in the Navy during the Second World War, earning a Silver Star, before being elected to the Senate in 1948 and becoming its majority leader from 1955 to 1961.

A Texas senator who became Kennedy's vice president in 1961 to secure Southern Democratic support, he assumes the presidency after Kennedy's assassination on 22 November 1963 and puts the full weight of his legislative experience behind passage of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, which banned racial discrimination in public accommodations and employment.

After images of the crackdown on the Selma march in 1965, he pushed through the Voting Rights Act, ending discriminatory barriers to Black voter registration in the South, telling Congress he was borrowing the movement's own slogan: "We Shall Overcome." He also builds his Great Society programme (Medicare, Medicaid, anti-poverty measures), but sees his presidency undermined by the escalating Vietnam War and domestic racial unrest; after poor showings in the 1968 Democratic primaries, he declines to seek reelection. He dies of a heart attack on 22 January 1973 in Johnson City, Texas, aged sixty-four.
