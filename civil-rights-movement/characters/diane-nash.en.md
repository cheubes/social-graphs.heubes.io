Born on 15 May 1938 in Chicago, she is raised mainly by her grandmother Carrie Bolton and studies first at Howard University before transferring to Fisk University in Nashville, where she first encounters the city's Jim Crow laws.

A student in Nashville, at twenty-two she led the sit-ins that desegregated the city's lunch counters between February and May 1960, a turning point marked by her public confrontation with Mayor Ben West, who was forced to concede that racial discrimination was unjust. She takes part that same year in founding the SNCC. When the 1961 Freedom Rides were halted by violence in Birmingham, it was she who organized, against the advice of more cautious leaders, the relay of volunteers that allowed the buses to continue on into Mississippi.

She married James Bevel, with whom she drafted the Alabama Voting Rights Project proposal and planned several SCLC campaigns, including the 1965 Selma march. Awarded the Rosa Parks Award in 1965 and the Presidential Medal of Freedom by President Biden in 2022, she is still living in 2026, aged eighty-eight.
