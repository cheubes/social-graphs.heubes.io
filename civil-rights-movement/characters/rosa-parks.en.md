Secretary of the local NAACP branch and trained in civil disobedience at the Highlander Folk School, she refused on December 1, 1955, to give up her seat to a white passenger on a Montgomery bus, a deliberate act rather than simple fatigue, as the popular myth has it.

Her arrest triggered a bus boycott led by Martin Luther King that lasted 381 days and ended with a Supreme Court ruling declaring segregation on public transit unconstitutional. She remained a symbolic figure of the movement for decades afterward.
