After her son Emmett's murder in 1955, she insisted that his mutilated body be displayed in an open casket at his Chicago funeral, so the whole country could see, in her words, "what they did to him." Photographs published by the Black press sparked national outrage.

She spent the rest of her life campaigning for racial justice and preserving her son's memory, testifying at his killers' trial and speaking to the public for decades afterward.
