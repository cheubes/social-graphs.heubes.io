A Methodist minister and conscientious objector imprisoned for refusing conscription during the Korean War, he studied Gandhian civil disobedience in India before settling in Nashville in 1958. There he led nonviolence training workshops that durably shaped the local student movement.

Martin Luther King considered him the movement's foremost theorist of nonviolence. Expelled from Vanderbilt's divinity school in 1960 for supporting the sit-ins, he continued training generations of activists until his death in 2024.
