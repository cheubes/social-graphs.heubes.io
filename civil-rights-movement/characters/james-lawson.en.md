Born James Morris Lawson Jr. on 22 September 1928 in Uniontown, Pennsylvania, he grows up in Massillon, Ohio. A conscientious objector, he spends a year in prison for refusing conscription during the Korean War, then serves three years as a Methodist missionary in Nagpur, India, where he studies Gandhian civil disobedience.

Back in the United States, he studies at Oberlin College and then at Vanderbilt University's divinity school, where Martin Luther King, whom he meets around this time, introduces him to the civil rights movement and encourages him to settle in Nashville in 1958. There he leads nonviolence training workshops that durably shape the local student movement, training Diane Nash, John Lewis and Marion Barry among others, and takes part himself in the Freedom Rides and sit-ins.

Martin Luther King considered him the movement's foremost theorist of nonviolence. Expelled from Vanderbilt's divinity school in 1960 for supporting the sit-ins, he goes on to serve as a Protestant pastor in Memphis and then Los Angeles, devoting himself largely to human rights education, and continues training generations of activists until his death on 9 June 2024 in Los Angeles, aged ninety-five.
