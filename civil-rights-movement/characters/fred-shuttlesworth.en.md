A minister in Birmingham, Alabama, one of the most violently segregationist cities in the South, he founded the Alabama Christian Movement for Human Rights after the NAACP was banned in the state, then co-founded the SCLC with Martin Luther King in 1957. He survived several bombings targeting his home and church without ever abandoning direct action.

It was his insistence that convinced the SCLC to choose Birmingham as the target of its 1963 campaign: images of demonstrators, including many children, attacked with fire hoses and police dogs on the orders of Commissioner Bull Connor turned national opinion in the movement's favor.
