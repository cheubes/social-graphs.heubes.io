Born Freddie Lee Robinson on 18 March 1922 in Mount Meigs, Alabama, he earns a Bachelor of Arts in English literature from the University of Alabama and is ordained a Baptist minister in 1950, leading Birmingham's Bethel Baptist Church from 1953 to 1961.

A minister in one of the most violently segregationist cities in the South, he founded the Alabama Christian Movement for Human Rights after the NAACP was banned in the state, then co-founded the SCLC with Martin Luther King and Ralph Abernathy in 1957. He survived several bombings targeting his home and church without ever abandoning direct action.

It was his insistence that convinced the SCLC to choose Birmingham as the target of its 1963 campaign: images of demonstrators, including many children, attacked with fire hoses and police dogs on the orders of Commissioner Bull Connor turned national opinion in the movement's favor. He goes on to continue his fight against racism in Cincinnati, retires from the ministry in January 2006, and dies on 5 October 2011 in Birmingham, aged eighty-nine, having received the Presidential Citizens Medal in 2001.
