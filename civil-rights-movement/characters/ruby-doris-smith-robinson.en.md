Born on 25 April 1942 in Atlanta, the second of seven children raised in the Black middle-class neighborhood of Summerhill, she is inspired by the Greensboro Woolworth's sit-in and joins the Atlanta Committee on Appeal for Human Rights, taking part in the April 1960 meeting that founds the SNCC.

A student in Atlanta, she takes part in the sit-ins and then, at eighteen, travels in February 1961 to Rock Hill, South Carolina, where she pioneers the "Jail-No-Bail" tactic that leads to thirty-day jail terms for activists. That same year she takes part in the Freedom Rides, during which she was arrested and jailed at Parchman Penitentiary for forty-five days. A formidably effective organizer, she marries Clifford Robinson in 1964 and, despite the birth of a child, continues her professional activism, rising through the SNCC's ranks to become its first executive secretary in 1966, one of the few women to hold such a senior leadership position in the organization.

Diagnosed with leukemia, she resigns her post shortly before dying on 7 October 1967 in Atlanta, at just twenty-five, at a time when she was expected to play a growing role in the SNCC's turn toward Black Power.
