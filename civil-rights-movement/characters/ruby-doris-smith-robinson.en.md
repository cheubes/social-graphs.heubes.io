A student in Atlanta, she took part in the sit-ins and then the 1961 Freedom Rides, during which she was arrested and jailed at Parchman Penitentiary. A formidably effective organizer, she rose through the SNCC's ranks to become its executive secretary in 1966, one of the few women to hold such a senior leadership position in the organization.

She died of leukemia in 1967, at just twenty-five, at a time when she was expected to play a growing role in the SNCC's turn toward Black Power.
