A World War II veteran, he became the NAACP's first field secretary for Mississippi in 1954, one of the most dangerous states in the South for a Black activist. He investigated Emmett Till's murder in 1955 and supported James Meredith's admission to the University of Mississippi in 1962.

He was shot dead outside his Jackson home in June 1963, hours after President Kennedy's televised address on civil rights, by a Ku Klux Klan member who was not convicted until three decades later.
