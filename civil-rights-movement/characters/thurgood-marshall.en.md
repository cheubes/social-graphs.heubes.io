A lawyer trained at Howard University's law school, he led the NAACP Legal Defense Fund and directed the legal battle against school segregation, culminating in the Supreme Court's 1954 ruling in Brown v. Board of Education, which declared the "separate but equal" doctrine unconstitutional.

More committed to litigation strategy than to civil disobedience, he was sometimes critical of Martin Luther King's direct-confrontation methods, which he saw as risking the legitimacy of the movement's legal victories. In 1967 he became the first African American justice appointed to the US Supreme Court.
