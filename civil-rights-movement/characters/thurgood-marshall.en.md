Born on 2 July 1908 in Baltimore, Maryland, he earns his undergraduate degree from Lincoln University in Pennsylvania and then his law degree from Howard University's law school in 1933.

He joins the NAACP in 1936 and becomes its principal legal strategist, arguing numerous cases before the Supreme Court. He leads the NAACP Legal Defense Fund and directs the legal battle against school segregation, culminating in the Supreme Court's 1954 ruling in Brown v. Board of Education, which declared the "separate but equal" doctrine unconstitutional, a step regarded as decisive for the civil rights movement.

More committed to litigation strategy than to civil disobedience, he was sometimes critical of Martin Luther King's direct-confrontation methods, which he saw as risking the legitimacy of the movement's legal victories. Appointed by President Lyndon B. Johnson to the US Supreme Court in 1967, he becomes its first African American justice and serves for twenty-four years, until his retirement in 1991. Awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom that same year, he dies of heart failure on 24 January 1993, aged eighty-four, and is buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
