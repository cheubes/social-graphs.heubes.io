Born on 13 December 1903 in Norfolk, Virginia, she grows up in Littleton, North Carolina, in a devout Baptist family, and graduates from Shaw University in Raleigh in 1927. Settled in New York, she works as a journalist before holding various jobs during the Great Depression, including at the Works Progress Administration until 1938.

She joins the NAACP that year as a field assistant, then becomes director of branches in 1944, the first woman in the post, resigning in 1946 after internal tensions. A seasoned organizer, she creates the SCLC's Atlanta office in 1958 and becomes its first executive director, where her horizontal style of leadership clashes with the organization's more minister-centered way of working.

In 1960, she convinced the students who had just launched the sit-in movement to form their own organization rather than join the SCLC: the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC) was born at a conference she organized at Shaw University. She remained its quiet but influential mentor, advocating collective leadership rather than the cult of a single leader she felt was embodied by Martin Luther King. She dies on 13 December 1986 in New York, on her eighty-third birthday.
