Born Myrlie Louise Beasley on 17 March 1933 in Vicksburg, Mississippi, she is raised by her paternal grandmother and aunt, both schoolteachers who steer her toward education, and graduates from Magnolia High School in 1950. She meets Medgar Evers, a World War II veteran eight years her senior, on her first day at Alcorn A&M College, where she studies education; they marry on Christmas Eve 1951 and settle in Mound Bayou, where their three children are born, Darrell Kenyatta, Reena Denise and James Van Dyke.

A full-fledged NAACP activist alongside her husband, she spent the decades following his 1963 assassination seeking justice, after two early trials ended with all-white juries unable to reach a verdict. Her persistence paid off in 1994, when a third trial finally convicted the killer.

She went on to chair the NAACP, continuing the couple's political commitment, and receives the Spingarn Medal in 1998. She delivers a notable speech at President Obama's second inauguration, on 21 January 2013, and remains, in 2026, alive at ninety-three.
