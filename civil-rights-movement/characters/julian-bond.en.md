Born on 14 January 1940 in Nashville, Tennessee, the son of Horace Mann Bond, the first African American president of Lincoln University, and librarian Julia Agnes Washington, he graduates from the George School, a Quaker private school, in 1957, before studying at Atlanta's Morehouse College.

He took part in founding the SNCC on 17 April 1960 and became its communications director, responsible for relaying the organization's actions to the national press. His media ease made him a natural spokesman for the student movement early on.

Elected to the Georgia House of Representatives on 15 June 1965, he is initially refused his seat in January 1966 because of his support for an SNCC statement against the Vietnam War, before the Supreme Court overturns that decision on 5 December 1966 and he is sworn in on 9 January 1967. He serves four terms in the House (1965-1974) and then six in the Georgia Senate (1975-1987), before chairing the NAACP from 1998 to 2010. He dies on 15 August 2015 in Fort Walton Beach, Florida, aged seventy-five.
