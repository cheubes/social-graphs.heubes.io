Born on 11 March 1926 in Linden, Alabama, the grandson of an enslaved man and the son of a deacon and local leader, he serves in the US Army during the Second World War, reaching the rank of sergeant, before earning a degree from Alabama State University in 1950 and a master's in sociology from Clark Atlanta University in 1951.

A minister in Demopolis and then Montgomery from 1951, he meets Martin Luther King in 1954 and becomes one of the organizers of the bus boycott alongside him after Rosa Parks's arrest in 1955, forging a lasting friendship and political partnership. He co-founded the SCLC with King in 1957 and remained his closest lieutenant throughout the following decade's campaigns.

Upon King's death on 4 April 1968, he succeeded him as SCLC president and carried forward, from that May, the Poor People's Campaign King had launched, though he struggled to keep the organization at the same level of influence in the years that followed; he leaves the SCLC in 1977 to pursue political office. He dies on 17 April 1990 in Atlanta and is buried in Lincoln Cemetery.
