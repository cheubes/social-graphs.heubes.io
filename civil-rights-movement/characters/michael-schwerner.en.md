A New York social worker, he moved with his wife Rita to Mississippi in January 1964 to open a CORE field office in Meridian, where he organized Black voter registration and trained volunteers ahead of Freedom Summer. His visible activism made him a particular target of the local Ku Klux Klan.

On June 21, 1964, while investigating a church burning with James Chaney and Andrew Goodman, the three men were arrested and then handed over at night to Klan members who killed them. The ensuing federal investigation, popularized under the code name "Mississippi Burning," led to the first federal convictions of white supremacists for a crime of this kind.
