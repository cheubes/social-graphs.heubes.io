Born on 12 March 1932 in New Orleans, the son of a dentist and a teacher, he briefly studies at Dillard University before graduating from Howard University, then earns a theology degree from Hartford Seminary in 1955.

A minister and close adviser to Martin Luther King, he joined the SCLC in 1961 and became its executive director, nicknamed King's "right hand" in the movement; he plays a key role in negotiations with local authorities during the Birmingham and Selma campaigns, and is with King at the moment of his assassination in Memphis in 1968.

He goes on to a prominent political career: the first African American congressman from the Deep South since 1897 (1973-1977), the first Black US Ambassador to the United Nations under President Carter (1977-1979), and then mayor of Atlanta (1982-1990). He later co-founds the consulting firm GoodWorks International and remains, in his nineties, an occasional voice on public policy matters.
