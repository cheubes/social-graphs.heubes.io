Born Daisy Lee Gatson on 11 November 1914 in Huttig, Arkansas, she loses her mother, murdered by white men while she is still an infant, and is adopted by Orlee Smith; this founding trauma feeds a lifelong commitment against racial injustice. In 1941 she founds the Arkansas State Press with her husband L.C. Bates, a newspaper devoted to civil rights coverage, and becomes president of the Arkansas NAACP branch in 1952.

She took charge of organizing the integration of Little Rock Central High School in 1957, selecting and personally accompanying the nine Black students who faced hostile crowds and ultimately had to be protected by federal paratroopers sent by President Eisenhower, acting as their spokesperson and offering them shelter at her home during the violent standoff with state authorities.

Her own newspaper, which she ran with her husband, suffered a segregationist advertising boycott in retaliation and had to close a few years later. Awarded the Spingarn Medal in 1958 and a speaker at the 1963 March on Washington, she steps back from activism after a heart attack in 1965 but relaunches her newspaper in 1984. She dies on 4 November 1999 in Little Rock, aged eighty-four.
