Born Theophilus Eugene Connor on 11 July 1897 in Selma, Alabama, he becomes Birmingham's Commissioner of Public Safety for nearly a quarter century (1936-1954, then 1957-1963), with oversight of police, fire services, schools, public health and libraries, and embodied the most brutal enforcement of segregation in a city nicknamed "Bombingham" for the frequency of unpunished racist bombings there.

In 1963, he ordered the use of high-pressure fire hoses and police dogs against peaceful demonstrators, including many children, during the campaign organized by the SCLC and Fred Shuttlesworth. Photographs of these scenes, broadcast around the world, turned public opinion in the movement's favor and hastened local negotiations, as well as passage of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Elected to the Alabama Public Service Commission in 1964, he suffers a stroke on 7 December 1966 that confines him to a wheelchair, then a second on 26 February 1973 that leaves him unconscious. He dies weeks later, on 10 March 1973, in Birmingham.
