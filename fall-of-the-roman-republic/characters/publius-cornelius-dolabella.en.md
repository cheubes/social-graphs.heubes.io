A member of a patrician branch of the Cornelia gens, he led a dissolute, debt-ridden youth before marrying, in 50 BC, Tullia, Cicero's beloved daughter, a stormy marriage that ended in divorce as early as 46 BC. His notorious misconduct and infidelities did not stop Cicero from remaining attached to him for a long time.

Pressed by his creditors, he joined Caesar's camp in 49 BC and fought at his side in Africa and then in Spain, where he was wounded at the Battle of Munda. Caesar promised him the consulship on his return to Rome before changing his mind, a promise Dolabella finally secured on the dictator's death: he seized the consular insignia and had himself confirmed in office by making common cause with the conspirators, including Brutus and Cassius.

Later appointed to govern Syria, he travelled there in 43 BC to raise troops, but clashed with Cassius, who had won over the region's legions to the tyrannicides' cause. Besieged at Laodicea and about to be captured, he had one of his own soldiers kill him rather than fall alive into his enemy's hands.
