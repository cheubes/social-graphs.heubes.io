Born into an equestrian family from the land of the Marsi, he joined Caesar as soon as he crossed the Rubicon in 49 BC and served under him in Africa, Illyria, and then Spain, where he held a command during the campaign against Pompey's sons. A direct witness to the great episodes of the civil war, he later wrote a history of the period, now lost, but heavily drawn upon by later ancient historians such as Appian and Plutarch.

After Caesar's assassination, he sided with Mark Antony, for whom he governed Cisalpine Gaul for a time; it was in this post that he protected the young poet Virgil's land from confiscation during the distribution of estates to veterans. Consul in 40 BC, he helped negotiate the Treaty of Brundisium, which temporarily reconciled Antony and Octavian, and in 39 BC he triumphed over an Illyrian people, the Parthini, at the head of an army he commanded in Macedonia.

Enriched by the spoils of that campaign, he built Rome's first public library and then withdrew from active military and political life, refusing to take part in the war between Antony and Octavian. A patron of the poets of his day, Virgil and Horace foremost among them, he died of old age in AD 4, at his villa in Tusculum.
