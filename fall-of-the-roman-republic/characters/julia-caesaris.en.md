The only child of Caesar and his first wife Cornelia, she lost her mother while still a child and was raised by her paternal grandmother, Aurelia. Originally betrothed to a young Roman noble, she saw that engagement broken off by her father in 59 BC in favour of a far more strategic match.

Caesar married her instead to Pompey, more than twenty years her senior, to seal the political alliance of the First Triumvirate. Against all expectation, the marriage proved a happy one, and Pompey, known for his infidelities, showed her genuine devotion, so much so that their mutual affection helped ease tensions between her husband and her father for a time.

In 54 BC, while pregnant, she died in childbirth along with her child, a shock to Pompey and to Rome as a whole: her public funeral, organised by the people on the Campus Martius against the Senate's wishes, attested to her popularity. Her death deprived Caesar and Pompey of the last personal bond restraining their rivalry, paving the way for the final break and civil war.
