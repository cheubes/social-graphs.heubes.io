The only child of Caesar and his first wife Cornelia, she lost her mother while still a child and was raised by her paternal grandmother, Aurelia. Originally betrothed to Quintus Servilius Caepio, a young Roman noble, she saw that engagement broken off by her father in 59 BC in favour of a far more strategic match.

Caesar married her instead to Pompey, more than twenty years her senior, to seal the political alliance of the First Triumvirate. Against all expectation, the marriage proved a happy one, and Pompey, known for his infidelities, showed her genuine devotion, so much so that their mutual affection helped ease tensions between her husband and her father for a time.

In 54 BC, while pregnant, she died in childbirth, her child, whose sex ancient sources disagree on, surviving her by only a few days. Pompey wished her ashes to rest at his villa in Alba, but the people, by a special senatorial decree and against the objections of consul Lucius Domitius Ahenobarbus, insisted on a public funeral and burial on the Campus Martius, attesting to her popularity. Caesar honoured her memory in 46 BC with gladiatorial games held alongside festivities for Venus Genetrix, the mythical goddess from whom the Julian family claimed descent. Her death deprived Caesar and Pompey of the last personal bond restraining their rivalry, paving the way for the final break and civil war.
