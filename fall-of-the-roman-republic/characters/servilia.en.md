Born around 100 BC, Servilia belonged to a prestigious senatorial family and was the half-sister of Cato the Younger, sharing his political influence without sharing his legendary moral rigour. Her first marriage, to Marcus Junius Brutus the Elder, produced her son Brutus; widowed, she remarried Decimus Junius Silanus, with whom she had three daughters, the Juniae, who linked her by marriage to several great Roman families, including that of Cassius, her future son-in-law.

Around 59 BC, she began a lasting affair with Julius Caesar, who once gave her a pearl of considerable value; some ancient historians hostile to Caesar went so far as to suggest he might have been Brutus's true father, a chronologically shaky claim but a telling sign of how well known the affair was.

After Caesar's assassination, in which her son and her son-in-law Cassius played leading roles, Servilia placed her political weight and connections fully behind the conspirators, to the point that her opinions, according to Cicero himself, carried more weight in their deliberations than those of the senators present. She outlived Brutus's death at Philippi in 42 BC and died several years later, though the exact date of her death is unknown.
