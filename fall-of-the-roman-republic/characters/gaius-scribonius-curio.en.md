Son of an orator and statesman of the same name, he first made his name as a declared opponent of Caesar, before a spectacular reversal that would define his reputation. Deeply in debt but a brilliant speaker, he was elected tribune of the plebs for 50 BC fully intending to oppose Caesar's ambitions in Gaul.

Caesar, learning of his financial troubles, paid off his enormous debts in exchange for his allegiance. Once won over, Curio used his tribunate to multiply procedural manoeuvres that delayed Caesar's recall and stoked the crisis with the Senate, playing a decisive part in the chain of events that led to the civil war of 49 BC.

After Caesar crossed the Rubicon, he was given command of an expedition to Sicily and then Africa against Pompeian forces and the Numidian king Juba I, an ally of Pompey. Defeated at the Battle of the Bagradas on 24 August 49 BC, he refused to flee the field and died there along with much of his army.
