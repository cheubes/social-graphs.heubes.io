Aedile and then praetor, Marcus Calpurnius Bibulus reached the consulship in 59 BC alongside Caesar, whose fierce opponent he became: an uncompromising conservative, he tried every legal means to block his colleague's agrarian legislation, until Caesar's supporters violently drove him from the forum. Reduced to impotence, he spent the rest of his term shut up at home, contenting himself with announcing unfavourable omens to undermine the validity of laws passed in his absence.

His marriage to Porcia, daughter of Cato the Younger, sealed his alliance with the most uncompromising wing of the senatorial party. As governor of Syria in 51-50 BC, he drew the resentment of his own troops by claiming credit for victories won by his officers against the Parthians.

At the outbreak of civil war he took command of the Pompeian fleet tasked with blockading the Adriatic to prevent Caesar from reaching Greece. He died of illness during that blockade in early 48 BC, shortly after learning that two of his sons had been killed in Egypt.
