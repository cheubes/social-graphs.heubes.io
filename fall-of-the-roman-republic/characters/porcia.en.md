Born around 73 BC, Porcia was the daughter of Cato the Younger, from whom she inherited a reputation for strength of character. First married to Marcus Calpurnius Bibulus, nearly thirty years her senior, with whom she had two sons, she was later widowed and, in 45 BC, married her cousin Marcus Junius Brutus, whom she had previously prompted to divorce Claudia.

According to Plutarch, when Porcia suspected her husband was planning something serious as the Ides of March approached, she deliberately wounded her own thigh with a knife to prove, by bearing the pain without complaint, that she was worthy of being trusted with the plot against Caesar.

After the assassination and Brutus's departure for the East, she remained in Rome, separated from her husband, in an increasingly dangerous climate for the conspirators' relatives. Her death in June 43 BC is presented by ancient sources as a grief-driven suicide by a dramatic method: she is said to have swallowed hot coals. Modern historians have, however, suggested she may instead have died of illness in the months before the Battle of Philippi.
