The younger brother of the orator Marcus Tullius Cicero, Quintus Tullius Cicero pursued a quieter but no less active political career: aedile and then praetor, he governed the province of Asia for three years, where his integrity as an administrator contrasted with a temper his own brother sometimes judged too quick.

He served as a legate under Caesar during the Gallic Wars, taking part in the second expedition to Britain in 54 BC and holding out with his troops through a gruelling siege of his camp by the Nervii during Ambiorix's uprising. He later resumed the role of legate for his brother, this time during Marcus's governorship of Cilicia.

At the outbreak of civil war he sided, like his brother, with Pompey, before obtaining Caesar's pardon. But their relationship, already strained by Quintus's bitter divorce from Pomponia, sister of their mutual friend Atticus, deteriorated further under Caesar's dictatorship. Caesar's death did not save them: both placed on the Second Triumvirate's proscription lists, Quintus, his son and his brother Marcus were hunted down and executed in 43 BC.
