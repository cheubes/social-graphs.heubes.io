Born into an impoverished but prestigious patrician family, he was early on associated with the popular faction through his marriage to Cornelia, daughter of Cinna, and through his uncle Marius. Forced into hiding under Sulla's dictatorship after refusing to divorce his wife, he built a political career marked by heavy debt and consuming ambition, culminating in 60 BC in the First Triumvirate, an informal alliance with Pompey and Crassus that came to dominate Roman politics.

Consul in 59 BC, he secured the governorship of Gaul, which he conquered over eight years of campaigning (58-50 BC), recorded in his own Commentaries. His break with the Senate and with Pompey led him, in 49 BC, to cross the Rubicon with his legions, triggering a civil war that ended in his total victory over the defenders of the Republic.

Master of Rome, appointed dictator repeatedly and then for life in 44 BC, he launched a broad programme of reforms while concentrating unprecedented power in his hands, which part of the Senate came to see as a threat to republican institutions. He was assassinated on 15 March 44 BC by a group of conspiring senators, among them Brutus and Cassius.
