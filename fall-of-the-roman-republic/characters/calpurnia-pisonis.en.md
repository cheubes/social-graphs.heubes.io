Daughter of Lucius Calpurnius Piso Caesoninus, the future consul of 58 BC, she married Caesar at the end of 59 BC, just as he was completing his own consulship. Like Caesar's earlier marriages, this one served above all as a political alliance, strengthening his ties with the influential Piso family within the Senate.

Although the couple had no children, Calpurnia remained Caesar's wife until his assassination in 44 BC, making her, of all his wives, the one to whom he stayed married the longest. Ancient sources describe her as reserved and self-effacing, standing apart from her husband's political intrigues.

Ancient tradition credits her with an ominous premonition: on the night before the Ides of March, she is said to have had a foreboding dream announcing Caesar's death and to have begged him not to attend the Senate that day. Caesar, briefly shaken by her account and by similar omens, went to the fatal session regardless. After the assassination, Mark Antony obtained Caesar's private papers and funds from her.
