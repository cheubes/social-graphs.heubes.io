Son of Celtillus, an Arverni chieftain executed for aspiring to kingship over all of Gaul, he took command of a broad coalition of Gallic peoples in 52 BC, seizing the opportunity of a general uprising against Roman occupation while Caesar was held up in Italy. Elected war leader by the Arverni and then recognised by many other peoples, he imposed a scorched-earth strategy aimed at depriving the Roman army of supplies.

He inflicted a notable setback on Caesar at the siege of Gergovia, but made the mistake of entrenching his forces in the hill fort of Alesia, where the Roman legions surrounded him behind a double line of fortifications. Despite the arrival of a Gallic relief army, the siege turned in Caesar's favour, and Vercingetorix, to spare his men, surrendered by laying down his weapons at the Roman general's feet.

Taken captive to Rome, he was held for six years before being displayed in the triumph Caesar celebrated in 46 BC for his victory over Gaul. In keeping with Roman custom for defeated enemy leaders, he was then executed by strangulation in the Tullianum prison. His defeat marked the end of organised Gallic resistance and the region's lasting submission to Rome.
