Born around 81 BC, Decimus Junius Brutus Albinus, not to be confused with his distant relative Marcus Junius Brutus, belonged to a branch of the gens Junia adopted into the Postumii Albini. He distinguished himself as early as 56 BC commanding Caesar's fleet in the decisive victory over the Veneti in Armorica, and later fought against Vercingetorix in 52 BC.

One of Caesar's most trusted generals throughout the Gallic Wars and the civil war, he was showered with honours: governor of Transalpine Gaul, named second heir in Caesar's will, and destined for the consulship of 42 BC. Caesar's affection for him was almost paternal, which made his joining the conspiracy all the more striking.

He played a key role on 15 March 44 BC in making sure Caesar, shaken by bad omens and Calpurnia's misgivings, still went to the Senate, where he then took part in the attack. Appointed governor of Cisalpine Gaul, he was besieged there by Mark Antony at Mutina; the consuls Hirtius and Pansa broke the siege at the cost of their own lives, but Octavian refused to press the advantage alongside him.

Abandoned by his troops, who defected to Octavian, he attempted to flee toward Macedonia to join Marcus Brutus, but was captured and executed in September 43 BC on Antony's orders by a Gallic chieftain named Camelus.
