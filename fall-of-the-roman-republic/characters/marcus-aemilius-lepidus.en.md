Born into a prestigious patrician family and married to Junia, Brutus's half-sister, by whom he had several children, Marcus Aemilius Lepidus owed his rise to his steady loyalty to Julius Caesar, who made him his Master of the Horse in 46 BC. After the dictator's assassination in 44 BC, he supported Mark Antony and placed his legions at his disposal, becoming Pontifex Maximus on the strength of that support.

In 43 BC, he formed the Second Triumvirate with Antony and Octavian, which granted him control of Hispania and Narbonese Gaul, though he had to yield most of his troops to his two colleagues for their campaign against Caesar's assassins. The proscriptions that sealed the triumvirate's pact did not spare his own family: his brother Paullus was placed on the list of the proscribed, though he managed to escape. After Philippi, his authority was reduced to the single province of Africa, a gradual marginalisation within the triumvirate.

In 36 BC, called in as reinforcement against Sextus Pompey in Sicily, he attempted to claim the island for himself after victory, but his legions defected to Octavian. Stripped of all his powers except the title of Pontifex Maximus, he was banished to Circeii, where he lived out his life in obscurity for twenty-three years, until his death around 12 BC.
