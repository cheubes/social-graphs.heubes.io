An early general, Gnaeus Pompeius made his name raising a private army for Sulla during the civil war, who granted him the nickname "Magnus" and a triumph even though he had not yet reached the legal age for any magistracy. His rapid rise took him from crushing the partisans of Sertorius in Spain to a first consulship in 70 BC alongside Crassus.

Granted extraordinary commands through popular legislation, he cleared the Mediterranean of piracy within months before leading the war against Mithridates, thoroughly reorganising the eastern provinces and vastly increasing Rome's revenues. In 60 BC he allied with Caesar and Crassus in the First Triumvirate, sealed by his marriage to Caesar's daughter Julia; he went on to hold a second and then a third consulship, the latter exceptionally as sole consul, amid the gang violence of Clodius and Milo.

Julia's death in 54 BC, followed by Crassus's the next year, undid the triumvirate's balance; Pompey drew closer to the Senate and the optimates, becoming their de facto champion against Caesar. Defeated at Pharsalus in 48 BC, he fled to Egypt, where he was assassinated on the orders of the young Ptolemy XIII, who hoped thereby to win Caesar's favour.
