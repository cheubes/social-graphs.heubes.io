The full sister of Octavian, born to Gaius Octavius and Atia, she first married Gaius Claudius Marcellus, consul in 50 BC and one of Caesar's most determined opponents in the Senate. Widowed in 40 BC, she was married within the year to Mark Antony as the human seal of the Treaty of Brundisium, the reconciliation between Antony and her brother after years of rivalry that had nearly tipped into open war.

For several years Octavia worked to hold that fragile alliance together, most notably securing the Treaty of Tarentum in 37 BC, and was widely praised by contemporaries for her loyalty and dignity. Antony's growing attachment to Cleopatra VII strained the marriage past repair: he settled Octavia in Rome while wintering in Alexandria, and the 34 BC Donations of Alexandria, in which he distributed royal titles to his children by Cleopatra, amounted to a public repudiation. He formally divorced her around 32 BC, handing Octavian a powerful propaganda weapon in the run-up to the war against Antony and Cleopatra.

After Antony's death, Octavia raised his children by Fulvia alongside her own, and even took in Cleopatra Selene II, Alexander Helios and Ptolemy Philadelphus, his children by Cleopatra. She died in 11 BC, mourned as one of the most respected women of her generation.
