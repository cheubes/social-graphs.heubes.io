Born into the prestigious gens Claudia, Livia Drusilla was first married to Tiberius Claudius Nero, by whom she had two sons, Tiberius and Drusus. In 38 BC, while pregnant with her second son, she divorced to marry Octavian, who himself broke off his own engagement for her: a union that would last more than fifty years, until Augustus's death.

An influential wife, Livia enjoyed throughout Augustus's reign the status of privileged counsellor, interceding on behalf of others and weighing on some of his political decisions. She also managed a considerable personal fortune, including copper mines, estates, and papyrus marshes in Egypt.

After Augustus's death in AD 14, she continued to wield major political influence as the mother of Emperor Tiberius, whom she had actively promoted in the line of succession over other claimants. She died in AD 29 and was deified by her grandson Claudius in AD 42.
