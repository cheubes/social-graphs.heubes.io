Born on 30 January 59 BC into the prestigious gens Claudia, she married Tiberius Claudius Nero in 43 BC, by whom she had two sons, Tiberius, born in 42 BC, and Drusus, born in 38 BC. The young couple, supporters of the faction opposed to the Second Triumvirate, had to flee Italy during the Perusine War, taking refuge with Sextus Pompey in Sicily and then with Mark Antony in Greece, before returning to Rome under an amnesty.

In 38 BC, while pregnant with her second son, she divorced to marry Octavian on 17 January, who himself broke off his own engagement for her: a union that would last more than fifty years, until Augustus's death. An influential wife, Livia enjoyed throughout Augustus's reign the status of privileged counsellor, systematically consulted before her husband convened his inner council; she interceded on behalf of others and weighed on some of his political decisions. She also managed a considerable personal fortune, including copper mines, estates, and papyrus marshes in Egypt.

After Augustus's death in AD 14, she continued to wield major political influence as the mother of Emperor Tiberius, whom she had actively promoted in the line of succession over other claimants. Ancient sources, hostile to such exorbitant female influence, blamed her for several suspicious deaths among Augustus's potential heirs, Marcellus, Gaius and Lucius Caesar, and then Agrippa Postumus, though the truth of these alleged poisonings was never established. She died on 29 September AD 29 and was deified by her grandson Claudius on 17 January AD 42, an unprecedented honour for a woman.
