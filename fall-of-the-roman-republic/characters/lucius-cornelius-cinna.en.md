A patrician by birth, he emerged as one of the leaders of the popular faction alongside Marius during Rome's first civil war. Elected consul in 87 BC alongside Cnaeus Octavius, he championed distributing Rome's new Italian citizens, enfranchised after the Social War, across all thirty-five tribes rather than confining them to the newly created ones, clashing head-on with his optimate colleague; driven from the city by force and stripped of his consulship by the Senate, he returned at the head of an army, seized Rome together with Marius, and had many of their optimate opponents killed.

Master of Rome after Marius's death, he held the consulship without interruption until 84 BC, four consecutive years in violation of the law requiring an interval between magistracies, an unprecedented arrangement that let him govern on the strength of the Marian party. It was during this period that he married his daughter Cornelia to the young Julius Caesar, then barely seventeen, sealing Caesar's allegiance to the popular cause; his son, also named Lucius Cornelius Cinna, would later rise to the praetorship.

While preparing an expedition against Sulla, who was returning victorious from the East, he was killed in 84 BC in a mutiny by his own troops at Ancona, who refused to embark to face his rival's legions.
