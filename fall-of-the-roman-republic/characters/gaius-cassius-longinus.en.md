Born around 86 BC into a plebeian family of senatorial rank, Gaius Cassius Longinus first distinguished himself as an officer during Crassus's Parthian campaign: after the disaster at Carrhae in 53 BC, he organized the retreat of the survivors and defended Syria against a Parthian counter-offensive. He was elected tribune of the plebs in 49 BC.

He initially sided with Pompey during the civil war, commanding a fleet in the Mediterranean, before submitting to Caesar after Pharsalus. He was promised the praetorship for 44 BC, but saw his rival Marcus Junius Brutus favoured with the more prestigious urban praetorship, feeding his resentment toward Caesar.

Regarded by ancient sources as the true instigator of the plot against Caesar, he recruited most of its members and persuaded Brutus, whose name and reputation lent the scheme its moral legitimacy, to join. On 15 March 44 BC, he personally struck Caesar alongside the other conspirators.

De facto governor of Syria after the assassination, he raised a powerful army there before joining Brutus. Defeated at Philippi in 42 BC in the first engagement against Mark Antony, and wrongly believing Brutus had also been defeated, he had his freedman Pindarus assist his suicide.
