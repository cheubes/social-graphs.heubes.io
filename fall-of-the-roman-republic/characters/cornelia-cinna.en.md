Daughter of Lucius Cornelius Cinna, leader of the popular faction, she was married in 84 BC to the young Caesar, then seventeen years old, a match arranged by her father to strengthen ties between the two families within the Marian camp. The marriage produced their only child, Julia, born in 76 BC.

When Sulla, victorious in the civil war, ordered Caesar to divorce Cornelia as he demanded of all supporters of Marius and Cinna, Caesar refused, at the risk of his life: stripped of his priesthood, his wife's dowry, and several family inheritances, he was forced into hiding for several months before finally being pardoned through the joint intervention of the Vestal Virgins and relatives on his mother's side, Mamercus Aemilius and Aurelius Cotta. This episode, one of the first notable political acts of Caesar's career, revealed his character early on and helped shape his reputation.

Cornelia died in 69 BC, shortly before Caesar's departure for Spain as quaestor. He publicly delivered her funeral oration from the Rostra, an unusual honour at the time for a woman so young, a gesture that contributed to the future dictator's growing popularity with the Roman people.
