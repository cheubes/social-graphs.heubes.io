Casca's exact identity remains debated among ancient sources, which disagree between the praenomina Publius and Gaius for this member of the gens Servilia; the manuscript tradition most often favours Publius Servilius Casca Longus. Little is known of his career before 44 BC beyond his belonging to the circle of officers who had served under Caesar.

He joined the conspiracy against the dictator and, according to Plutarch, was chosen to strike the first blow, a risky role assigned to the conspirator least likely to arouse Caesar's suspicion. On 15 March 44 BC, after Tillius Cimber gave the signal by pulling at Caesar's toga, Casca struck him from behind; the panic of his botched blow, "Casca, you villain, what are you doing?" Caesar reportedly cried, triggered the other conspirators' combined attack.

Elected tribune of the plebs in December 44 BC, he used the office to defend the conspirators' cause in Rome. He later joined Brutus and Cassius in the East and probably died by suicide in the aftermath of the defeat at Philippi in 42 BC.
