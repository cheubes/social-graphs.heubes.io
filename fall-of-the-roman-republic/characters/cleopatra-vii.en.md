The last active ruler of the Ptolemaic dynasty, Cleopatra VII came to power in 51 BC alongside her brother Ptolemy XIII, before establishing herself as sole ruler of Egypt. When Julius Caesar arrived in Alexandria in pursuit of Pompey, she allied herself with him and bore him a son, Caesarion, whose paternity the dictator never publicly acknowledged.

After Caesar's assassination, she grew close to Mark Antony, then master of the Roman East, with whom she had three children. She provided crucial financial and military support for his campaigns against the Parthians and Armenia. The "Donations of Alexandria", by which Antony granted their children control of vast eastern territories, were presented by Octavian to the Senate as proof of treason against Rome.

Defeated alongside Antony at the Battle of Actium in 31 BC, and again at Alexandria the following year, she took her own life shortly after her lover, probably by poisoning, to avoid the humiliation of being displayed in Octavian's triumph in Rome. Her death marked the end of Egyptian independence and the kingdom's annexation by Rome.
