The last active ruler of the Ptolemaic dynasty, Cleopatra VII came to power in 51 BC alongside her brother Ptolemy XIII, before establishing herself as sole ruler of Egypt. Remarkably well educated, she was the only ruler of her dynasty to speak Egyptian, alongside Greek, Aramaic and other eastern languages. When Julius Caesar arrived in Alexandria in pursuit of Pompey in late 48 BC, tradition holds that she had herself smuggled into his palace rolled in a carpet to meet him unseen by her brother; she allied herself with him and bore him a son, Ptolemy XV, known as Caesarion, born around 47 BC, whose paternity the dictator never publicly acknowledged.

After Caesar's assassination, she grew close to Mark Antony, then master of the Roman East, with whom she had three children: the twins Alexander Helios and Cleopatra Selene, born in 40 BC, and Ptolemy Philadelphus, born in 36 BC. She provided crucial financial and military support for his campaigns against the Parthians and Armenia. The "Donations of Alexandria", by which Antony granted their children control of vast eastern territories, were presented by Octavian to the Senate as proof of treason against Rome.

Defeated alongside Antony at the Battle of Actium in 31 BC, and again at Alexandria the following year, she took her own life on 12 August 30 BC, shortly after her lover, according to legend by the bite of an asp smuggled in a basket of figs, to avoid the humiliation of being displayed in Octavian's triumph in Rome. Her son Caesarion, sent toward India for his safety, was caught and executed on Octavian's orders, leaving no direct heir of Caesar alive. Her death marked the end of Egyptian independence and the kingdom's annexation by Rome.
