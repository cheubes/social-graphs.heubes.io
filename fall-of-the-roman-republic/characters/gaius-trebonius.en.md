Born around 92 BC into an equestrian family, Gaius Trebonius owed his political rise to his steadfast support of Caesar. As tribune of the plebs in 55 BC, he carried the lex Trebonia granting Pompey and Crassus their provinces for five years, before joining Caesar as a legate in Gaul from 54 BC onward, and then throughout the civil war against Pompey.

Urban praetor in 48 BC, he went on to govern Hispania Ulterior, where he had to put down a Pompeian revolt led by Pompey's sons. Caesar appointed him suffect consul in 45 BC, one of the highest honours he could bestow on a new man.

Despite a career built entirely on Caesar's favour, Trebonius joined the conspiracy and was assigned, on 15 March 44 BC, the task of keeping Mark Antony in conversation outside the Theatre of Pompey to prevent him from coming to Caesar's aid.

Appointed governor of Asia, he raised money and troops there for the cause of Brutus and Cassius, but was caught at Smyrna by Publius Cornelius Dolabella, sent by Antony to seize the province. Tried for treason, he was tortured and beheaded in January 43 BC, becoming the first of Caesar's assassins to die.
