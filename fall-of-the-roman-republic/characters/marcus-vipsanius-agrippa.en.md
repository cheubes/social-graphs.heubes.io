Of modest origin, Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa met the future Augustus while studying together at Apollonia and became his most loyal companion. After Caesar's assassination, he helped him prevail over his rivals, fought at his side at Philippi, then commanded his armies against Sextus Pompey and Mark Antony.

His most celebrated achievement remains his command of Octavian's fleet at the Battle of Actium in 31 BC, where he defeated the combined forces of Antony and Cleopatra. This decisive victory secured Octavian's supremacy over the entire Roman world. He subsequently received honours and powers unprecedented for a private citizen, nearly equal to those of Augustus himself.

In 21 BC, he married Julia, Augustus's only daughter, further tightening the dynastic ties between the two men. Several children were born of this union who would play leading roles in the Julio-Claudian dynasty. On his death in 12 BC, Augustus gave him a magnificent funeral and had him buried in his own mausoleum.
