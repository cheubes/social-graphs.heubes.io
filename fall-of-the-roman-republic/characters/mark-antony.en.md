A lieutenant of Julius Caesar during the conquest of Gaul and the civil war that followed, Mark Antony rose to become his closest military second, then positioned himself as the dictator's political heir after Caesar's assassination in 44 BC, briefly controlling his treasury and papers.

After an initial rivalry with Octavian, Caesar's great-nephew and designated heir, the two men reconciled and formed the Second Triumvirate with Lepidus in 43 BC. Together they defeated Caesar's assassins, Brutus and Cassius, at Philippi in 42 BC, after which Antony received the wealthy eastern provinces. He married Octavian's sister Octavia to seal their alliance, while simultaneously conducting an affair with Cleopatra VII, Queen of Egypt, with whom he had three children.

This affair, together with his decisions in the East favouring Cleopatra and their children (the "Donations of Alexandria"), gave Octavian grounds for a final break. Declared a public enemy by the Senate, Antony was defeated at the naval Battle of Actium in 31 BC and again at Alexandria the following year; he took his own life shortly before the city fell, leaving Octavian sole master of Rome.
