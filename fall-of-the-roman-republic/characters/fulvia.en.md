Fulvia belonged to a wealthy plebeian family and married three times into major figures of the popular faction: Clodius Pulcher, killed in 52 BC, then Curio, who died in battle in 49 BC, and finally Mark Antony around 47-46 BC. Each marriage placed her at the heart of Roman political life, where she was actively involved, including through the networks of popular support inherited from Clodius.

During the Second Triumvirate, while Antony was in the East, Fulvia wielded considerable influence in Rome and did not hesitate to openly oppose Octavian. In 41-40 BC, she allied with Antony's brother, Lucius Antonius, against Octavian and raised several legions in Italy: this conflict, known as the Perusine War, ended with the surrender of Perusia and her exile to Greece.

Having taken refuge in Sicyon, near Corinth, she died there in 40 BC of an illness, without ever seeing Antony again, who partly blamed her for this political failure.
