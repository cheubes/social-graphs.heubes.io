Son of Ptolemy XII Auletes, he inherited the throne of Egypt in 51 BC at about eleven years old, ruling jointly with his elder sister Cleopatra VII, whom he married according to Ptolemaic dynastic custom. The arrangement collapsed within two years: guided by the regent Pothinus, Ptolemy's court drove Cleopatra into exile in 48 BC and kept the young king as a figurehead of their own power.

When Pompey, defeated at Pharsalus, sought refuge in Egypt that same year, Ptolemy's advisors had him murdered on the shore near Pelusium, hoping to win Caesar's favour. Caesar arrived shortly after, reportedly appalled by the killing, and took it upon himself to arbitrate the succession dispute between Ptolemy and Cleopatra, siding with her. Ptolemy and his sister Arsinoe IV rose against Caesar and Cleopatra, besieging them in the palace quarter of Alexandria in the war that followed.

Defeated at the Battle of the Nile in early 47 BC, Ptolemy drowned while trying to flee across the river, still not fifteen years old. Cleopatra was left in sole control of Egypt, propped up by Caesar and by the Roman legions he left behind.
