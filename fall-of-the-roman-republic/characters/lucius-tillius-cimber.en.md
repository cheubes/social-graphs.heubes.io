Little is known of Lucius Tillius Cimber's background before he entered Caesar's circle, whom he likely served as an officer during the Gallic Wars or the civil war. His loyalty earned him the praetorship in 45 BC and, promised for the following year, the governorship of the wealthy provinces of Bithynia and Pontus.

Despite this favour, he joined the conspiracy against Caesar and was given a decisive role on 15 March 44 BC: on the pretext of pleading the case of his exiled brother Publius, he approached the dictator and, with a sudden movement, pulled his toga from his shoulders; Caesar reportedly exclaimed "Ista quidem vis est!" ("But this is violence!"), unable to rise, giving the other conspirators their agreed signal to attack.

He then departed to take up his governorship of Bithynia and Pontus, where he raised a fleet for the cause of the Liberators. He probably died during the Philippi campaign in 42 BC, though the exact circumstances of his death remain poorly documented.
