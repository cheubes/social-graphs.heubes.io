Born around 85 BC, Marcus Junius Brutus lost his father at the age of eight, executed on Pompey's orders after a failed revolt. He was raised by his mother Servilia and his maternal uncle Cato the Younger, who instilled in him a lifelong attachment to Stoic philosophy and republican tradition. He began his political career as quaestor in Cilicia in 53 BC.

Despite the enmity that had cost his father's life, he sided with Pompey during the civil war, but was pardoned by Caesar after Pharsalus and drawn into his circle, which appointed him governor of Cisalpine Gaul and then urban praetor in 44 BC. That same year he married, as his second wife, Porcia, daughter of Cato and widow of Bibulus.

Despite this closeness to Caesar, Brutus was persuaded by Cassius to join the plot against the dictator, carried along by his reputation for integrity and his prestigious lineage: he claimed descent from the legendary founder of the Republic. He played a central role in the assassination of 15 March 44 BC, then took control of Macedonia as his province.

Defeated alongside Cassius by the forces of Mark Antony and Octavian at the Battle of Philippi in 42 BC, he took his own life by the sword rather than be captured.
