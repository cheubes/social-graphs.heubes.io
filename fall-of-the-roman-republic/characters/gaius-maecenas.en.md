Born around 70 BC in Arezzo, in Etruria, into an equestrian family claiming Etruscan princely descent and rounding out his education with studies in Greece, Gaius Maecenas deliberately chose never to seek public office, preferring to remain in the shadow of power rather than hold it directly. From 40 BC, the year he negotiated Octavian's marriage to Scribonia, he became one of Octavian's closest advisers, assisting him in diplomacy and in administering Rome and Italy during his absences.

A skilled negotiator, he played a key role in concluding the Treaty of Brundisium in 40 BC, which temporarily eased tensions between Octavian and Mark Antony. His position of trust gave him de facto authority over the capital whenever Octavian was on campaign, notably during the Actium campaign, when he suppressed several conspiracies with speed and discretion, though he never held any official title for it.

Maecenas owes his lasting fame above all to his role as patron of letters: he introduced Horace to the court's literary circles and supported Virgil, who dedicated the Georgics to him. Married to Terentia, he lived lavishly in his sumptuous palace on the Esquiline Hill and amassed considerable wealth, from Italian estates renowned for their wine to lands in Egypt. His favour declined in his final years after his wife allegedly indiscreetly revealed the Murena conspiracy he had confided to her; he nonetheless died in 8 BC, leaving his entire estate to Augustus. His name has since become, in several European languages, the common noun for any patron of the arts and letters.
