A member of the prestigious Claudii Marcelli, Gaius Claudius Marcellus married Octavia, sister of the future Augustus, around 54 BC, before becoming one of Caesar's most determined political opponents in the Senate. His cousin, also named Gaius Claudius Marcellus, succeeded him as consul the following year; the two men, often confused by modern historians, pursued an equally hostile policy toward Caesar.

As consul in 50 BC, he repeatedly tried to have Caesar recalled from Gaul before the legal end of his command and without his army, and opposed allowing him to stand for the consulship in absentia, a guarantee Caesar considered essential to his political safety once he returned to civilian life. This intransigence, shared by a faction of the Senate led by Cato, directly helped precipitate the break that led to civil war.

His marriage to Octavia produced a son, Marcus Claudius Marcellus, whom his uncle Augustus would later choose as his first designated heir before his premature death, immortalised by Virgil in the Aeneid. Gaius Claudius Marcellus died in May 40 BC, without having witnessed the definitive fall of the Republic he had so feared under Caesar.
