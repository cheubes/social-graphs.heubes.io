Richard III's nephew through his mother Elizabeth of York, the king's elder sister, and through his father John de la Pole, 2nd Duke of Suffolk, John de la Pole grew up in the immediate circle of the Yorkist court and served his uncle steadily from his accession in 1483, notably holding the presidency of the Council of the North, the institution charged with administering the vast territories Richard III knew intimately, having governed them himself.

On the death of Richard III's only son in 1484, and then of Queen Anne Neville the following year, the king, now without a direct heir, unofficially designated him his presumptive successor, a position several contemporary chroniclers confirm even though no formal document survives. Richard III's defeat and death at Bosworth in August 1485 swept that prospect away overnight and left him, barely twenty-five, without a crown under Henry VII's new regime, which at first treated him with cautious clemency.

Refusing to submit for long, he fled in 1487 to his aunt Margaret of Burgundy, Edward IV's sister and a fierce opponent of the Tudors, and rallied to the cause of a young pretender, Lambert Simnel, put forward as Edward, Earl of Warwick, a surviving nephew of Edward IV who was in fact still held prisoner by Henry VII. Landing in England at the head of an army of Irish and German mercenaries funded by his aunt, he faced royal troops at the Battle of Stoke Field on 16 June 1487, where he was killed, ending the last serious attempt at a Yorkist restoration by force of arms.
