The eldest daughter of Richard Woodville and Jacquetta of Luxembourg, Elizabeth first married a Lancastrian knight, John Grey, killed in 1461 at the Second Battle of St Albans, leaving her widowed with two young sons and little fortune. Tradition holds that it was while waiting under an oak tree near her Grafton estate to intercept the young King Edward IV and beg the restoration of her late husband's lands that she caught his attention.

The king married her in secret on 1 May 1464, without informing his council or even his own mother, a choice as romantic as it was politically perilous: no King of England had ever married a commoner subject before, and the announcement, made several months later, caused a scandal at court. The meteoric rise of her large family that followed, five brothers and seven sisters showered with titles and married into the kingdom's greatest houses, drew the jealousy and hostility of the established nobility, the Earl of Warwick chief among them, the disappointed architect of a proposed French match for the king.

On Edward IV's death in 1483, she took sanctuary at Westminster Abbey with her daughters and youngest son, as Richard III seized the throne, had her brother Anthony and her elder son by her first marriage executed, and then had her two royal sons, confined in the Tower, vanish. In this desperate position, she secretly allied with Margaret Beaufort, once a rival in her own rise, to promote her eldest daughter Elizabeth's marriage to the exiled Lancastrian claimant Henry Tudor, which finally reunited the two rival houses in 1486. Reconciled with the new regime, she withdrew in her final years to Bermondsey Abbey, where she died in 1492.
