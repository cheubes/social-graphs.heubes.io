The eldest son of Ralph Neville, 1st Earl of Westmorland, and Joan Beaufort, Richard Neville acquired the earldom of Salisbury through his marriage to the heiress Alice Montagu, a match that made him, unlike his elder half-brother who remained a mere Earl of Westmorland, head of the junior but far wealthier branch of the Neville family. This material success fed a long-standing rivalry with the senior branch, as well as with the Percys, Earls of Northumberland, neighbours and competitors for influence in the north of England.

This territorial dispute, old and embittered by contested marriages and inheritances, turned into armed clashes as early as 1453 at Heworth Moor, two years before the Wars of the Roses officially began, turning a local dispute over precedence into a small private war between the two families. Richard of York's brother-in-law, whose cause he embraced as early as 1455, he fought at his side at the First Battle of St Albans, where the two men triumphed over the Beauforts and their allies, then shared with him the exile and disgrace of the following years.

Father of the Earl of Warwick, who would become the most powerful lord in the kingdom, Salisbury placed his entire fortune and network of alliances at the Yorkist cause's service until the Battle of Wakefield in December 1460, where York's forces, having rashly sortied from their fortifications, were crushed by the Lancastrian army. Captured while fleeing, he was taken to Pontefract Castle where, despite his rank, he was summarily beheaded the following day on the orders of the victorious Lancastrian lords, his head joining those of Richard of York and Edmund of Rutland on the walls of the city of York.
