The youngest of Richard of York's sons, Richard was born in 1452 at Fotheringhay and was only eight when his father's death at Wakefield left him, with his mother and brother George, exposed to the victorious Lancastrian troops. Raised for a time in the Earl of Warwick's household, he forged in his youth an unwavering loyalty to his elder brother Edward IV, whom he served from adolescence as a military commander in the north of the kingdom.

Unlike Clarence, he never turned against Edward IV, including during Warwick's 1469-70 rebellion, remaining at his side through the Burgundian exile and then fighting valiantly at Barnet and Tewkesbury in 1471. Rewarded for this loyalty, he received most of Warwick's possessions in the north, married Warwick's own daughter Anne Neville in 1472, widow of the Lancastrian prince killed at Tewkesbury, and governed that region for over a decade with widely acknowledged competence.

On Edward IV's death in April 1483, appointed Protector of the Realm for the young Edward V, he intercepted the new king's procession, had his mother's Woodville relatives arrested and then executed, and settled in London where he indefinitely postponed the coronation. Citing the sudden revelation of an earlier bigamy by Edward IV that would render his children illegitimate, he had himself proclaimed king on 26 June 1483. The two young princes, confined in the Tower of London, were seen playing in the gardens over the summer and then vanished, their fate never established with certainty, a mystery that has lastingly tarnished his reputation despite the absence of direct proof of his responsibility.

His reign, marked by a rebellion from his own ally Buckingham as early as autumn 1483, then by the death of his only son in 1484 and of his wife Anne the following year, ended on 22 August 1485 at the Battle of Bosworth, where Henry Tudor's landing and the decisive defection of Lord Stanley's troops turned the course of the fighting. Richard III, personally charging the enemy ranks in a desperate attempt to reach Tudor, was killed in the fighting, becoming the last King of England to die on a battlefield and the last monarch of the Plantagenet dynasty.
