Owen Tudor and Catherine of Valois's second son, Jasper was made Earl of Pembroke in 1452 at the same time as his elder brother Edmund received the earldom of Richmond, both acknowledged and ennobled by their half-brother Henry VI. On Edmund's death in 1456, he inherited the guardianship of his orphaned nephew, the still-infant Henry Tudor, whom he protected with unwavering constancy through every reversal of the war.

A leading Lancastrian fighter, he took part in several campaigns in Wales and the Marches, but suffered decisive defeat at Mortimer's Cross in February 1461, where his own father Owen Tudor was captured and executed. Stripped of his earldom by the new King Edward IV, he went into exile in France and then spent several years waging an itinerant resistance, making one failed attempt after another to keep the Lancastrian cause alive in the west of the kingdom.

After the final defeat at Tewkesbury in 1471, which finished off the Lancastrian cause, he understood that his nephew's survival, by then one of the last living Lancastrian claimants, could no longer be gambled in England: he spirited him away to Brittany, where the two men spent nearly fourteen years in exile, escaping several extradition attempts orchestrated in turn by Edward IV and then Richard III. He returned with Henry Tudor for the decisive 1485 campaign, landing in Wales, raising troops on his home ground, and fighting at his side at the Battle of Bosworth. Victory secured, he became one of the pillars of the new regime, rewarded with the dukedom of Bedford and vast estates, and died in 1495, his unfailing devotion to his nephew's cause having made him the quiet but indispensable architect of the Tudor accession.
