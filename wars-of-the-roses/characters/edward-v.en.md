Edward IV's eldest son, born in November 1470 at Westminster Abbey where his mother had taken refuge during Henry VI's brief restoration, Edward grew up away from court, in his own princely council established at Ludlow under the presidency of his maternal uncle Anthony Woodville, charged with his education and military training from an early age.

On his father's sudden death on 9 April 1483, he was only twelve when he became king. His paternal uncle Richard of Gloucester, named Protector of the Realm by the council, rode out to meet him as he travelled to London for his coronation, had his Woodville escort arrested at Northampton, Anthony Woodville among them, and took sole control of his person. Lodged "for his own safety" in the royal apartments of the Tower of London to await the ceremony, initially set for 22 June, he was soon joined by his younger brother Richard of Shrewsbury, extracted from the sanctuary at Westminster where their mother had placed him for safekeeping.

Days before the planned coronation, a preacher close to Richard of Gloucester publicly claimed that Edward IV's marriage to Elizabeth Woodville had been bigamous, an earlier precontract rendering their children illegitimate. Parliament endorsed this claim in the act known as Titulus Regius, and Richard of Gloucester had himself crowned king on 26 June as Richard III. The two princes, still seen playing in the Tower gardens over the summer, then vanish from all contemporary record; their fate, never established with certainty, remains one of the most debated mysteries in English history, Richard III's guilt presumed but unproven.
