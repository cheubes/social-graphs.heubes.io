The elder daughter of the Earl of Warwick and Anne Beauchamp, Isabel Neville was born in 1451 at Warwick Castle and grew up in one of the wealthiest and most powerful households in the kingdom, promised from childhood to a dynastic future matching her father's fortune. Her marriage became a first-rank political stake when Edward IV, refusing to see it as an innocuous match, formally opposed her marrying one of his own brothers.

Warwick pressed ahead regardless: the ceremony was held at Calais in July 1469, conducted by her own brother George Neville, Archbishop of York, in the presence of the Duke of Clarence himself, eager to embrace his father-in-law's cause against the king. This marriage sealed an alliance that upended the kingdom's political balance: Warwick and Clarence briefly captured Edward IV that same year, before the venture collapsed for lack of sufficient support.

Isabel accompanied her husband and father into exile in France in 1470, where she witnessed Warwick's improbable reconciliation with Margaret of Anjou, his enemy of old, and her own younger sister Anne's remarriage to the Lancastrian prince Edward of Westminster. She gave birth, in the middle of a storm, aboard the ship carrying her back to England, to a first child who did not survive. Back in England after Clarence's 1471 pardon, she bore three more children, two of whom reached adulthood, before dying in December 1476, probably from complications of childbirth or tuberculosis. Her death, which her husband attributed without evidence to poisoning, precipitated Clarence's increasingly erratic behaviour, leading him to have a servant executed without royal authorisation, an episode that directly contributed to his own downfall two years later.
