The seventh child of Richard of York and Cecily Neville, Margaret was only fourteen when her brother Edward took the throne in 1461, making her a princess whose marriage quickly became a first-rank diplomatic stake for the new dynasty. In 1468, she married Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, at Bruges, in lavish ceremonies still remembered for their magnificence, sealing an Anglo-Burgundian alliance meant to contain France.

Having become one of the most powerful women in Europe at the head of one of the continent's wealthiest states, she proved a passionate patron, protector of William Caxton, whom she encouraged to print his first English-language works, and a skilled manager of Burgundian affairs after her husband's death in 1477. This position let her repeatedly welcome and support members of her Yorkist family who had fallen out of favour in England, notably her brother Edward IV during his 1470-71 exile.

A fierce opponent of Henry VII, whose accession in 1485 ended her own family's reign, she spent her final decades funding every attempt at Yorkist restoration from Burgundy: she publicly acknowledged Lambert Simnel as a genuine prince in 1487 and funded his mercenary army, then repeated the exercise a decade later on behalf of Perkin Warbeck, whom she welcomed and presented as her nephew, miraculously escaped from the Tower. Despite both ventures' failure, she never abandoned her cause and died in 1503, the last direct survivor of Richard of York's generation of children.
