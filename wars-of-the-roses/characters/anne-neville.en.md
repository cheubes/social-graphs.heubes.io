The younger daughter of the Earl of Warwick, Anne Neville was born in 1456 at Warwick Castle and grew up, like her elder sister Isabel, in the immediate circle of Edward IV's court, where her own future husband Richard of Gloucester spent part of his childhood for a time. Her life turned in 1470 when her father, having broken with Edward IV, sought a reverse alliance with the House of Lancaster: she was only fourteen when he married her to Edward of Westminster, Margaret of Anjou's son, sealing a reconciliation as sudden as it was improbable between two sides that had fought each other for fifteen years.

This marriage made her, potentially, a future restored Lancastrian queen. But the venture collapsed: her father-in-law Henry VI was deposed again in 1471, and her young husband was killed at the Battle of Tewkesbury barely a year after their wedding. Widowed at fifteen, she found herself under the guardianship of her own brother-in-law George of Clarence, married to her sister Isabel, who sought to keep the Neville paternal inheritance entirely for himself and openly opposed her remarrying.

According to a tradition reported by chroniclers, Richard of Gloucester, Clarence's younger brother, found her disguised as a kitchen maid in London, where Clarence was said to be keeping her hidden, and married her in 1472 despite his brother's opposition. The couple shared the administration of the north of the kingdom for over a decade from Middleham Castle, where their only son Edward was born around 1473. Made queen in turn when Richard seized the crown in 1483, she lost that only child the following year, and then died herself on 16 March 1485, possibly of tuberculosis, during a solar eclipse contemporaries read as an ill omen. Her death, coming five months before her husband's fall at Bosworth, left Richard III without a direct heir at the decisive moment of his reign.
