A mere knight in the service of the Duke of Bedford, regent of England in France, Richard Woodville owed his rise to a marriage as bold as it was unexpected: around 1436 he secretly married Jacquetta of Luxembourg, the duke's widow and a princess by birth through the House of Saint-Pol, causing a scandal at court and a royal fine the couple had to pay for this unauthorised misalliance. Despite this modest origin, he won Henry VI's trust over the following decades, who made him Baron Rivers in 1448 and then Earl under the new reign of Edward IV in 1466.

The couple had a very large family, fourteen children several of whom reached adulthood, among them the eldest, Elizabeth, widow of a Lancastrian knight killed in battle, who married King Edward IV himself in 1464 under the most romantic circumstances. That royal match propelled the entire Woodville family to the forefront of the court: Richard became treasurer of the realm and Constable of England, while his other children married into the kingdom's greatest houses.

This meteoric rise drew the jealousy of the established nobility, the Earl of Warwick above all, whose influence declined as the Woodvilles' grew. During Warwick's revolt against Edward IV in 1469, Richard Woodville and his son John were captured near Chepstow while trying to flee into Wales, then executed without trial at Kenilworth on Warwick's direct orders, who accused the family of sorcery as much as of monopolising power. This execution foreshadowed the hatred Warwick would soon bear the entire royal family, up to his own open break with Edward IV the following year.
