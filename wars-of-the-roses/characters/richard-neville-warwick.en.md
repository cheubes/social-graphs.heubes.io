The Earl of Salisbury's eldest son, Richard Neville became Earl of Warwick through his 1449 marriage to Anne Beauchamp, heiress to one of the oldest and largest earldoms in the kingdom. This union, combined with an already considerable family inheritance, made him, before he was even thirty, the wealthiest peer in England, at the head of a network of vassals and fortresses stretching from Wales to the Scottish border.

An early ally of Richard of York, he fought at his side from the First Battle of St Albans in 1455 and quickly established himself as the Yorkist cause's chief military support, notably through his command of Calais, England's last possession in France, which gave him a base to fall back on and a formidable fleet. After York's death at Wakefield, it was he who ensured the party's continuity and backed the young Edward IV's accession to the throne in 1461, winning the bloody victory at Towton at his side.

Accustomed to governing through the king he had himself crowned, which earned him the lasting nickname "the Kingmaker," Warwick saw his influence challenged by Edward IV's secret 1464 marriage to Elizabeth Woodville and by the rise of the new queen's family, which supplanted his own in royal favour. Also piqued by the failure of his own plans for a French royal marriage, he turned against Edward IV in 1469, allying with George of Clarence, the king's brother, whose elder daughter Isabel he married off to him: the two men briefly captured Edward IV but failed to secure lasting power for lack of popular support.

Forced into exile in France in 1470, Warwick then made the most spectacular about-face of all by allying with Margaret of Anjou, his enemy of old, sealing the reconciliation with his younger daughter Anne's marriage to the Lancastrian prince Edward of Westminster. He restored Henry VI to the throne in autumn 1470, but this "Readeption" lasted only six months: Edward IV, back from Burgundy with his brother-in-law the duke's backing, landed in spring 1471 and confronted his former mentor directly at the Battle of Barnet on 14 April, where Warwick was killed in the fighting, ending a career entirely devoted to making and breaking kings.
