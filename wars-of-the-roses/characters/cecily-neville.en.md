The youngest of the twenty-two children of Ralph Neville, 1st Earl of Westmorland, and Joan Beaufort, herself a granddaughter of John of Gaunt, Cecily Neville married Richard of York in 1429, a match arranged in childhood that sealed the alliance between their two families and, through the generations, made her herself a blood relation of the very Lancastrians her husband would fight. Known as "the Rose of Raby" for her past beauty, she accompanied her husband on his missions to France and Ireland, and bore thirteen children, eight of whom reached adulthood.

Her position at the summit of the English nobility went hand in hand, throughout the 1450s, with direct exposure to the dangers of her husband's political life: she was residing at Ludlow with two of her youngest children, George and Richard, when the town was sacked by Lancastrian troops in 1459, an episode that forced the duchess to present herself in person, with her children, before the victors to plead for mercy. Widowed on her husband's death at Wakefield in 1460, along with that of her second son Edmund, she saw her eldest son Edward take the throne the following year.

She spent the following two decades in the background of her sons' successive reigns, maintaining an outward piety that contrasted with the tragedies striking her family: George's execution in 1478 on Edward IV's orders, then Edward's own death in 1483, followed by the accession of her last surviving son Richard III, whose overthrow and death at Bosworth two years later left her without a single son remaining. She outlived them all, as well as the disappearance of her two grandsons in the Tower of London, and died in 1495 at eighty, during the reign of her grandson Henry VII, whose own marriage united the two houses she had watched collide.
