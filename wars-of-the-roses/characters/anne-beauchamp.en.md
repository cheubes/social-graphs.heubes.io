The daughter of Richard Beauchamp, 13th Earl of Warwick, one of the most illustrious lords of his time, Anne Beauchamp became heiress to the wealthy earldom of Warwick in 1449, on the deaths of her brother and then her niece, both without direct heirs. Her marriage, celebrated years earlier to Richard Neville, then a mere younger son of the Neville family, proved in hindsight one of the most decisive matches of the period: it brought her husband the lands and title that made him, overnight, the most powerful lord in England.

Mother of two daughters, Isabel and Anne, she watched her husband arrange their marriages as political instruments in their own right: Isabel married George of Clarence, King Edward IV's brother, in 1469 against the king's explicit wishes, while Anne, in 1470, married the Lancastrian prince Edward of Westminster to seal her husband's spectacular about-face in favour of Henry VI. Taking refuge with her family in France during the 1470 exile, she was waiting at Southampton for the outcome of her husband's English campaign when news reached her of his death at the Battle of Barnet in April 1471.

Widowed, she took refuge in sanctuary at Beaulieu Abbey, where she soon learned of her son-in-law Edward of Westminster's death at Tewkesbury. Deprived of any direct male support, she watched her own sons-in-law, Clarence and the future Richard III, who married her younger daughter Anne, divide her entire inheritance between them during her lifetime with the consent of a Parliament that legally declared her "as if she were dead," an exceptional legal fiction that stripped her of all her rights without requiring her actual death. She recovered only a fraction of her estate under the reign of her step-grandson-in-law Henry VII, at the very end of her life, and died in 1492, having outlived her husband, both her daughters, and most of her close family.
