Edward IV's second son, born in August 1473, Richard of Shrewsbury received at only four the title of Duke of York, traditionally reserved for the younger son of the royal family, as well as that of Duke of Norfolk through his arranged marriage, at five, to that duchy's heiress, Anne de Mowbray, who died prematurely soon after, the union never having been consummated.

On their father's death in April 1483, he was only nine when his uncle Richard of Gloucester, now Protector of the Realm, demanded his presence in London to attend his elder brother Edward V's coronation. Their mother, in sanctuary at Westminster Abbey with her daughters since the arrest of the Woodville household, gave in only reluctantly, after several days of negotiation with the Archbishop of Canterbury, to the pressure to let her younger son go, invoking the sanctuary's right of asylum in vain to protect him.

Installed alongside his brother in the royal apartments of the Tower of London, he was seen with him, according to contemporary witnesses, playing in the fortress gardens over the summer of 1483, before all contemporary mention of the two boys abruptly ceased. Their disappearance, traditionally blamed on their uncle, now Richard III, without any conclusive proof ever being established, gave rise a few years later to the affair of the pretender Perkin Warbeck, who claimed to be none other than Richard of Shrewsbury, miraculously escaped from the fate that befell his brother.
