The only son of Henry VI and Margaret of Anjou, Edward was born on 13 October 1453, only weeks after his father's first bout of madness, which meant Henry did not even recognise him at first. This coincidence immediately fuelled rumours at court about his legitimacy, with some Yorkist chroniclers going so far as to insinuate that Edmund Beaufort was his true father. Invested Prince of Wales the following year, he nonetheless grew up as the sole legitimate heir to the Lancastrian crown.

The 1460 Act of Accord, which forced Henry VI to disinherit his own son in favour of Richard of York, made the seven-year-old Edward the central stake of the civil war in his mother's eyes. Raised in exile in Scotland and then France after the Lancastrian rout of 1461, contemporaries described him as a precocious youth, keenly interested in military tactics and already eager to win back by force what the Act of Accord had taken from him.

In 1470, the Earl of Warwick's spectacular defection from Edward IV offered the Lancastrian cause an unhoped-for chance: Edward's marriage to Anne Neville, Warwick's daughter, sealed this improbable alliance between two sides that had fought each other for fifteen years. But the 1471 landing in England went badly wrong: barely arrived with his mother, he was killed at the Battle of Tewkesbury on 4 May, either in the fighting or executed shortly afterwards on Edward IV's orders, depending on the source. His death at seventeen ended the direct Lancastrian line and left Margaret of Anjou with no cause left to fight for.
