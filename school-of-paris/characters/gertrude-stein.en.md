Born on 3 February 1874 in Allegheny West, Pennsylvania, to a wealthy Jewish family who had emigrated from Germany, she studies psychology at Radcliffe College (1893-1897) under William James, then medicine at Johns Hopkins University. Settled in Paris from 1903 with her brother Leo, she opens with him a salon at 27 rue de Fleurus where artists and writers from around the world cross paths, and becomes one of the very earliest collectors of Picasso, Matisse and Cézanne.

She meets Alice B. Toklas in 1907, sharing her life with her from 1909, a relationship which, combined with her championing of Cubism, permanently estranges her from her brother until their split in 1913; she then continues alone in her role as patron, notably supporting Juan Gris during her dealer Kahnweiler's wartime exile. She pursues in parallel an experimental literary body of work that influences the generation she herself dubs the "Lost Generation", including Hemingway and Fitzgerald, her most accessible book remaining The Autobiography of Alice B. Toklas (1933).

Sheltering with Toklas in the countryside during the Second World War, she dies of stomach cancer on 27 July 1946 in Neuilly-sur-Seine and is buried in Père-Lachaise cemetery.
