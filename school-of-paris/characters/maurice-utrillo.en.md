The natural son of the painter Suzanne Valadon, he begins painting on a doctor's advice as a way to fight the alcoholism that afflicts him from adolescence onward. A self-taught artist, he specialises in views of Montmartre streets and buildings, often painted from postcards.

His "white period", around 1910-1914, dominated by whites mixed with plaster, remains the most sought-after phase of his work. Despite growing commercial success, he alternates throughout his life between clinic stays and relapses, under the constant watch first of his mother, then of his wife.
