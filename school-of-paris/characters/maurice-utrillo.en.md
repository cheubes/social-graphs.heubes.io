Born on 25 December 1883 in Paris's 18th arrondissement, he is the natural son of the painter Suzanne Valadon, then eighteen years old; his biological father remains uncertain, possibly Puvis de Chavannes or Toulouse-Lautrec, but it is the Catalan painter Miquel Utrillo who legally recognises him in 1891 and gives him his name. Raised by his grandmother in Pierrefitte-sur-Seine, he has a difficult childhood and sinks into alcoholism from adolescence onward, leading to repeated asylum stays from 1901.

He begins painting regularly around 1910, encouraged by his mother and by painter Alphonse Quizet, having already exhibited at the Salon d'Automne from 1909; a self-taught artist, he specialises in views of Montmartre streets and buildings, often painted from postcards. His "white period", around 1910-1914, dominated by whites mixed with plaster, remains the most sought-after phase of his work, followed by a more colourful period from 1922.

Despite growing commercial success, crowned by the Legion of Honour in 1929 and promotion to officer in 1950, he alternates throughout his life between clinic stays and relapses, under the constant watch first of his mother, then of his wife Lucie Valore, whom he marries in 1935 against her wishes. He dies on 5 November 1955 in Dax and is buried in Montmartre's Saint-Vincent cemetery.
