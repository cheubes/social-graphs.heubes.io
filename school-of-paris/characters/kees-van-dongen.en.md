Trained at the Rotterdam Academy of Fine Arts, he settles in Paris in 1897 and initially lives in great poverty before establishing himself at the Bateau-Lavoir, where he mixes with Picasso, and takes part in the 1905 Salon d'Automne alongside the Fauves.

A sought-after society portraitist in the 1920s, he paints the figures of Parisian high society with a vivid palette and a deliberately provocative line. Naturalised French in 1929, he enjoys a success that contrasts sharply with the destitute beginnings of his Montmartre youth.
