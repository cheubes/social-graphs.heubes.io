Born on 26 January 1877 in Delfshaven, near Rotterdam, to a middle-class family, the second of four children, he studies at the Rotterdam Academy of Fine Arts from the age of sixteen, in 1892, under J. Striening and J. G. Heyberg, also frequenting the port's red-light district to sketch maritime and working-class scenes.

He first visits Paris in 1897 before settling there for good in December 1899 to join the woman who becomes his wife, Augusta Preitinger, known as Guus, whom he marries on 11 July 1901 at the church of Saint-Pierre in Montmartre; he initially lives in great poverty before establishing himself at the Bateau-Lavoir, where he mixes with Picasso, and exhibits at the Salon des Indépendants from 1904 and then the 1905 Salon d'Automne alongside the Fauves.

Teaching at the Académie Vitti, he becomes in the 1920s a sought-after society portraitist, painting the figures of Parisian high society with a vivid palette and a deliberately provocative line; he divorces Guus in 1921 and receives the Legion of Honour in 1926, then the rank of officer in 1954. Naturalised French on 31 January 1929, he enjoys a success that contrasts sharply with the destitute beginnings of his Montmartre youth, and dies on 28 May 1968 in Monaco, aged ninety-one.
