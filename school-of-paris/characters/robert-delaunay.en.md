Born on 12 April 1885 in Paris to a bourgeois family, he is raised by his aunt after his parents' divorce when he is nine. Trained in theatrical design between 1902 and 1904, he first paints in a neo-Impressionist vein before being discharged from military service for a heart condition in 1907-1908.

Close to early Cubism, he moves away from it from 1912 onward, together with his wife Sonia Terk, whom he marries in 1910 after her divorce from Wilhelm Uhde, to develop a pictorial language built on the contrast of pure colours, which the poet Apollinaire names "Orphism". His Windows series, followed by the Circular Forms, explores colour as the painting's sole subject.

Taking refuge in Spain and then Portugal during the First World War, he mixes after the war with Surrealists such as Tristan Tzara while pursuing his own path, before returning to abstraction in the 1930s and creating large-scale decorations for the 1937 Paris International Exposition. Fleeing the Occupation, he dies on 25 October 1941 in Montpellier; Sonia goes on to carry their common work forward alone.
