Born on 2 January 1872, an American physician and chemist, he partners in 1899 with the German chemist Hermann Hille to develop Argyrol, a silver-nitrate antiseptic notably used to prevent blindness in newborns; the company they found together in 1902 brings him a considerable fortune, the firm later being sold in 1929.

He discovers a passion for modern painting as early as 1905 and gradually assembles one of the greatest private art collections of his time. In 1922-1923, on the advice of the dealer Paul Guillaume, he discovers and buys en masse the canvases of Chaïm Soutine, then unknown and destitute, a move that radically transforms the painter's career.

He founds the Barnes Foundation in Pennsylvania in 1922 to house his collection, also devoted to art education, which also includes Cézanne, Matisse and Modigliani; it now holds more than four thousand works, including around nine hundred paintings. He dies on 24 July 1951 in a car accident in Pennsylvania, aged seventy-nine.
