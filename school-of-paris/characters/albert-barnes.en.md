An American physician and chemist, he makes his fortune from inventing an antiseptic, Argyrol, before discovering a passion for modern painting and assembling one of the greatest private art collections of his time.

In 1922-1923, on the advice of the dealer Paul Guillaume, he discovers and buys en masse the canvases of Chaïm Soutine, then unknown and destitute, a move that radically transforms the painter's career. He founds the Barnes Foundation in Pennsylvania to house his collection, which also includes Cézanne, Matisse and Modigliani.
