Born Marie Mélanie Laurencin on 31 October 1883 in Paris, the unacknowledged natural daughter of member of parliament Alfred Toulet and an embroiderer from the Cotentin, she studies porcelain painting at the École de Sèvres (1901-1904) then enrols in 1902 at the Académie Humbert, where she meets Braque and Picabia and frequents the Bateau-Lavoir.

Introduced by Picasso to Guillaume Apollinaire in May 1907, she becomes his companion the following year, in a relationship marked by the poet's excessive drinking and violence, which nonetheless inspires several poems, including "Le Pont Mirabeau", until their break-up in 1912. She develops in parallel a personal style of pastel tones and stylised female figures, dubbed "nymphism", far removed from the angular aesthetic of the Cubism practised around her.

Her marriage to the German baron Otto von Wätjen on 21 June 1914, celebrated on the eve of the war, costs her French citizenship; she goes into exile in Spain, first Madrid then Barcelona, where she mixes with the Dada group, before divorcing in 1921 and returning to Paris for good. A fashionable society portraitist and sought-after ballet designer of the Roaring Twenties, she dies on 8 June 1956 in Paris, aged seventy-two, and is buried in Père-Lachaise.
