Originally a law clerk, he turns to painting during a convalescence and becomes in 1905 the leading figure of Fauvism, the movement whose group earned the nickname "fauves" (wild beasts) for the violence of its pure colours.

An admiring rival of Picasso for decades, he pursues a body of work built around colour and line, from The Dance to his Nice interiors, before devoting his final years to cut-outs, culminating in the Vence chapel he designs in its entirety.
