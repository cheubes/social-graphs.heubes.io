Born on 31 December 1869 in Le Cateau-Cambrésis to a family of grain merchants, he first works as a notary's clerk. In 1889, while convalescing, he discovers painting; his mother gives him a box of paints, a decision that sets him permanently on the path to art. He moves to Paris in 1892 and studies at the École des Beaux-Arts under Gustave Moreau, who tells him he "will simplify painting".

He becomes in 1905 the leading figure of Fauvism: at that year's Salon d'Automne, his pure-coloured canvases, including Woman with a Hat, bought by Gertrude Stein, earn his group the nickname "fauves" (wild beasts), coined by critic Louis Vauxcelles. An admiring rival of Picasso for decades, he pursues a body of work built around colour and line, from The Dance and Music, commissioned by Russian collector Sergei Shchukin (1909-1910), to the interiors and odalisques of his Nice period.

Weakened by cancer surgery in 1941, he invents cut-outs, which let him keep creating from his bed, culminating in the album Jazz (1943-1947) and then the Rosary Chapel in Vence, which he designs in its entirety between 1949 and 1951. He dies on 3 November 1954 in Nice, aged eighty-four, and is buried in the Cimiez cemetery.
