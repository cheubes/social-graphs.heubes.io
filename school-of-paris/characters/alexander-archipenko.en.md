Born on 30 May 1887 in Kyiv, he first studies mathematics before turning to art at the Kyiv School of Art and then in Moscow from 1902, developing a passion for Byzantine frescoes and mosaics. He settles in Paris in 1908, frequenting La Ruche, where he mixes with Fernand Léger and Sonia Delaunay, and prefers studying archaic Greek sculpture at the Louvre to academic classes.

He exhibits at the Salon des Indépendants from 1910 and then at the Salon d'Automne in 1911, opens his own art school in 1912 and joins the Section d'Or group, developing his "sculpto-peintures", painted reliefs inspired by ancient Egyptian art. His innovative sculpture introduces void as a compositional element in its own right, exploring the interplay of concave and convex forms, notably in his Medrano series (1912), which mixes glass, wood and metal.

Emigrating to the United States in 1923, where he becomes a citizen in 1928, he founds several art schools there and teaches at various institutions, pursuing his innovations through to illuminated sculptures (1947) and motorised "archipeintures" combining movement and painting. He dies in New York on 25 February 1964, leaving a substantial part of his work to the Saarland Museum.
