Born Marie-Clémentine Valadon on 23 September 1865 in Bessines-sur-Gartempe, she works various odd jobs, seamstress, laundress, waitress, before becoming an acrobat at the Cirque Fernando and then the Cirque Molier, a career cut short by a fall. Turned model for Renoir, Puvis de Chavannes and Toulouse-Lautrec, who gives her the Italianised name Suzanne after the biblical Susanna and the Elders, she studies their technique and teaches herself to draw.

She gives birth to Maurice Utrillo at eighteen, in 1883, whose vocation she later encourages, and does not take up oil painting until 1892. Her marriage to the financier Paul Mousis in 1896 brings her the financial stability to devote herself fully to her art; she thus becomes the first woman admitted to the Société Nationale des Beaux-Arts.

She marries the painter André Utter in 1914 and paints female nudes with a frankness unusual for a woman of her time, alongside portraits and still lifes, in a vigorous, brightly coloured style that sets her apart from her contemporaries. She dies on 7 April 1938 in Paris, surrounded by fellow artists including Picasso and Braque, and is buried in the Saint-Ouen cemetery.
