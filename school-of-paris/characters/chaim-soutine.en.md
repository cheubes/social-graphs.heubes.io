Born into a very poor Jewish family near Minsk, he studies in Vilnius before moving to Paris in 1913 and settling at La Ruche, where he becomes a close friend of Modigliani, who introduces him to his dealer Zborowski.

His expressionist painting, with its violent impasto and tortured colours, notably his series of beef carcasses inspired by Rembrandt, finally finds an audience thanks to the collector Albert Barnes in 1923. A Jew hunted by the occupying forces, he dies in 1943 from an untreated ulcer while fleeing in hiding.
