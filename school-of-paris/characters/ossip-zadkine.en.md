Born Yossel Aronovitch Tsadkine on 4 July 1888 in Vitebsk, like Chagall, he studies in Sunderland and then London between 1907 and 1909, attending an art school and visiting the British Museum, before settling in Paris around 1910. He frequents La Ruche, studies at the École des Beaux-Arts and befriends artists of the Cubist circle while developing his own sculpture, between expressionist figuration and African influences, exhibited from 1911 at the Salon d'Automne.

Naturalised French, he serves in the Foreign Legion as a stretcher-bearer during the First World War (1916-1917), before marrying the painter Valentine Prax in 1920 and establishing himself as one of the leading Cubist sculptors, with more than four hundred works to his name. He flees to the United States during the Second World War, teaching at the Art Students League from 1944, before returning to France in September 1945.

He is best known for The Destroyed City, a six-metre monument unveiled in Rotterdam on 15 May 1953, commemorating the German bombing of the city in 1940. He dies on 25 November 1967 in Neuilly-sur-Seine and is buried in Montparnasse cemetery.
