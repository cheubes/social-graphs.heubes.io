Born on 19 February 1876 in Hobița, Romania, to a family of well-off peasants managing monastery lands, he studies at the Craiova School of Arts and Crafts (1894-1898) and then at the Bucharest School of Fine Arts (1898-1901), initially producing work of a classical cast. He then travels to Paris on foot, via Vienna and Munich, in 1903-1904, and enrols at the École Nationale Supérieure des Beaux-Arts in 1905.

He works briefly, for a single month, in Rodin's studio, which he leaves declaring that "nothing grows in the shade of great trees". He then develops a radically pared-down style, reducing his subjects, birds, heads or columns, to essential forms polished to the edge of abstraction, far beyond traditional forms of representation.

Settled for life in his Montparnasse studio, which he treats as a total work of art, he lastingly influences Zadkine, Archipenko and Modigliani, whom he briefly introduces to direct carving. Naturalised French in 1952, he dies on 16 March 1957 in Paris, aged eighty-one, bequeathing his entire studio to the French state, now reconstituted near the Centre Pompidou.
