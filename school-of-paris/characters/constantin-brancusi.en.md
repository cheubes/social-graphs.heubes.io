The son of Romanian peasants and a self-taught sculptor trained at the Bucharest School of Fine Arts, he travels to Paris on foot from Romania in 1904 and works briefly in Rodin's studio, which he soon leaves, declaring that "nothing grows in the shade of great trees".

He develops a radically pared-down style, reducing his subjects, birds, heads or columns, to essential forms polished to the edge of abstraction. Settled for life in his Montparnasse studio, which he treats as a total work of art, he lastingly influences Zadkine, Archipenko and Modigliani, whom he briefly introduces to direct carving.
