Born on 4 April 1876 in Paris to musician parents of Flemish descent, a pianist mother and a violinist father, he first earns a living as a professional cyclist and violinist before turning seriously to painting around 1893, without any academic training.

His meeting with Derain, after a train derailment in June 1900, marks the start of a lasting friendship and a shared studio in Chatou, where Fauvism is born alongside Matisse and shown at the scandalous 1905 Salon d'Automne, nicknamed the "cage of wild beasts". Struck by a Cézanne retrospective in 1907, he darkens his palette and briefly moves closer to Cubism, before returning to a late Fauvism of turbulent landscapes; an early collector of African art, he is praised for it by Apollinaire as early as 1912.

Under the Occupation, he publishes articles hostile to Picasso's influence on French painting (1942), which earns him a one-year professional ban at the Liberation in 1946. He dies on 11 October 1958 in Rueil-la-Gadelière, aged eighty-two; his epitaph sums up his life: "I never asked for anything, life gave me everything."
