A professional cyclist and violinist before turning to painting, he shares a studio in Chatou with Derain, where Fauvism is born alongside Matisse, a movement he claims to practise instinctively, without academic training.

Struck by a Cézanne retrospective in 1907, he darkens his palette and briefly moves closer to Cubism, before returning to a late Fauvism of turbulent landscapes. A vocal critic of Picasso, he comes to embody a rejection of the very avant-gardes he had helped found.
