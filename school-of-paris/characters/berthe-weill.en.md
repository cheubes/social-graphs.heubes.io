Born on 20 November 1865 in Paris's 1st arrondissement to a modest Alsatian Jewish family, she learns the art trade as an employee of the antiquarian Salvator Mayer, specialising in eighteenth-century prints. In 1901, with her modest dowry, she opens the "Galerie B. Weill" on rue Victor-Massé, becoming Paris's first female art dealer, making a point of backing emerging artists while established galleries focused on already-recognised Impressionists.

Shortly after Picasso arrives in Paris, she is the first to buy and sell his work and organises his 1902 exhibition; she exhibits Matisse for the first time that February, then represents the Fauves from 1905 onward, including Raoul Dufy, who nicknames her "little Mother Weill". In 1917 she organises Modigliani's only solo exhibition during his lifetime, interrupted by the police at the opening itself because of the nudes displayed in the window, which draw a crowd; the exhibition reopens only after their removal.

Forced by financial difficulties to close her gallery in 1939, she benefits in 1946 from a benefit sale organised by the artists she supported, raising four million francs, and receives the Legion of Honour in 1948. Nicknamed "Mother Weill" by the artists, she dies on 17 April 1951, aged eighty-five, in poverty despite her role as a talent-spotter.
