Formerly a shop assistant in an antiques store, she opens her own gallery on Rue Victor-Massé in 1901 and becomes one of the first dealers to exhibit Picasso, Matisse and Dufy, before their reputations were made.

In 1917 she organises Modigliani's only solo exhibition during his lifetime, shut down by the police on its very first day because of the nudes displayed in the window. Nicknamed "Mother Weill" by the artists she supports, she dies in poverty despite her role as a talent-spotter.
