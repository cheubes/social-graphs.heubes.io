Born Sarah Stern on 14 November 1885 in Gradizhsk, Ukraine, she is adopted at five by her uncle Henri Terk, a well-off Saint Petersburg lawyer, who raises her in a cultured household and introduces her to art and travel. She studies drawing in Karlsruhe, Germany (1903-1905), then moves to Paris in 1905 to attend the Académie de la Palette, where she turns toward emerging Fauvism.

In December 1908 she marries, in a marriage of convenience meant to secure her French citizenship, the German gallerist Wilhelm Uhde, whom she divorces to marry the painter Robert Delaunay on 15 November 1910; their son Charles is born on 8 January 1911. Together they develop Orphism, an art of pure colour named by Apollinaire, which she applies equally to painting, textiles and fashion from 1923 onward, blurring the boundary between art and craft, going on to open her own boutique at the 1925 International Exhibition of Decorative Arts.

After her husband's death in 1941, she devotes herself to promoting his work while continuing her own, founding the Salon des Réalités Nouvelles in 1946 to champion abstract art. She continues painting and exhibiting until her own death in Paris on 5 December 1979, aged ninety-four.
