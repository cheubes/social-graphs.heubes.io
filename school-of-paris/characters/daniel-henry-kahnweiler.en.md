Born on 25 June 1884 in Mannheim, Germany, to a wealthy bourgeois family, he is raised in Stuttgart, where his great-uncle Joseph Goldscheider introduces him to music and painting on museum visits across Europe. Destined for a career in finance, he moves to Paris in 1902, frequents the Louvre and the Luxembourg museum and discovers Impressionism, and then Cézanne, in whom he sees the beginnings of a new kind of painting.

He instead chooses the art trade and opens his own gallery on rue Vignon in February 1907, where he meets Picasso a few months later and becomes the principal dealer of early Cubism, tying exclusive contracts with Picasso, Braque, Gris and Léger; he also publishes artist's books, including Apollinaire's L'Enchanteur pourrissant.

A German national, he is in Italy when war breaks out in August 1914 and, refusing to fight against the France that adopted him, goes into exile in Switzerland, while his Paris gallery is seized as enemy property and its contents auctioned off. Married to Lucie Godon in Bern in July 1919, he reopens in 1920 under the name Galerie Simon and remains, naturalised French in 1937, one of the great theorists and champions of Cubism until his death on 11 January 1979, aged ninety-four.
