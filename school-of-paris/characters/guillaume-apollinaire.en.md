Born Guglielmo Alberto Wladimiro Alessandro Apollinare de Kostrowitzky on 26 August 1880 in Rome, to a mother from the Polish nobility and a father whose identity remains uncertain, he settles in Paris around 1900, where he first struggles financially before establishing himself as a literary journalist and art critic.

A close friend of Picasso from 1907, he becomes the principal theorist and champion of Cubism, publicising it through his articles and his essay The Cubist Painters (1913), while mixing with many Montparnasse artists. He himself coins the term "Surrealism", used in a letter to Paul Dermée and then in the programme of the ballet Parade in 1917, shortly before his death.

Naturalised French in March 1916, he volunteers for the army and is wounded in the temple by a shell fragment on 17 March that year, leading to trepanation in May and months of convalescence. He marries Jacqueline Kolb on 2 May 1918 and dies of the Spanish flu on 9 November 1918, two days before the armistice, officially "died for France".
