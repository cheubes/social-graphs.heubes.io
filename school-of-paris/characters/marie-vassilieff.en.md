Trained at the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg, she settles in Paris in 1907 and founds her own free academy in Montparnasse in 1912, quickly frequented by many artists of the district.

During the First World War, she turns her academy into an improvised canteen where Picasso, Modigliani, Léger, Zadkine and many others gather, making her studio one of Montparnasse's social hubs. A painter in her own right, her work remains overshadowed by this role as a central figure of the bohemian scene.
