Born on 12 February 1884 in Smolensk to a well-off family who first steer her toward medicine, she follows her artistic passion and enters the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg in 1903. She settles in Paris in 1905, studying at the Académie de la Palette and then at Matisse's academy, and founds her own free academy on avenue du Maine, in Montparnasse, in 1912, quickly frequented by many artists of the district, including Modigliani, Zadkine and Picasso.

She exhibits Cubist works at the 1914 Salon des Indépendants. That December, she closes her academy to open an improvised canteen for cash-strapped artists and models, run as a private club to get around wartime curfew rules, making her studio one of Montparnasse's social hubs.

Also recognised for her distinctive dolls and textile sculptures, she designs costumes for the Ballets Suédois (1923-1924) and decorative work for major Parisian venues such as La Coupole. Retired to Cagnes-sur-Mer from 1939 to 1946, then turning to ceramics, she dies on 14 May 1957 in Nogent-sur-Marne; her former studio now houses the Villa Vassilieff, dedicated to women artists.
