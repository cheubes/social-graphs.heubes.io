Born Pablo Ruiz Picasso on 25 October 1881 in Malaga, the son of an art teacher who trains him from childhood, he paints his first pictures at eight and enters Barcelona's Llotja school in 1896. After the suicide of his friend Carles Casagemas in 1901, he goes through a Blue Period of dark tones and melancholy subjects, followed from 1904 by a warmer Rose Period, as he settles permanently in Paris and establishes himself at the Bateau-Lavoir in Montmartre.

In 1907 he paints Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, a radical break that opens the way to the Cubism he develops with Braque, breaking the image into multiple facets to depict objects from several angles at once. Supported early on by the dealers Vollard and Kahnweiler as well as by the collector Gertrude Stein, he marries the Russian dancer Olga Khokhlova in 1918 and goes on to pass through many stylistic periods, from the classicism of the 1920s to a return to a more personal Cubism.

Horrified by the German bombing of Guernica in 1937, he creates his political masterpiece of the same name for the Spanish pavilion at the Paris World's Fair. He dies on 8 April 1973 in Mougins, aged ninety-one, leaving a monumental body of work of nearly fifty thousand creations and remaining until his death the most famous figure of twentieth-century art.
