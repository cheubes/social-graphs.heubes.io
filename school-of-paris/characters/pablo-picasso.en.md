An early prodigy trained by his art-teacher father, he settles permanently in Paris in 1904 and establishes himself at the Bateau-Lavoir in Montmartre, where in 1907 he paints Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, a radical break that opens the way to the Cubism he develops with Braque.

Supported early on by the dealers Vollard and Kahnweiler as well as by the collector Gertrude Stein, he goes on to pass through many stylistic periods, from the classicism of the 1920s to Guernica (1937), and remains until his death in 1973 the most famous figure of twentieth-century art.
