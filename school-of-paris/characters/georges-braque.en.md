Born on 13 May 1882 in Argenteuil to a family of craftsmen, he grows up in Le Havre, where his father works as a decorative painter. Trained through evening classes at the Le Havre School of Fine Arts and an apprenticeship as a decorative painter in Paris, he settles in Montmartre in 1902 and attends the Académie Humbert, where he meets Marie Laurencin and Francis Picabia.

He exhibits at the 1905 Salon d'Automne alongside Matisse and Derain, adopting Fauvism's pure colours, before turning to Cézanne from 1907 and painting his Estaque landscapes, rejected by the 1908 Salon d'Automne in an episode that gives "Cubism" its name, coined by critic Louis Vauxcelles. His meeting with Picasso that same year opens a decade of joint work, the two men later saying they had worked "roped together like mountaineers", which invents Analytic and then Synthetic Cubism, introducing papiers collés and stencilled lettering from 1911-1912.

Mobilised in 1914, he is severely wounded and trepanned in May 1915 at Neuville-Saint-Vaast, ending his collaboration with Picasso. Recovered by 1917, he develops a personal body of work built around still lifes and studio scenes of increasingly free form, recognised by a retrospective at the Kunsthalle Basel in 1933 and then, in 1961, by an exhibition at the Louvre that makes him the first living painter exhibited there. He dies on 31 August 1963 in Paris and receives a state funeral.
