Trained in his father's decorative painting workshop in Le Havre, he discovers Fauvism in 1905 before joining forces with Picasso, with whom he develops Cubism from 1908 onward, to the point that the two men later said they had worked "roped together like mountaineers".

Wounded in the head during the First World War, he grows apart from Picasso on his return and develops a more personal body of work built around still lifes and studio scenes of increasingly free form. In 1961 he becomes the first living painter exhibited at the Louvre.
