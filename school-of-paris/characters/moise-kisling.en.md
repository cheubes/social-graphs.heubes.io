Born on 22 January 1891 in Kraków, he studies at the city's School of Fine Arts before a professor encourages him to try his luck in France. He settles in Paris at nineteen, in 1910, and immerses himself in the artistic circles of Montmartre and then Montparnasse, becoming an unmissable social figure nicknamed the "prince of Montparnasse" for his generosity, a close friend of Modigliani with whom he shares a studio for a time.

Enlisting in the Foreign Legion during the First World War, he is severely wounded at the Battle of Artois, which earns him French citizenship; he marries Renée Gros in 1917. His first solo exhibition, at the Galerie Druet in 1919, brings him wide recognition, and his painting, made up of elegant portraits and stylised, brightly coloured nudes, achieves considerable commercial success during his lifetime.

To escape antisemitic persecution, he flees to Lisbon and then New York during the Second World War, where his work is highly appreciated, before returning to settle in France after the war, in Sanary-sur-Mer. He dies there on 29 April 1953, aged sixty-two.
