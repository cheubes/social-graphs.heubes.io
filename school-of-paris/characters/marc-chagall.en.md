Born into a Hasidic Jewish family in Vitebsk, he studies painting in Saint Petersburg before moving to Paris in 1910, settling at La Ruche among other Eastern European Jewish artists and developing a dreamlike style blending childhood memories and Russian folklore.

Returning to Russia during the revolution, he emigrates permanently to France in 1923. Fleeing antisemitic persecution, he escapes to the United States in 1941 before returning to settle in France after the war, where he continues a body of painted work, later extended to stained glass and monumental decor, into old age.
