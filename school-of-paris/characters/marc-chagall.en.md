Born on 7 July 1887 in Liozna, near Vitebsk, into a Hasidic Jewish family in which his mother runs a grocery store and his father works in a herring warehouse, he begins studying painting in 1906 under Yehuda Pen in Vitebsk and then in Saint Petersburg under Nicholas Roerich and Léon Bakst.

He moves to Paris in 1911, settling at La Ruche among other Eastern European Jewish artists, befriending Blaise Cendrars and Robert Delaunay, and developing a dreamlike style blending childhood memories and Russian folklore. Trapped in Vitebsk by the First World War, he marries Bella Rosenfeld there in 1915, then becomes, after the revolution, the city's commissar for fine arts and founds an art school there, before returning to Paris and then Berlin in 1922.

Naturalised French in 1937, he flees antisemitic persecution by emigrating to the United States in 1941, before returning to settle in France after the war. He continues into old age a body of painted work marked by biblical illustration, later extended to stained glass and monumental decor, and dies on 28 March 1985 in Saint-Paul-de-Vence, aged ninety-seven.
