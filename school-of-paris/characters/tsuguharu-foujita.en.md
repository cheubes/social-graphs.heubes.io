The son of a general in the Imperial Japanese Army, he studies Western-style painting in Tokyo before settling in Paris in 1913, where he quickly befriends Modigliani, Soutine and Kisling and adopts a bohemian lifestyle at odds with his upbringing.

He develops a unique "white background" technique blending Japanese-style ink with Western oil, which makes his female nudes instantly recognisable and brings him great success in the 1920s. Converting to Catholicism and taking French citizenship under the name Léonard Foujita after the Second World War, he ends his life in France.
