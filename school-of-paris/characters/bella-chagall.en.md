Born on 14 December 1889 in Vitebsk into a wealthy jewellery-trading family, she pursues higher education in Moscow until 1914. She meets Marc Chagall in 1909, then a poor apprentice, who paints her that same year in My Fiancée with Black Gloves, before marrying him in 1915; the couple settles in Petrograd, where their daughter Ida is born in May 1916.

Present throughout much of her husband's painted work, notably Bella with a White Collar (1917), she accompanies him through all his exiles: revolutionary Russia, then Lithuania and Germany, before settling in Paris in 1924. Arrested in Marseille in 1941 under the Occupation, she manages to flee to the United States with Chagall.

A writer herself, she pens memoirs in Yiddish about her childhood in Vitebsk, published after her death as Burning Lights (1946), illustrated by Chagall. She dies on 2 September 1944 in New York of a viral infection, aged fifty-four, leaving Chagall inconsolable.
