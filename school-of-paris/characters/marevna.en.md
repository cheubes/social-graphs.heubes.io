Born Maria Vorobieff on 26 February 1892 in Cheboksary, the daughter of a Polish aristocrat and a Jewish artist, she studies art at the Stroganov Academy in Moscow from 1910, discovering the Italian primitives, Impressionism and Fauvism, before a stay in Italy where the writer Maxim Gorky, whom she meets in Capri in 1911, gives her the nickname "Marevna", after a little sea princess from a Russian folk tale, which she keeps for life.

Settling in Paris in 1912, she joins La Ruche and the Montparnasse Cubist circle, befriending Picasso, Chagall and Modigliani. Around 1915 she begins a passionate six-year affair with the Mexican painter Diego Rivera, then married, with whom she has a daughter, Marika, born in 1919; Rivera returns to Mexico in 1921.

A painter herself, working in a personal Cubist idiom, she settles on the French Riviera in 1942 and then in England in 1949, and leaves valuable memoirs of the Montparnasse bohemia she moved through, published as Life in Two Worlds (1962) and then Mémoires d'une nomade (1979). She dies on 4 May 1984 in London, aged ninety-two, and is buried in Mexico City, near Diego Rivera's monument.
