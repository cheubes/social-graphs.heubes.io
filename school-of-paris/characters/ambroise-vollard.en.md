Born on 3 July 1866 in Saint-Denis, Réunion, the son of a notary, he moves to Paris to study law before turning to the trade in paintings and prints from his Montmartre flat from 1890, then opening his own gallery on rue Laffitte in September 1893, where he exhibits Cézanne, Gauguin and Van Gogh early on.

In June 1901 he organises the first Paris exhibition of a young, still-unknown Pablo Picasso, and later supports Matisse as well as Derain and Vlaminck in their early Fauvist years. A publisher of art books as much as a dealer, his Éditions Ambroise Vollard pair poets with artists across nearly eighty illustrated volumes.

He dies on 23 August 1939 near Versailles, aged seventy-three, in a car accident whose exact circumstances remain disputed, a Maillol statue or a cast-iron dish having struck his head as he dozed in his vehicle. His collection, dispersed after his death, is now held by major museums worldwide.
