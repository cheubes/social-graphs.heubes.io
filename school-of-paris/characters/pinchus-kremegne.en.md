Born on 28 July 1890 in Jaloudok, in present-day Belarus, the son of a craftsman who fashions folk-art objects despite Jewish religious restrictions on representational art, he enters the Vilnius Academy of Arts in 1909, where he meets Soutine and Kikoïne. Fleeing Russian restrictions on Jewish emigration, he reaches Paris in 1912, paying a smuggler to cross the German-Russian border illegally.

Settled at La Ruche with his two fellow Vilnius students, where he also mixes with Chagall and Léger, he turns to painting from 1914 after first practising sculpture; Modigliani paints his portrait, now held at the Bern Museum of Fine Arts. An expressionist painter of landscapes and still lifes, he marries the Swedish governess Birgit Strömbäck in 1923 and gains financial stability through the dealer Paul Guillaume.

He remains in the shadow of his friends Soutine and Chagall despite a prolific career spanning nearly seven decades. After the upheavals of the Second World War, he has a studio-home built in Céret, in the Pyrenees, in 1960, where he continues painting until his death on 5 April 1981, aged ninety; he is buried in Montparnasse cemetery.
