Born on 10 March 1889 in Zalishchyky, Poland, a poet by training, he earns a degree from the Jagiellonian University before coming to Paris in 1913 to study art history at the Sorbonne. The First World War interrupts his studies and pushes him into dealing prints and then paintings, for lack of sufficient income.

From 1916, he becomes Modigliani's exclusive dealer, paying him fifteen francs a day in exchange for his work, along with lodging, a studio and a regular income until the painter's death in 1920; he organises in 1917 his only solo exhibition, which is shut down by the police for indecency. He also champions Soutine and Kisling, and opens a gallery on rue de Seine in April 1926, where he exhibits Derain, Chagall and Fautrier.

Married to Anna Sierzpowska, with whom he has a daughter, Jacqueline, born in 1924, he is ruined by the 1929 crash after spending his fortune on his artists. He dies in poverty on 24 March 1932 in Paris's 6th arrondissement, aged forty-three, and is buried in Père-Lachaise.
