Born into a Sephardic Jewish family from Livorno, he studies painting in Italy before settling in Paris in 1906, where he befriends Soutine, Kisling and Foujita and devotes himself initially to sculpture, under the influence of Brâncuși and African art.

Returning to painting around 1914-1915, he develops an instantly recognisable style of elongated faces and blank gazes, championed by his dealer Zborowski and by his companion and model Jeanne Hébuterne. Ravaged by tuberculosis, alcohol and poverty, he dies in January 1920, followed the next day by Jeanne's suicide, pregnant with their second child.
