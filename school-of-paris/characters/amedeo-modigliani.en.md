Born on 12 July 1884 in Livorno into a Sephardic Jewish family, bourgeois but ruined on the very day of his birth, he has fragile health from childhood, marked by tubercular pleurisy in 1895 and typhoid fever in 1898. Trained at the Livorno Academy of Fine Arts and then in Florence and Venice between 1898 and 1905, he settles in Paris in early 1906, where he befriends Soutine, Kisling and Foujita.

Supported by his first patron, Dr Paul Alexandre, he devotes himself initially to sculpture, under the influence of Brâncuși and African art discovered at the Trocadéro museum, producing some twenty directly carved female heads. Forced to give up sculpture around 1914 due to chronic lung problems, he returns to painting and develops an instantly recognisable style of elongated faces and blank gazes, championed from 1917-1918 by his dealer Zborowski and by his companion and model Jeanne Hébuterne, whom he meets around 1914-1915 and with whom he has a daughter in 1918.

Ravaged by the tuberculosis he contracted in his youth, alcohol and poverty, he dies on 24 January 1920 in Paris, aged thirty-five, and is buried in Père-Lachaise; Jeanne Hébuterne, pregnant with their second child, takes her own life two days later. His daughter Jeanne Modigliani publishes the first biography of her father in 1958, helping make him one of the twentieth century's most widely loved painters.
