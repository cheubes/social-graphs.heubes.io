Born José Victoriano Carmelo Carlos González-Pérez on 23 March 1887 in Madrid to a well-off family, he studies industrial drawing at the School of Arts and Crafts from 1902. He settles in Paris in September 1906 and lodges at the Bateau-Lavoir, next door to Picasso, with whom he befriends along with Apollinaire, initially supporting himself through press illustrations.

He devotes himself entirely to painting from 1910, the date of his first known still life, and joins the Cubist movement; he exhibits at the Salon des Indépendants from 1912 and signs an exclusivity contract with the dealer Daniel-Henry Kahnweiler in February 1913. Often described as an "orthodox" Cubist for the theoretical rigour of his approach, he goes through financial difficulties during the First World War, when Kahnweiler's wartime exile temporarily deprives him of his support, before working with Léonce Rosenberg and then collaborating on set designs for Diaghilev's ballets.

His fragile health, undermined by long-standing tuberculosis, leads to his early death from uraemia on 11 May 1927 in Boulogne-Billancourt, aged forty, leaving his wife Josette and their son Georges.
