Trained as an industrial draughtsman in Madrid, he settles in Paris in 1906 and lodges at the Bateau-Lavoir, next door to Picasso, before devoting himself entirely to painting from 1910 onward and joining the Cubist movement.

Often described as an "orthodox" Cubist for the theoretical rigour of his approach, he is championed by the dealer Kahnweiler and, during the latter's wartime exile, by Gertrude Stein. His fragile health, undermined by tuberculosis, leads to his early death in 1927.
