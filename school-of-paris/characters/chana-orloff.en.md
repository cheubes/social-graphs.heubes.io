Born on 12 July 1888 in Tsarekonstantinovka, Ukraine, the daughter of a tutor and granddaughter of a midwife, she emigrates with her family to Ottoman Palestine after the 1905 pogroms destroy their home, and first works as a seamstress in Jaffa. She moves alone to Paris around 1910 to study dressmaking before enrolling in sculpture classes at the Russian Academy in Montparnasse.

She befriends many Jewish artists of Montparnasse, including Chagall, Lipchitz and Modigliani, who also paints her portrait, and exhibits from the 1913 Salon d'Automne. Now a sought-after portrait sculptor, she marries the writer Ary Justman in 1916, who dies of the Spanish flu in 1919, and gains French citizenship in 1925, the year she receives the Legion of Honour.

Taking refuge in Switzerland in 1942 with her son, while her Paris studio is vandalised and some of her sculptures destroyed, she returns after the war to a recognised career, increasingly dividing her time with Israel, where she creates public monuments and portrait sculptures. She dies in Tel Aviv on 16 December 1968, aged eighty.
