Born on 31 May 1892 in Gomel, in the Russian Empire, he meets Chaïm Soutine in 1905 in a painting studio in Minsk, beginning an artistic friendship that lasts a lifetime, before studying with him and with Pinchus Krémègne at the Vilnius Academy of Arts from 1908. He moves to Paris in 1912 and trains in Fernand Cormon's studio.

Settled at La Ruche from 1914 like his two compatriots, forming with them a trio that stays close for life, he has two children there, including the future painter Jacques Yankel. An expressionist painter of landscapes, still lifes and nudes, he develops a body of work in muted colours shaped by Bonnard and above all by Soutine, holding his first solo exhibition in Paris in 1919; naturalised French in 1924, he later settles in Burgundy and then the Paris suburbs.

Taking refuge in Toulouse with his son during the Second World War, where he joins the artistic group Le Chariot, he explores colour lithography in the 1950s and travels through Spain, Italy and Israel, without ever achieving his friends' renown. He spends his final decade on the French Riviera and dies on 4 November 1968 in Cannes, aged seventy-six.
