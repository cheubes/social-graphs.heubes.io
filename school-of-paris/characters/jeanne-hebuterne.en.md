Born on 6 April 1898 in Meaux into a conservative Catholic family, the daughter of an accountant, she is introduced to painting by her elder brother André, himself a landscape painter. From 1917 she studies at the Académie Colarossi in Montparnasse, where she also models for Foujita while practising painting, jewellery-making and clothing design herself.

Sculptor Chana Orloff introduces her to Amedeo Modigliani in March 1917 at La Rotonde. She becomes his companion and favoured model, painted by him in a long series of portraits that ranks among his most celebrated work, despite her family's fierce opposition, hostile to their religious difference and to the painter's reputation as an alcoholic. The couple settles on the rue de la Grande-Chaumière, in a studio secured through Modigliani's dealer Zborowski, and in November 1918 has a daughter, also named Jeanne.

Pregnant with their second child when Modigliani dies on 24 January 1920, she throws herself from the fifth floor of her parents' apartment two days later and dies aged twenty-one. First buried at Bagneux cemetery, her remains are transferred in 1930 to Père-Lachaise, next to Modigliani's. A painter in her own right, her work long remains overshadowed by his: only six paintings are known until 2002, when nine previously unseen works are discovered in her brother André's studio.
