Born on 8 January 1900 in Moscow, the thirteenth of fourteen children of a horse-breeder father of Kyrgyz descent, he is fascinated as a child by icons on church visits with his mother. Scattered by the 1917-1918 revolution, he leaves Russia and reaches Constantinople in 1920, surviving as a guitarist through Sofia, Belgrade, Vienna and Berlin, before settling for good in Paris in 1923, where he continues performing in Russian cabarets.

He enrols at the Académie de la Grande Chaumière in 1929 and initially paints in an academic vein, until a formative stay in London between 1935 and 1937, where he discovers abstract art and the luminosity of Egyptian sarcophagus colours. Back in Paris, he mixes with Kandinsky as well as Sonia and Robert Delaunay, who introduce him further to abstraction, and develops after the war a personal style of abstract compositions with simple forms and dense colours, aligning him with the later generation of the School of Paris associated with lyrical abstraction.

He achieves financial stability in the early 1950s through regular contracts; in 1962 a room is reserved for his work at the Venice Biennale, the same year he obtains French citizenship. He dies on 12 October 1969 in Paris's 6th arrondissement and is buried in the Russian cemetery of Sainte-Geneviève-des-Bois.
