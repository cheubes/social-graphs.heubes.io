Born Emmanuel Radnitsky on 27 August 1890 in Philadelphia to a Russian-Jewish family, the son of a tailor and a seamstress, who adopts the surname Ray in 1912, he attends the Modern School, an anarchist-influenced institution that shapes his unconventional artistic philosophy. In New York he frequents Alfred Stieglitz's Gallery 291 and works closely with Marcel Duchamp to found American Dada, before concluding that "Dada cannot live in New York".

He settles in Paris on 14 July 1921 on Duchamp's advice and becomes a central figure of early Surrealism. He opens a photography studio in 1922, which quickly becomes his main livelihood as a portraitist of Montparnasse artists and writers and through fashion photography. Kiki de Montparnasse's companion from 1921 to 1929, he creates with her some of his most famous images, including Le Violon d'Ingres, then develops with the photographer Lee Miller, his companion from 1929 to 1932, the solarisation technique.

After France's 1940 defeat, he emigrates to Los Angeles, where he marries Juliet Browner in 1946, before returning to settle in Paris in 1951, where he remains throughout his life attached to painting, which he considers his true art. He dies on 18 November 1976 in Paris, aged eighty-six, and is buried in Montparnasse cemetery.
