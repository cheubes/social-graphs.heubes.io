Born on 5 July 1889 in Maisons-Laffitte to a bourgeois family, he loses his father, an amateur painter, to suicide in 1898, an event that marks him deeply. Expelled from the lycée Condorcet in 1904, he is introduced to Parisian artistic circles by the actor Édouard de Max, who organises his first poetry recital around 1908, and becomes by 1910 a fixture of the capital's salons.

His collaboration with Diaghilev's Ballets Russes brings him close to Picasso, whom he brings in 1917 to the ballet Parade, set to music by Erik Satie, whose scandal marks Cubism's debut on stage. In 1919 Max Jacob introduces him to the young poet Raymond Radiguet, whose sudden death from typhoid in 1923 devastates him and coincides with the start of his opium addiction, out of which comes his best-known novel, Les Enfants terribles (1929), written during a course of detoxification.

A tireless go-between across the arts, he pursues a body of work blending poetry, theatre and film, from La Machine infernale (1934) to The Blood of a Poet and Beauty and the Beast (1946), while maintaining relationships with Jean Desbordes and then the actor Jean Marais. Elected to the Académie française in 1955, he decorates the Villa Santo Sospir with frescoes and shoots his final film, Testament of Orpheus, in 1960. He dies on 11 October 1963 in Milly-la-Forêt, hours after learning of Édith Piaf's death, with the epitaph: "I remain with you."
