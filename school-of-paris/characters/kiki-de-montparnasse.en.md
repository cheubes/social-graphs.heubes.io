Born Alice Prin on 2 October 1901 in Châtillon-sur-Seine, to an unmarried nineteen-year-old mother, she grows up in poverty with her grandmother before joining her mother, a linotype operator, in Paris in 1913. From the age of thirteen she works a string of odd jobs before becoming a model for numerous Montparnasse artists, including Modigliani and Foujita, whose Reclining Nude on a Jouy Fabric causes a sensation at the 1922 Salon d'Automne; it is Kisling who gives her the nickname Kiki.

The muse and companion of the photographer Man Ray from 1921 to 1929, she poses for his most famous images, including Le Violon d'Ingres (1924), which becomes one of Surrealism's most iconic photographs. A central figure of Montparnasse bohemian life, nicknamed "the Queen of Montparnasse", she performs as a cabaret singer and opens her own venue, Chez Kiki, in 1937.

She publishes her memoirs as early as 1929, prefaced by Ernest Hemingway for the American edition, whose content, deemed scandalous, gets the book censored in the United States. She dies on 23 March 1953 at the Laennec hospital in Paris, aged fifty-one, first buried in Thiais before her remains are transferred to Montparnasse cemetery in 1974.
