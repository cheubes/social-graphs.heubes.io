Born into a Sephardic Jewish family from Vidin, he grows up in Romania and studies in Germany, where he works as a caricaturist, before settling in Paris in 1905 and immediately frequenting the Montparnasse bohemian scene.

Nicknamed "the Prince of Montparnasse" for his generosity and frenetic nightlife, he becomes an American citizen during the First World War before returning to France. Consumed by depression and alcoholism, he takes his own life in June 1930, the eve of an exhibition opening.
