Born Julius Mordecai Pincas on 31 March 1885 in Vidin, Bulgaria, into a prosperous Sephardic Jewish family, the son of a grain merchant, he grows up in Bucharest from 1892 and then studies in Vienna in 1902 and Munich in 1903, where he works as a caricaturist under the pseudonym Pascin, adopted at his father's request to protect the family name.

He settles in Paris on 24 December 1905 and immediately frequents the Montparnasse bohemian scene, in the cafés of the Dôme and La Rotonde. To escape Bulgarian conscription, he flees to the United States in October 1914, marries Hermine David there in 1918 and becomes an American citizen in 1920, before returning to France that October, where he becomes the symbolic figure of Parisian bohemia, nicknamed "the Prince of Montparnasse" for his generosity and frenetic nightlife.

Consumed by depression, alcoholism and doubt about his figurative art, he takes his own life in his studio on boulevard de Clichy on 2 June 1930, aged forty-five, the very day an exhibition of his opens; more than a thousand people attend his funeral.
