He meets Matisse at the Académie Carrière, then Vlaminck, with whom he shares a studio in Chatou: their pure-coloured canvases shown at the 1905 Salon d'Automne give birth to Fauvism.

He later moves toward a darker style close to emerging Cubism, befriending Picasso and Braque, before returning between the wars to a more classical manner of painting. His reputation remains lastingly tarnished by a trip to Nazi Germany organised by the occupation authorities in 1941.
