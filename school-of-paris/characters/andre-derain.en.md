Born on 17 June 1880 in Chatou to a well-off family, he begins painting around age fifteen and studies under Eugène Carrière before his military service (1901-1904). He meets Vlaminck by chance on a suburban train in 1900, then Matisse at the Louvre in 1901, and shares a studio with Vlaminck in Chatou.

Alongside Matisse in Collioure in 1905, he takes part in the birth of Fauvism and shows five oils and four pastels in the famous "cage of wild beasts" room at the Salon d'Automne, before a stay in London where he paints vivid views of Westminster. After 1911 he moves closer to emerging Cubism, befriending Picasso and Braque, then gradually abandons the avant-garde for a more classical style, also becoming known as a designer for theatre and ballet from the 1920s to the 1950s.

Invited to Nazi Germany in 1941, officially to secure the release of prisoners and recover his confiscated Chambourcy estate, he returns with a reputation lastingly tarnished, although a tribunal clears him in October 1944. He dies on 8 September 1954 in Garches following a car accident, and is buried in Chambourcy, where he had lived since 1935.
